ABSTRACT: A family of pyrrolo[3,4-c]pyrrol-1(2H)-one (PPO) and 2,5-diazapentalene (DAP) chromophores and DAP-containing polymers were synthesized, and their optical and electrochemical properties were studied. PPO and DAP chromophores are readily obtained by chemical transformation of the lactam unit in diketopyrrolopyrrole (DPP) dyes and can be used as versatile building blocks for construction of a variety of conjugated low-band-gap compounds and polymers. ### INTRODUCTION

The field of organic electronic devices, such as organic light- emitting diodes (OLEDs), thin-film transistors (OTFTs), and photovoltaic cells (OPVs), is characterized by fast-paced progress and mainly driven by the discovery and availability

of key chromophores as building blocks. Diketopyrrolo[3,4- c]pyrrole (DPP) is present as a key structural unit in an important class of red pigments that has exceptional light, weather, and heat stability,<sup>1</sup> and is widely used as a building block in the construction of a variety of compounds and

Thiophene,<sup>4</sup> furan,<sup>14,15</sup> benzene,<sup>3,16</sup> thienothiophene,<sup>5</sup> fluorene,<sup>17</sup> carbazole,<sup>18</sup> cyclopenta[2,1-b:3,4-b′]dithiophene,<sup>17</sup> dithieno[3,2-b:2′,3′-d]pyrrole,<sup>19</sup> and other groups have been introduced at the 3,6-positions, affording many compounds and polymers with different band gaps and some unique properties. However, a decrease in the band gap of DPP compounds is always accompanied along by an increase in the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) level.<sup>17</sup> For solar cell applications, donor materials with a narrow band gap and low-lying HOMO level are desirable because of their contribution to an increase in short-circuit current (J<sub>sc</sub>) and open-circuit voltage (V<sub>oc</sub>),<sup>20,21</sup> and improvement of the long- term stability of organic optoelectronic devices. For photo- detector applications, narrow-band-gap polymers can extend photocurrent spectral response to the near infrared (NIR) region, and low-lying HOMO level could lead to a high detectivity by decreasing the dark current.<sup>22</sup> However, as in the case of the development of DPP-based optoelectronic

DPP has a planar, conjugated 8π-electron bicyclic ring and contains the indigo-like, cross-conjugated vinylogous amidic and vinylogous hydrazine units. Its lactim form has a bicyclic 2,5-diazapentalene (DAP) unit (Figure 1). By chemical

transformation of the lactam group in DPP to the lactim derivatives, the two chromophores PPO (pyrrolo[3,4-c]pyrrol-1(2H)-one) and DAPO<sup>25</sup> could be produced. By analogy, dithioketopyrrolopyrrole (DTPP), derived from DPP by thionation,<sup>26</sup> could also yield the two new chromophores, PPT (pyrrolo[3,4-c]pyrrol-1(2H)-thione) and DAP (Figure 1). However, due to relative reactivity S-alkylation > N-alkylation > O-alkylation, DPP, PPO, and DAP are preferably produced during the alkylation of DPP and DTPP, respectively. Therefore, PPO and DAP are potentially useful building blocks for a variety of new optoelectronic compounds and polymers that may show some interesting properties.

The computational calculation was carried out on the three chromophores DPP, PPO, and DAP. The energy gap of three chromophores shows the order DPP > PPO > DAP, and their HOMO levels gradually decease in order from DPP to PPO to DAP (Figure 2). In comparison with DPP, lack of the donation from lone-pair electrons on the nitrogen to the π-orbit of the diene moiety may contribute to lowering the HOMO levels in PPO and DAP. Furthermore, the NIR photodetectors with photocurrent spectral response up to 1000 nm were fabricated and characterized.

### EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

■

Materials. All chemicals and reagents were used as received from commercial sources without purification. Solvents for chemical synthesis were purified by distillation. Chemical reactions were carried out under an argon atmosphere. 3,6-Di(thiophen-2-yl)pyrrolo[3,4-c]pyrrole-1,4(2H,5H)-dithione (9),<sup>26</sup> 2,5-bis-(2- ethylhexyl)-3,6-di- thiophen-2-ylpyrrolo[3,4-c]pyrrole-1,4-dione (1),<sup>27</sup> 2,2′-(9,9-dioctyl-9H-fluorene-2,7-diyl)bis(4,4,5,5-tetramethyl-1,3,2-dioxaborolane),<sup>28</sup> 9-(2-ethylhexyl)-2,7-bis(4,4,5,5-tetramethyl-1,3,2-dioxaborolan-2-yl)-9H-carbazole,<sup>29</sup> 1-dodecyl-2,5-bis(5-(4,4,5,5-tetramethyl-1,3,2-dioxa- borolan-2-yl)thiophen-2-yl)-1H-pyrrole,<sup>30</sup> and 2,6-bis(trimethyltin)-4,8-bis(octyloxy)benzo[1,2-b:4,5-b′]dithiophene (14)<sup>31</sup> were prepared according to literature methods.

General Methods. <sup>1</sup>H and <sup>13</sup>C NMR spectra were recorded using a Bruker Avance 300 NMR spectrometer ( <sup>1</sup>H NMR, 300 MHz; <sup>13</sup>C NMR, 75 MHz). <sup>1</sup>H−<sup>1</sup>H COSY and <sup>1</sup>H−<sup>13</sup>C HSQC were recorded using a Bruker Avance 400 NMR spectrometer. Elemental analysis was performed on a Vario EL Elementar Analysis Instrument. High- resolution mass spectrometry (HRMS) was obtained from 7.0 T actively shielded Fourier transform ion cyclotron resonance mass spectrometers. Mass spectroscopy data (electrospray ionization, ESI) were measured with a Micromass Quattro LC ESI mass spectrometer. Mass spectroscopy data (matrix-assisted laser desorption ionization, MALDI) were measured with Bruker Daltonics Reflex III MALDI- TOF (MALDI-time-of-flight). The number-average (M<sub>n</sub>) and weight- average (M<sub>w</sub>) molecular weights of the polymers were determined by gel-permeation chromatography (GPC) with a Waters 410 instrument and polystyrene as standards and tetrahydrofuran (THF) as eluent. Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and differential scanning calorim- etry (DSC) were carried out in nitrogen with a heating rate of 10 °C/ min on a Hi-Res TGA 2950 thermogravimetric analyzer and a TA- DSC Q100 (TA Instruments), respectively. Absorption and fluorescence spectra were recorded with a Lambda 900 Perkin-Elmer UV/vis/NIR spectrometer and PTI fluorescence system (near-infrared region) or Shimadzu RF-1501 spectrofluorophotometer (visible region), respectively. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) was performed using a BAS 100B/W electrochemical workstation. Anhydrous dichloro- methane was used as the solvent under an argon atmosphere, and 0.1 M of tetrabutylammonium hexafluorophosphate was used as the supporting electrolyte. A platinum disk (2 mm diameter) was used as working electrode, a platinum wire was used as counter electrode, and a Ag/AgCl electrode was used as reference electrode. All measure- ments were done using a scan rate of 50 mV/s at room temperature. The energy levels were calculated relative to a ferrocenium/ferrocene redox couple as an internal reference.

Computational Method. The structures were drawn with GaussView 4. The geometries were initially optimized using the AM1<sup>32</sup> method in the gas phase, followed by gas-phase B3LYP/6-31+G(d)<sup>33,34</sup> optimization using Gaussian 09<sup>35</sup> running on an AMD Aspire X3400 computer (AMD Athlon II quad core 645 processor, 3.1 GHz with 4GB RAM). The optimized geometries were checked for negative vibrations. The HOMO and LUMO energies and coordinates were taken from the Gaussian output, and the graphical oribtal plots were generated with GaussView 4. The calculation time was reduced by replacing the long alkyl chains with methyl groups.

Device Fabrication. The active layer solution comprised of polymer 6d and [6,6]-phenyl-C<sub>61</sub>-butyric acid methyl ester (PC<sub>61</sub>BM) at a weight ratio of 1:3 was dissolved in chlorobenzene and stirred at 30 °C for more than 48 h in the air to form a solution of 20 mg/mL. Indium tin oxide (ITO) coated glass substrates were cleaned, sequentially, by ultrasonic treatment in detergent at 90 °C for 3 h, then washed with water and deionized water, dried under a stream of air and then in an oven at 110 °C. Prior to use, the ITO-coated slides were treated with UV-ozone for 15 min. A conducting film of poly(3,4- ethylenedioxythiophene) doped with poly(styrenesulfonate) (PE- DOT:PSS) was spin coated at a speed of 3000 rpm for 60 s, followed by annealing at 120 °C for 30 min, forming a film of about 35 nm thickness. The active layer solution was spin coated at a speed of 1000 rpm for 60 s and Al (100 nm) electrode was deposited by thermal evaporation on top of the active layer. The devices were annealed at 140 °C for 30 min, then taken out of the oven immediately to cool them quickly. The PD device area is 12 mm<sup>2</sup>. All the procedures were carried out in air except for the evaporation of Al electrode.

External Quantum Efficiency (EQE) and Detectivity Meas- urement. EQE measurements were done using tungsten lamp (250 W) passing through a monochromator, which was chopped at 160 Hz and focused on the active area of the device. The reference was a calibrated Si photodiode with known EQE spectra. The photocurrent from the device is converted to the voltage and measured with a lock- in amplifier to eliminate background noise. The photodiode signal was first amplified using a low-noise current amplifier and then detected by

a lock-in amplifier (SR830, Stanford Research Systems). Reverse bias on the device was provided by Keithley 236 Source Measure Unit. The detectivity was calculated according to the ref 36. If the shot noise from the dark current is the major contribution, the detectivity (D\*) is calculated from the following equation:

### RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

■

Design and Synthesis. The chemical structures of the PPO and DAP-based compounds and polymers are illustrated in Figure 3, and the synthetic routes are outlined in Scheme 1. As a result of competitive N- and O-alkylations, PPO 2 was obtained in 14% yield along with N-alkylated DPP 1 (Scheme

1). Bromination of 2 yielded only monobrominated PPO, and subsequent Suzuki cross-coupling reaction afforded three PPO compounds 4a−c.

A large family of DAP-containing compounds can be readily derived in high yields from the corresponding DTPP compounds. As an example, alkylation of DTPP gave exclusively DAP 3. Bromination at the two terminal thiophene rings of 3 yielded a dibromo compound that is useful as a building block for constructing a variety of DAP-containing compounds (e.g., 5) and as a monomer for making many conjugated polymers (e.g., 6a−d) by the Suzuki or Stille cross-coupling reaction. The fluorene, carbazole, and other electron- donating moieties were introduced in compounds 4 and polymers 6 in order to tune the optical and electrochemical properties.

Thermal and Optical Properties. The thermal properties of 4a−c and polymers 6a−d were evaluated by differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) and thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) (Figure S1, Supporting Information). The TGA study indicates that both the PPO-based compounds and DAP-based polymers possess good thermal stability in nitrogen with a decomposition temperature (5% weight loss) up to 250 °C. Compound 4a exhibits crystallization (T<sub>c</sub>) and melting transitions (T<sub>m</sub>) at 95 and 149 °C, respectively. Polymer 6a has a clear glass-transition temperature (T<sub>g</sub>) at 107 °C.

The absorption and emission maxima of DPP 1 are at 546and 567 nm, respectively (Figure 4). In comparison, the absorption onset of PPO 2 is red-shifted about 25 nm and the emission shifts to 600 nm (Table 1). Similarly, the absorption onset of 3 is red-shifted about 70 nm relative to DPP 1 and 45 nm relative to PPO 2. Extension of π-conjugation of PPO chromophore leads to further red-shift in both absorption and emission spectra, as compounds 4a−c having the fluorene, carbazole, and thiophene-pyrrole-thiophene units display a red-shift of 80 nm in absorption and 30−50 nm in emission relative to 2 (Figure S2, Supporting Information; Table 1). In comparison with DAP 3, the absorption maxima of compound 5 and polymer 6a are red-shifted by 120 and 160 nm (Figure 4b). Polymerization allows for further band gap tuning and realization of other unique optoelectronic properties of the resulting polymers through incorporation of many other conjugated moieties (Figure S3 and Table S1, Supporting Information). Among all the representative compounds and polymers, polymer 6d absorbs at the longest wavelength at 807 nm as a result of incorporation of a benzodithiophene moiety in the conjugated polymer backbone. Similar to compounds 3 and 5, polymers 6a−d are not fluorescent, presumably due to the presence of thio-lactim group.

Photodetector and Detectivity. To demonstrate the potential applications of DAP-based materials, polymer 6d with a narrow band gap of 1.19 eV was used to fabricate polymer photodetector devices. The device configuration is ITO/ PEDOT:PSS (35 nm)/active layer (100 nm)/Al (100 nm), the active layer comprises a blend of polymer 6d and [6,6]-phenyl-C<sub>61</sub>-butyric acid methyl ester (PC<sub>61</sub>BM) in a weight ratio of 1:3. The dark current density was 3.27 × 10<sup>−9</sup> (A/cm<sup>2</sup>) at bias of −0.1 V (Figure S10, Supporting Information) and even lower than that of the reported high-detectivity photo- detector based on a narrow-band gap polymer,<sup>22</sup> which may be attributed to, at least in part, the lower HOMO level of polymer 6d. The photocurrent spectral response covered a wide range from 400 nm to 1000 nm, matching well with the absorption profile of the film of polymer 6d and PC<sub>61</sub>BM mixture (Figure

7). The EQE and detectivity were calculated at 800 nm (points A and B in Figure 7d) as 4.0% and 1.6 × 10<sup>11</sup> Jones, respectively. The relatively low EQE value may be due to the large domain size of the active layer (about 100 nm, Figure S10, Supporting Information) and the close LUMO levels of 6d and PC <sub>61</sub>BM, which could lead to insufficient exciton dissociation. At the wavelengths from 400 to 850 nm, the device exhibited a photodetectivity greater than 10<sup>11</sup> Jones, being comparable to previously reported polymer photodetectors.<sup>46,47</sup>

In conclusion, by simple chemical transformation of readily available DPP derivatives, a new family of DAP chromophores and polymers are obtained. DAP-based materials have smaller energy gaps and lower HOMO levels relative to DPP analogs and display unique acid-induced halochromic fluorescence in the NIR spectral region. Further band gap tuning is likely to afford suitable DAP polymers for applications in NIR optoelectronic devices such as NIR photodetectors and NIR OLEDs.
