ABSTRACT: We report the synthesis and properties of three novel acetylenic materials based on the new electron-rich building block, 2,2′-ethyne-1,2-diylbis[3-(alk-1-yn-1-yl)thiophene] (EBT). The synthesis of this new nonacene core is efficient and straightforward, and variation among n-hexyl, n-tetradecyl, and 2-ethylhexyl substituents substantially impacts the materials proper- ties. Appending 2-ethylhexyl substituted diketopyrrolopyrrole (DPP) units to either terminus of the EBT core yields a series of low band gap molecules that are characterized in detail by a range of experimental microstructure and electronic structure probes and by density functional theory (DFT) computation. Detailed morphology/microstructure characterization of spin-cast

films by X-ray diffraction and AFM reveals instructive microstructure and electronic/photovoltaic response relationships in both organic field-effect transistors and bulk-heterojunction organic photovoltaic cells. Thus, the former devices exhibit hole mobilities

2

(μ<sub>h</sub>) as large as ∼0.2 cm /(V s) which fall as thermal annealing increases long-range order. The latter devices using PC<sub>61</sub>BM as the electron acceptor exhibit power conversion efficiencies as high as ∼2%, which appear to fall as the materials become less ordered. These results are in accord with a model where evolving grain boundaries and crystallinity impedes hole transport and excitonic charge generation.

### INTRODUCTION

■

Over the past several years, the field of organic electronics has received intense research attention.<sup>1</sup> Striking new materials systems have been developed to fulfill the requirements of organic field-effect transistors (OFETs)<sup>2</sup> and organic photo- voltaic (OPV) cells.<sup>3</sup> Organic semiconductors represent an attractive, low cost alternative to amorphous silicon for numerous devices, and in fact, some materials already out- perform it in terms of carrier mobility. Moreover, the use of organic molecules paves the way for an array of new applications where low cost, minimal weight, and mechanical flexibility will be required.<sup>4</sup> Currently, only a small percentage

of the devices in consumer electronics is fabricated with organic semiconductors. However, this trend is likely to change very soon in view of the materials performance reported in the recent literature.<sup>1b,2c</sup>

Materials Synthesis. The reagents 3-bromo-2-iodothiophene,<sup>30</sup> 3-bromo-2-ethynylthiophene,<sup>30</sup> 4-ethyloct-1-yne,<sup>31</sup> and 3-(2-thienyl)-6-(5-bromo-2-thienyl)-2,5-bis(2-ethylhexyl)pyrrolo[3,4-c]pyrrole-1,4-dione (Br-TDPP)<sup>14b</sup> were synthesized according to published procedures. All starting organic reagents were purchased from Aldrich, Alfa Aesar, and TCI America and used without further purification. All reactions were carried out under N<sub>2</sub> at 1 atm unless mentioned otherwise. THF was distilled from Na/benzophenone. Analytical thin- layer chromatography (TLC) was performed on aluminum sheets, precoated with silica gel 60-S2 F254 (Merck 5554). Flash column chromatography was carried out using silica gel 60 (Silicycle) as the stationary phase.

General Procedure for Stannylation of Alkyne-Containing Building Blocks. Compounds 2, 3, and 4 (1.0 equiv) were dissolved in anhydrous THF (0.05 M) and the solutions cooled to −78 °C under N<sub>2</sub>. Next, n-butyllithium (2.2 equiv) was added, and the stirring was continued for 2 h. Trimethylstannyl chloride (3.0 equiv) was then added to the reaction mixture, and the mixture was allowed to warm up to room temperature, stirred overnight, and quenched by adding 25 mL of water. The mixture was extracted with diethyl ether, the organic layer dried over MgSO <sub>4</sub>, filtered, and concentrated in vacuo. The crude monomer was dissolved in 5 mL of THF and then dropped into methanol (200 mL). The product was allowed to precipitate in the freezer overnight before collecting by filtration and washing with cold methanol, affording yellow fluffy powder as the title compound.

Thermal Properties. Thermal analysis of the present EBT- based small molecules was carried out by differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) at a temperature ramp rate of 10 °C/min. As shown in Figure S1 (Supporting Information), no obvious thermal features can be detected in 8, 9, or 10, and heating beyond 250 °C risks thermal decomposition. Small molecules exhibit sharp thermal transitions compared to polymers;<sup>38</sup> however, considering the size and bulkiness of the substituents in the present small molecules, there may be too much disorder in the molecular packing to exhibit significant crystallinity. The presence of thermal transitions is, of course, a useful guide for determining appropriate thin film annealing temperatures for OFET and OPV processing.

### CONCLUSIONS

■

In this contribution we show that alkyne-functionalized electron donor building blocks can be very efficiently synthesized via Sonogoshira coupling. The new EBT core was prepared with various alkyl substituents, which play a significant role not only in the resulting physiochemical properties of the materials but also in the performances of TFT and OPV devices incorporating them. The three new EBT derivatives flanked by the DPP π-acceptor unit afford low band gaps and low-lying HOMOs as indicated by optical spectros- copy and cyclic voltammetry. Good hole mobilities as high as 0.17 cm<sup>2</sup>/(V s) for compound 8 with I<sub>ON</sub>/I<sub>OFF</sub> greater than 10<sup>4</sup> are measured even under ambient conditions. XRD and AFM characterization of the spin-coated films reveal modest crystallinity but good homogeneity, affording very uniform/ reproducible device performance. Furthermore, DFT calcu- lations indicate low reorganization energies for both holes and electrons, which is desirable for efficient charge transport. BHJ solar cells fabricated with these small molecules yield PCEs close to 2%. Thus, EBT-related cores show promise for developing new organic opto-electronic materials.
