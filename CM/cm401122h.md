### INTRODUCTION

■

Ambipolar OTFTs based on polymeric semiconductors with high and balanced electron and hole mobilities are currently of great interest,<sup>1</sup> due to their applications in complementary-like circuits<sup>2</sup> and in light-emitting transistors.<sup>3</sup> Effective methods developed thus far for fabricating ambipolar OTFTs include blending of unipolar p- and n-channel materials<sup>3b,4</sup> or using a single-component layer but with asymmetric electrodes of different work functions.<sup>5</sup> These methods, however, signifi- cantly increase the device complexity and therefore the manufacturing cost. Covalently linked donor−acceptor (D-A) copolymers provide an effective strategy to develop single- component ambipolar semiconductors,<sup>6</sup> because fine-tuning of energy levels through appropriate combination of D−A building blocks makes it possible for efficient charge injection of both holes and electrons from the same-type electrodes. Proper selection of acceptor units is a key step for ambipolar D−A polymers, including diketopyrrolopyrrole (DPP),<sup>7</sup> naphthalenediimide (NDI),<sup>8</sup> and benzothiadiazole (BZ).<sup>9</sup> Recently, improved mobilities of both holes and electrons (over 1 cm<sup>2</sup> V<sup>−1</sup> s <sup>−1</sup>) have been obtained for DPP-based copolymers.<sup>10</sup> However, most of them were achieved under nitrogen, and few are known to operate in ambient conditions. Development of air-stable ambipolar polymeric semiconductors is of crucial importance for practical applications, especially, for utilizing the superior rheological properties of polymer materials in the printing process.

For achieving high performance air-stable ambipolar polymers, three key criteria need to be met: (i) low-lying lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO < −4.0 eV) for air-stable electron injection and conduction; (ii) appropriate highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) ensuring low- energy bandgap (<1.8 eV) for both hole and electron injection from the same-type electrodes; (iii) structural planarity of the polymer backbone for tight solid-state packing and therefore efficient charge delocalization. Optimizing these criteria requires concurrent up- and/or down-movement of both HOMO and LUMO energy levels of D−A polymers. This, however, is not an easy task with respect to molecular design, because a limited box of available acceptors makes it difficult to systematically modify the LUMO levels of single-type-acceptor copolymers. For example, due to its highly electron-deficient character of NDI acceptors, NDI-based D−A copolymers are air-stable but inherently favored for electron transport.<sup>11,12</sup> Although introduction of strong electron-donating groups, such as dialkoxybithiophene, can lead to ambipolar NDI-based D−A polymers, the hole-mobility (maximum 10<sup>−3</sup> cm <sup>2</sup> V<sup>−1</sup> s <sup>−1</sup>) is an order of magnitude lower than the electron-mobility.<sup>2b,13</sup>

mT(BZ)mT (m = 1, 2). BZ groups are conventional acceptors widely used in D−A polymers for organic photovoltaics (OPVs)<sup>14</sup> and OTFTs.<sup>3d,15</sup> However, in PNDI-mT(BZ)mT polymers, the coplanarity of BZ core with flanking thiophenes makes mT(BZ)mT a whole as electron donors rather than acceptors. We found that decreasing the number of thiophene linkers from m = 2 to 1 lowers both LUMO and HOMO energy levels. Under ambient conditions, PNDI-2T(BZ)2T exhibits unipolar p-type transport with hole mobility up to 0.07 ± 0.02 cm<sup>2</sup> V<sup>−1</sup> s<sup>−1</sup>, whereas PNDI-T(BZ)T presents balanced ambipolar transport with electron and hole mobilities up to 0.05 ± 0.02 and 0.1 ± 0.03 cm<sup>2</sup> V <sup>−1</sup> s<sup>−1</sup>, respectively. Furthermore, OTFT devices based on both polymers are air-stable and exhibit negligible variations when stored in ambient over 3 months. Our results provide an example for rational design and synthesis of air-stable ambipolar polymeric semi- conductors.

Device Fabrication and Characterization. Top-contact/bottom- gate OTFT devices were fabricated using n<sup>++</sup>-Si/SiO<sub>2</sub> substrates where n<sup>++</sup>-Si and SiO<sub>2</sub> were used as the gate electrode and gate dielectric, respectively. The substrates were subjected to cleaning using ultrasonication in acetone, ethanol, and deionized water. After cleaning the SiO <sub>2</sub>/Si wafer with piranha solution (7:3 mixture of H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> and H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub> by volume ratio) and UV-ozone plasma treatment, the SiO <sub>2</sub> surface was modified with n-octadecyltrimethoxysilane (OTS) self- assembled monolayer (SAM) as reported.<sup>18</sup> The thin films of the polymers were deposited on the treated substrates by spin-coating using a polymer solution (8−10 mg/mL) at 1500 rpm for 60 s, optionally followed by thermal annealing at 120 °C, 160 °C, 200 °C, and 240 °C under ambient condition. After organic thin-film deposition, about 60-nm thick gold thin films were deposited as source and drain contacts using a shadow mask. The OTFT devices had a channel length (L) of 200 (or 30) μm and a channel width (W) of 500 μm.

The evaluations of the OTFTs were carried out in atmosphere using a Keithley 4200 parameter analyzer on a probe stage. The carrier mobility, μ, was calculated from the data in the saturated regime according to the equation I<sub>SD</sub> = (W/2L)C<sub>i</sub>μ(V<sub>G</sub> − V<sub>T</sub>)<sup>2</sup>, where I<sub>SD</sub> is the drain current in the saturated regime. W and L are, respectively, the semiconductor channel width and length, C<sub>i</sub> (C<sub>i</sub> = 10 nF) is the capacitance per unit area of the gate dielectric layer, and V<sub>G</sub> and V<sub>T</sub> are the gate voltage and threshold voltage. V<sub>G</sub> − V<sub>T</sub> of the device was determined from the relationship between the square root of I<sub>SD</sub> and V<sub>G</sub> at the saturated regime.

### RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

■

Synthesis and Thermal Stability. Synthetic routes to polymers, PNDI-mT(BZ)mT and m = 1, 2, are depicted in Scheme 1. The monomers were synthesized by Stille coupling of NDI-Br 1 with tributylstanny(bi)thiophenes and then bromide with NBS, while polymers were readily prepared via Suzuki coupling polymerization between monomer Br-mT- NDI-mT-Br (3a, 3b) and 4,7-bis(boronic acid pinacol ester)-2,1,3-benzothiadiazole. The obtained polymers were precipi- tated in methanol and then purified by Soxhlet extraction to remove oligomers and other impurities. Molecule weights of two polymers were evaluated by high temperature gel permeation chromatography (GPC) eluted with 1,2,4-trichlor- obenzene (TCB) at 150 °C. PNDI-T(BZ)T (31.0 kDa) showed a higher Mn than PNDI-2T(BZ)2T (11.5 kDa) as well as a slightly lower polydispersity index (PDI: 2.4 vs 2.2) (Figure S1). The lower molecule weight of PNDI-2T(BZ)2T is probably due to its poor solubility in the reaction solvent of toluene. The thermal gravimetric analysis (TGA) revealed that both polymers exhibited good thermal stability: PNDI-T(BZ)T with 5% weight loss at 454 °C and PNDI-2T(BZ)2T with 5% weight loss at 288 °C (Figure S2). A differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) thermograph of PNDI-T(BZ)T showed endothermic and exothermic peaks at 362 and 297 °C, respectively, while only endothermic transition was observed at 232 °C for PNDI-2T(BZ)2T (Figure S3).

DFT-Calculated Molecular Geometries and Orbitals. To predict the minimum-energy conformation of the polymer backbone and the LUMO/HOMO, we performed DFT calculations (B3LYP/6-31G(d)) for trimers of NDI-mT(BZ)mT and m = 1, 2 (Figure 1). In order to reduce the calculation time, long alkyl chains were replaced by methyl groups in the calculations. The dihedral angles between the BZ core and the neighboring thiophene units are calculated to be 4° and 2° in NDI-T(BZ)T and NDI-2T(BZ)2T trimers, respectively, much smaller than that typically in oligothiophenes (12°).<sup>19</sup> The distance between the nitrogen atom of BZ core and the β-hydrogen atom of neighboring thiophene, i.e., short contacts of N···H (indicated by red-dashed lines), is calculated to be ca. 2.32 Å, much shorter than the sum of the van der Waals radii (2.59 Å).<sup>20</sup> It is these short contact heteroatom interactions

Cyclic voltammetry (CV) was further employed to investigate the electrochemical properties and determine the HOMO/LUMO energy levels of two polymers. The measure- ments were performed using polymer films drop-casted on a Pt disk electrode and placed in a 0.1 M solution of N(Bu)<sub>4</sub>PF<sub>6</sub> in anhydrous tetrahydrofurane. As shown in Figure 2b, the oxidation peaks are stronger than the reduction peaks for both two polymers. Moreover, both the oxidation peak and reduction peak of PNDI-mT(BZ)mT and m = 1, 2 polymers are reversible. This is in sharp contrast to previously reported NDI-based polymers, such as PNDI-T(BZ)<sub>0</sub>T, which exhibits irreversible oxidation peaks and only n-doping behavior in OTFTs. The reversible oxidation peaks of PNDI-mT(BZ)mT suggest the possibility of p-doping processes in both polymers. Both HOMO and LUMO levels of PNDI-T(BZ)T are simultaneously decreased as compared with those of PNDI-2T(BZ)2T. The LUMO/HOMO levels of the former are−4.17/−5.73 eV, 0.12/0.23 eV lower than those of the latter (Table 1).

Moreover, the energy bandgaps determined by CV results are 1.56 and 1.45 eV for m = 1, 2 polymers, slightly larger than those optical bandgaps (Table 1). Note that the LUMO levels of PNDI-mT(BZ)mT are even lower than most of air-stable n- type polymers, such as PNDI-T(BZ)<sub>0</sub>T (Table 1). Hence, air-stable ambipolar charge transport is expected for our polymers.

OTFTs Characterization. To test the charge transport capability of both polymers, typical bottom-gate/top-contact OTFTs were fabricated. The semiconducting layer was deposited by spin-coating the polymer solutions (8 mg/mL in CB/DCB) onto octadecyltrichlorosilane (OTS) treated SiO<sub>2</sub> (300 nm)/n<sup>++</sup>-Si substrate. After thermal annealing at optimized temperature of 200 °C, 60-nm-thick gold electrodes were deposited on the polymer films through a shadow mask. Figure 3 shows the typical output and transfer curves of OTFTs tested in ambient air conditions without any special precautions. Remarkable, OTFT devices based on PNDI-2T(BZ)2T exhibited unipolar p-type transport characteristics, whereas PNDI-T(BZ)T devices showed balanced ambipolar transport characteristics. For PNDI-2T(BZ)2T the average hole mobility was 0.07 ± 0.02 cm<sup>2</sup> V<sup>−1</sup> s<sup>−1</sup>, while PNDI-T(BZ)T exhibited the average electron and hole mobilities of 0.05 ± 0.02(μ<sub>e</sub>) and 0.1 ± 0.03(μ<sub>h</sub> ) cm<sup>2</sup> V<sup>−1</sup> s<sup>−1</sup>, respectively (Table

2). Note that PNDI-T(BZ)T can be considered as the introduction of BZ group to the reported copolymer PNDI- T(BZ)<sub>0</sub>T.<sup>12a</sup>

However, the major carries in OTFTs were switched from n-channel (electron transport) for PNDI-T(BZ)<sub>0</sub>T to balanced ambipolar (both hole and electron transport) for PNDI-T(BZ)T without sacrificing the electron mobility. It suggests that the introduction of the BZ group the polymer backbone does play an important role for the ambipolar behavior. While increasing the thiophene units from 1 to 2 in the polymer backbone of PNDI-mT(BZ)mT, p-channel only was observed. Moreover, it is worthy of notice that similar charge mobilities are measured for OTFTs of both polymers after storage in ambient for 3 months (Figure S5), indicating negligible deterioration due to the ambient trapping species (O<sub>2</sub>/H<sub>2</sub>O).

### CONCLUSIONS

■

We have reported the design, synthesis, and characterization of two polymers PNDI-mT(BZ)mT (m = 1, 2). Unipolar p-type transport (μ<sub>h</sub> = 0.07 ± 0.02 cm<sup>2</sup> V<sup>−1</sup> s <sup>−1</sup>) was observed for OTFTs based on PNDI-2T(BZ)2T. By simply reducing the thiophene number from 2 to 1 in the repeat unit, balanced ambipolarity was achieved in PNDI-T(BZ)T with electron and hole mobilities up to 0.05 ± 0.02 and 0.1 ± 0.03 cm<sup>2</sup> V <sup>−1</sup> s <sup>−1</sup>, respectively. Our results demonstrated an efficient approach to enhance the hole mobilities of NDI-based polymers without sacrificing the electron mobilities. The good air-stability was evidenced by the trifling variations in charge mobilities of OTFTs of both polymers when stored in ambient over 3 months.
