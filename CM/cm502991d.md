ABSTRACT: A new series of conjugated random terpolymers (PDPP2T-Se-Th) was synthesized from an electron-deficient diketopyrrolopyrrole (DPP)-based unit in conjugation with two electron-rich selenophene (Se) and thiophene (Th) species, with a view to inducing different crystalline behaviors of the polymers. The crystallinity of the polymers can be systematically controlled by tuning the ratio between Se and Th; an increase in Se content induced a remarkable increase in the melting and crystallization temperatures as well as the crystallinity of the PDPP2T-Se-Th terpolymers. These changes

in the crystalline properties of polymers had a dramatic effect on the performances of organic field-effect transistors (OFETs) and polymer solar cells (PSCs). However, their effect on each type of devices was very different. INTRODUCTION

■

The advance in electron-rich unit (D)−electron-deficient unit

(A) alternating low bandgap polymers has been the major driving force for the improvement of the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of polymer solar cells (PSCs) up to 8−9% in single junction PSCs.<sup>1−11</sup> However, further development of conjugated polymers is essential to achieve PSCs with theoretical PCEs of more than 10% for commercialization.

To design highly efficient electron donors, several important properties of polymers should be considered and optimized simultaneously, including light absorption ability, energy levels, charge mobility, and solubility.<sup>12−19</sup> Recently, random terpol- ymers, which are composed of three different units in the conjugated backbone, are promising candidates as efficient electron donors because adding a third component to D−A alternating copolymers could have synergetic effects on the various aforementioned important properties.<sup>5,10,20−24</sup>

Among the various properties of low bandgap polymers, the crystallinity of the active layer is a very important parameter

However, at the same time, such aggregations tend to drive strong phase separation in the BHJ blend film with fullerene acceptors.<sup>39,40</sup> For instance, when the DPP-based polymers with too high crystallinity produced the fibrils that are significantly wider than ∼10 nm, exciton dissociation into free charges becomes worse and limits the performance of the PSCs.<sup>30</sup> Thus, determining the optimal crystallinity of the conjugated polymers is critically important in order to optimize the BHJ blend morphology and produce highly efficient PSCs.

The phase separation behavior of the conjugated polymer donor and the fullerene acceptor blend has been studied intensively in terms of structural modification of the conjugated materials, such as changing the length of the side chains and the substituents on the conjugated polymers.<sup>41−46</sup> Even subtle modifications of the chemical structure of the D−A alternating polymers can markedly alter their surface energies and interchain interactions, inducing a dramatic influence on the morphology of the BHJ blend and the PSC perform-

In comparison with the D−A alternating copolymer approach, the terpolymer approach can provide opportunities to optimize the polymer properties by achieving a delicate balance between the competing effects, i.e., the crystallinity and the solubility of the polymers.<sup>20,21,24</sup> For example, in the terpolymer approach, the addition of the judicious choice of a relatively planar third component with a controlled ratio can enhance the π−π interaction of the polymers, while still retaining good solubility.<sup>49</sup> These random terpolymers have higher PCEs than their parent alternating copolymers because of the improved hole mobility and optimized BHJ morphology. However, successful examples of random terpolymers with high efficiencies for OTFTs and PSCs are limited.<sup>20</sup> In addition, the comparison of random terpolymers with different compositions to their parent polymers has been very limited;<sup>5,50</sup> such studies are critical for understanding the compositional effect of the random terpolymers on the device performance, in terms of structural, electrical, and morphological properties.

In this study, we describe the synthesis of a series of novel low bandgap random terpolymers (PDPP2T-Se-Th) that exhibit different crystalline behaviors. We also performed a systematic study of their structural, optical, and electrical properties and investigated their characteristics in the PSCs and OFETs. The monomer (DPP2T) consisting of strong electron- withdrawing DPP unit that was capped with thiophenes was copolymerized with two different electron-rich monomers, selenophene (Se) and thiophene (Th), to produce a series of novel random terpolymers with various Se/Th ratios in their backbone (Se/Th = 0/100 (PDPP2T-Th100, P1), 10/90 (PDPP2T−Se10-Th90, P2), 30/70 (PDPP2T−Se30-Th70, P3), 50/50 (PDPP2T−Se50-Th50, P4), and 100/0 mol % (PDPP2T-Se100, P5)). Because the lone pair of electrons of selenium atoms are easily mobile, predominantly quinoidal structures of selenophenes rather than aromatic structures are produced.<sup>51,52</sup> Thus, the incorporation of selenophene into terpolymers could improve the intermolecular interactions and molecular ordered structures of the terpolymers, which was clearly observed by differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) and grazing incidence X-ray scattering (GIXS) measurements. We achieved control of the crystallinity of DPP-based polymers via

the random terpolymer approach by using a third component of selenophene that had only one atom different from the thiophene unit of the parent polymers, thereby producing only minimal influence on the other properties of the polymer. Therefore, our random terpolymer system is ideal for studying the effect of the crystallinity of random terpolymers on the performances of organic electronic devices.

Although all polymers used in this study exhibited ambipolar characteristics, the balance between hole and electron mobilities was enhanced with increasing Se content, mainly due to the more enhanced electron mobility. The insertion of Se atoms into the π-conjugated backbone is known to provide a number of advantages: (i) interchain-charge transfer can be facilitated by intermolecular Se:Se contacts, (ii) oligo- and polyselenophenes enhance resistance against twisting along the main backbone due to a more quinoidal character.<sup>60</sup> Because of these characteristics, P5-based OFETs showed very high hole and electron mobilities (4.72 and 5.54 cm<sup>2</sup> V <sup>−1</sup> s <sup>−1</sup>, respectively) with well-balanced ambipolarity. Therefore, the enhancement in charge transport capability was also related closely to the more rigid characteristics of the polymers and their stronger crystalline behavior with higher Se content (vide infra).

The wavelength ranges of the EQE spectra of the PSC devices were well-consistent with the UV−vis absorption range of the terpolymer/PC <sub>71</sub>BM blend films (see Figure S4 in the Supporting Information). CONCLUSIONS

■

A new series of PDPP2T-Se-Th random terpolymers containing two different electron-rich units of Se and Th was synthesized to control the crystalline behavior of the low band gap polymers. Through the variation of relative compositions of Se and Th in the polymers, we successfully fine-tuned their crystallinity and charge transport ability and illustrated the benefits of this random terpolymer approach in relation to the performances of the OFETs and PSCs. Increasing the Se content induced a dramatic increase in the crystallinity as well as the T<sub>c</sub> and T<sub>m</sub> values. These changes in the crystallinity of the polymers were the major factor in determining their OFET and PSC performances, although their energy levels and light absorption abilities were slightly different from each other. The enhanced crystallinity of random terpolymers improved the hole and electron mobilities in the OFET devices in order of P1, P2, P3, P4, and P5, with the P5-based OFETs having very high hole and electron mobilities (4.72 and 5.54 cm<sup>2</sup> V<sup>−1</sup> s <sup>−1</sup>, respectively). In contrast, a different trend was observed in the PCE values of PSCs. This was because the morphologies of the polymer:PCBM BHJ films in the PSCs strongly depend on the Se content, with the polymers having increased size scales and degrees of phase separation as the Se content increased. Therefore, the best performance of the PSC devices was obtained for P2 polymers (PCE: 7.2%) with the optimal Se content, producing synergistic effects of the optimized BHJ morphology and high charge mobility. These findings suggested that the optimization of the polymer’s crystallinity, is an essential consideration for designing highly efficient materials for organic electronic devices (e.g., OFETs or PSCs). In addition, the random terpolymer approach provided a powerful route for optimizing the polymer’s properties and maximizing the performances of organic electronic devices.
