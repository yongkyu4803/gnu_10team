# Tetrathiafulvalene-Tetracarboxylate: An Intriguing Building Block with Versatility in Coordination Structures and Redox Properties

†	,†,‡	†	†	†	,†,‡

### Yu-Rong Qin, Qin-Yu Zhu,\* Li-Bin Huo, Zheng Shi, Guo-Qing Bian, and Jie Dai\*

†Department of Chemistry & Key Laboratory of Organic Synthesis of Jiangsu Province, Soochow University, Suzhou 215123, P. R. China, and ‡ State Key Laboratory of Coordination Chemistry, Nanjing University,

### Nanjing 210093, P. R. China

Received March 23, 2010

The synthesis of the transition metal coordination polymers containing a tetrathiafulvalene (TTF) moiety substituted with a tetracarboxylate group of the formulas [Mn(L)<sub>0.5</sub>(phen)(H<sub>2</sub>O)<sub>2</sub>]<sub>n</sub> 3nH<sub>2</sub>O (1), [Mn(L)<sub>0.5</sub>(bpy)]<sub>n</sub> 3 nH<sub>2</sub>O (2), [Mn(L)<sub>0.5</sub>(bpy)(CH<sub>3</sub>OH)]<sub>n</sub> 3 2nH<sub>2</sub>O (3), and [Cu(L)<sub>0.5</sub>(bpy)(DMF)]<sub>n</sub> 3 n(DMF) (4) (L<sup>4-</sup> = TTF-tetracarboxylate; phen = 1,10-phenanthroline; bpy = 2,2<sup>0</sup>-bipyridine) is reported. Complex 1 is a two-dimensional (2-D) coordination polymer constructed of infinite carboxylate bridged Mn(II) chains and TTF moiety linkages. Complex 2 possesses a 3-D polymeric structure formed by infinite -Mn-(O-C-O)<sub>2</sub>-Mn- bridged one-dimensional (1-D) chains, which are further coordinated by TTF-tetracarboxylate ligands of two different orientations. Both complexes 3 and 4 show 1-D extended chain structures but are constituted by different dinuclear metal(II) units with a -Mn-(O-C-O)<sub>2</sub>-Mn- bridge in 3 and a -Cu-(O)<sub>2</sub>-Cu- bridge in 4. Electrochemical study of the solid-state compounds attests to the redox activity of the coordination system. Magnetic investigations reveal the existence of antiferromagnetic interactions between magnetic centers in complexes 3 and 4, while in 1, the paramagnetic metallic centers are isolated, in agreement with the solid-state structure.

### Experimental Section

General Remarks. The precursor, tetrathiafulvalene-tetra- carboxylic ester, was prepared according to a reported method.<sup>13</sup> The compound, tetrathiafulvalene-tetracarboxylate sodium salt(Na<sub>4</sub> L), was obtained by hydrolization of tetrathiafulvalene- tetracarboxylic ester in a NaOH aqueous solution. Standard deionized water was used for syntheses of the complexes. HPLC grade solvents (Alfa Aesar) and doubly distilled water were used for measurements of electrochemistry and spectra. All other reagents or solvents for syntheses and analyses were of analy- tical grade and used as received. The IR spectra were recorded as KBr pellets on a Nicolet Magna 550 FT-IR spectrometer. Elemental analyses of C, H, and N were performed using an EA1110 elemental analyzer. Electronic absorption spectra were measured on a Shimadzu UV-3150 spectrometer. Thermal analysis was conducted on a SDT 2960 TGA-DCS microanalyzer. The samples were heated under a nitrogen stream of 100 mL/min with a heating rate of 20 °C/min. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) of the solid-state compounds was investigated on a CHI600 electrochemistry workstation in a three-electrode system, using a surface-modified Pt-plate working electrode,<sup>14</sup> a Pt wire auxiliary electrode, and a saturated calomel electrode (SCE) as the reference. As a typical technique for the surface-modified electrode, a slurry was prepared using a well-powdered solid-state sample dropped with some acetonitrile, and then the slurry was layed uniformly on the electrode. The surface-modified working electrode was obtained after evaporation of the solvent. The temperature dependence of the magnetic susceptibility of a powdered sample was measured by a Quantum Design SQUID magnetometer on the MPMS-7 system.
