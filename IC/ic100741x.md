We report on the crystal structure of a new polymorph of Li <sub>2</sub>FeSiO<sub>4</sub> (prepared by annealing under argon at 900 °C and quenching to 25 °C) characterized by electron microscopy and powder X-ray and neutron diffraction. The crystal

structure of Li<sub>2</sub> FeSiO<sub>4</sub> quenched from 900 °C is isostructural with Li<sub>2</sub>CdSiO <sub>4</sub>, described in the space group Pmnb with lattice parameters a = 6.2836(1) A˚, b = 10.6572(1) A˚, and c = 5.0386(1) A˚. A close comparison is made with the

structure of Li<sub>2</sub>FeSiO <sub>4</sub> quenched from 700 °C, published recently by Nishimura et al. (J. Am. Chem. Soc. 2008, 130, 13212). The two polymorphs differ mainly on the respective orientations and alternate sequences of corner-sharing

FeO<sub>4</sub> and SiO<sub>4</sub> tetrahedra.

1. Introduction

Lithium transition-metal silicates with the general formula Li<sub>2</sub>MSiO<sub>4</sub> (M=Fe, Mn, Co) have recently attracted research interest since the introduction of Li<sub>2</sub>FeSiO<sub>4</sub> as a potential positive electrode material in lithium batteries in 2001.<sup>1</sup> The incorporation of (SiO<sub>4</sub>)<sup>4-</sup>polyanions (strong Si-O bonds) in the crystal structure is anticipated, as in LiFePO<sub>4</sub>,<sup>2</sup>to result in enhanced electrochemical and chemical stability.<sup>3</sup> The theo- retical capacity of ca. 160 mAh g<sup>-1</sup>, corresponding to 1 Li<sup>þ</sup> extracted per Li<sub>2</sub>MSiO<sub>4</sub>, and the abundance of raw materials (particularly true for Fe and Si) have enhanced their potential to become cathode materials of choice for large-scale battery applications. Additionally, the possibility of extracting up to two Li<sup>þ</sup> per Li<sub>2</sub>MSiO<sub>4</sub> (i.e., operating on both M<sup>2þ</sup>/M<sup>3þ</sup> and M<sup>3þ</sup>/M<sup>4þ</sup> redox couples), especially for manganese,<sup>4,5</sup> would lead to a major breakthrough and is definitely worth a detailed investigation. Recently, the low intrinsic conducti- vity of Li<sub>2</sub>FeSiO<sub>4</sub> has been overcome not only by carbon coating but also by lowering of the particle size, resulting in a satisfactory performance in terms of capacity and cycle life.<sup>4,5</sup>

The proposed structural models for Li<sub>2</sub>FeSiO<sub>4</sub> are derived from Li<sub>3</sub>PO<sub>4</sub>-based structures, in which half of the tetrahe- dral sites, generated by a distorted hexagonal close packing of oxygen atoms, are occupied by cations. Li<sub>3</sub>PO<sub>4</sub> itself crystal- lizes in two main groups of polymorphs (β and γ), which differ in the respective orientations of the filled tetrahedra: all T<sup>þ</sup> (oriented upward) in the low-temperature β form and T<sup>þ</sup> and T <sup>-</sup> (oriented downward) for the high-temperature γ form.<sup>16,17</sup>In even more complicated systems (three types of cations, for instance), the structures of Li<sub>2</sub>MSiO<sub>4</sub> analogues (M=Zn, Mn, Mg, Co) have been reported to adopt “simple” β- or γ-type structures or their distorted derivatives.<sup>18-22</sup>

In this work, we report on the crystal structure of a new polymorph of Li<sub>2</sub>FeSiO<sub>4</sub> (prepared by annealing under argon at 900 °C and quenching to 25 °C) characterized by electron microscopy and powder X-ray and neutron diffraction. We present inductively coupled plasma atomic emission spectro- scopy (ICP-AES) analysis and M€ossbauer spectroscopy data for this new polymorph.

## 2. Experimental Section

2.2. Characterization. The Li:Fe:Si ratio of the obtained sample was determined using an ICP optical emission spectro- meter (Varian 715-ES) equipped with Sturman-Master spray chamber and a V-groove nebulizer. Prior to the analysis, 20 mg of each sample was dissolved in a few drops of concentrated HCl and the obtained solution was diluted up to 100 mL volume. The measurement uncertainty, which includes accuracy and repro- ducibility, was lower than 3% in this set of experiments.

The oxidation number of iron, its local environments, and the purity of the obtained products were analyzed by 57Fe Mossbauer experiments. The measurements were performed at 298 K. The source was <sup>57</sup>Co in a rhodium matrix. Velocity cali- bration and isomer shifts were quoted relative to an absorber of metallic iron at room temperature. The experiments were perfor- med in transmission geometry. Parameter fits were performed using a standard least-squares fitting routine with Lorentzian lines. The X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the Li<sub>2</sub>FeSiO<sub>4</sub> sam- ples were collected at 298 K using a Bruker D8 powder diffract- ometer in Bragg-Brentano θ-θ geometry with the Cu KR radiation. The machine was equipped with a LinxEye detector that allows energy discrimination (to remove part of the fluorescence). The data were recorded in the 2θ range 10-90° with a step size of ≈0.02° (2θ) and a constant counting time of 12 s per step. The patterns were analyzed by full pattern mat- ching and/or Rietveld refinement as implemented in the Fullprof program.<sup>23</sup> The first step of the structure determination was made ab initio using the FOX program.<sup>24</sup>

Time-of-flight powder neutron diffraction data were obtained on the Polaris high-intensity, medium-resolution instrument at ISIS at the Rutherford Appleton Laboratory. Because lithium is a neutron absorber, the data were corrected for absorption. The final structural model was refined by the Rietveld method using the program TOPAS Academic.<sup>25</sup>

Electron diffraction patterns were recorded using a FEI:: TECNAl F20 S-TWIN transmission electron microscope at an accelerating voltage of 200 kV and equipped with a double- tilt specimen holder. The powders were dispersed in acetonitrile, and droplets of these suspensions were then deposited on lacey- carbon grids inside a glovebox. In order to protect the crystal- lites from air exposure (Li <sub>2</sub>FeSiO<sub>4</sub> may be slightly sensitive to air, depending on the relative humidity and on the particle size), grids were set on the holder inside the glovebox and the holder was transferred to the microscope using a homemade device. Electron diffraction experiments were carried out on many crys- tallites, and reproducible results were observed. Particles studied were chosen as isolated as possible and as thin as possible. ## 3. Results and Discussion

As observed by the XRD patterns and scanning electron microscopy micrographs shown in Figure 1a (insets), the LFS@900 sample consists of well-crystallized particles. Owing to the high-temperature treatment, particles were strongly sin- tered and exhibited very large shape and size distributions, ranging from a few micrometers to 100 μm in size. The XRD pattern of the LFS@900 grayish-white powder shows sig- nificant differences in the Bragg positions and intensities in comparison with that of the same composition annealed at 700 °C (LFS@900)<sup>12,14</sup> (Figure 1b,c).

The obtained powder was further characterized by means of ICP and Mossbauer spectroscopy. The Li:Fe:Si ratio was found to be 2.02:1.1:1, i.e., close to the expected stoichio- metric values, given that traces of crystallized Fe<sub>1-x</sub>O were found to coexist with Li<sub>2</sub>FeSiO<sub>4</sub>. Such a small departure from 90° is far beyond the resolution of electron diffraction as well as that of “standard” powder XRD. Full-pattern-matching refinements of XRD data gave very good reliability factors for both space groups: R<sub>p</sub> = 6.2%, R<sub>wp</sub> =7.9%, and χ<sup>2</sup>=86.6 for Pmnb; R<sub>p</sub>=5.8%, R<sub>wp</sub>=7.4%, and χ<sup>2</sup>=75.0 for P2<sub>1</sub>/n, as illustrated in Figure 4. From these considerations only, the (very slight) monoclinic distortion appears plausible, but one should point out that the Bragg positions generated by both space groups are poorly distin- guishable, except in the 33.5 < 2θ<sub>Co KR</sub> < 35.0 region. The absence of any diffracted intensity in this region did not permit one, hence, to attribute with certainty the space group P2<sub>1</sub>/n to LFS@900, and we decided to adopt the Pmnb space group for the full structure determination, described below.

The crystal structure of the new LFS@900 phase was first solved ab initio by the global optimization of a structural model in direct space using simulated annealing (in parallel tempering mode), as implemented in the recently developed program FOX.<sup>24</sup> The initial structure model was then refined using the Rietveld method, and Fourier difference maps were used to check the accord between the electron density distribution and integrated intensities observed in the experi- mental pattern. The powder neutron diffraction pattern was refined in space group Pmnb using as starting coordinates those of Li<sub>2</sub>CdSiO<sub>4</sub>.<sup>29</sup> Fe<sub>1-x</sub>O was added as a second phase.

The refined structure consists of a distorted buckled hexagonal close-packed array of oxygen layers stacked per- pendicularly to the orthorhombic c axis, between which cations occupy half of the tetrahedral sites. Between oxygen “layers”, each cationic “slab” is built from a succession of FeO<sub>4</sub>/SiO<sub>4</sub> and LiO<sub>4</sub> chains running along [100] (Figure 6). Within each type of chain, FeO<sub>4</sub> and SiO<sub>4</sub> tetrahedra alter- nate in opposite directions, while all LiO<sub>4</sub> tetrahedra are pointing in the same direction. Figure 6 illustrates clearly the main structural differences between this new LFS@900 struc- ture and that of LFS@700 proposed by Nishimura et al.<sup>14</sup> While the 333 Fe-Si-Fe-Si-Fe 333 sequence is common to both structures (along [10-1]<sub>LFS@700</sub> equal to [100]<sub>LFS@900</sub>), the respective orientations of FeO<sub>4</sub> and SiO<sub>4</sub> tetrahedra along [010]<sub>LFS@700</sub> = [001]<sub>LFS@900</sub> are different: up-up-down- down-up-up-down-down for LFS@700 up-down-up- down-up-down-up-down for LFS@900. ## 4. Conclusion

We have found and described here the crystal structure of a new polymorph for Li<sub>2</sub>FeSiO<sub>4</sub>, quenched from 900 °C, thanks to precise complementary studies through electron microscopy, XRD, and neutron diffraction. It is isostructural with Li<sub>2</sub>CdSiO<sub>4</sub>, described in the space group Pmnb, with lattice parameters a=6.2836(1) A, b=10.6572(1) A, and c= 5.0386(1) A. It differs from the structure recently published by Nishimura et al. (quenched from 800 °C<sup>14</sup>) by the res- pective orientations and alternating sequences of corner- sharing FeO<sub>4</sub> and SiO<sub>4</sub> tetrahedra.

This study offers an interesting basis for the study of the electrochemical properties of such a composition. In close con- nection with the density functional theory calculations under- way<sup>17</sup> and precise electrochemical measurements/titrations, we investigate how the kinetics (rate at which Li<sup>þ</sup> can be extracted/inserted) and thermodynamics (equilibrium poten- tial for the Fe<sup>3þ</sup>/Fe<sup>2þ</sup> redox couple) of this material would be affected.
