It is well-known that cyano complexes of d-electron-rich Fe(II), Ru(II), and Os(II) show significant solvent dependence in their various properties, including metal-centered redox potentials.<sup>52</sup> The solvent dependence has been interpreted in terms of the Lewis acid (solvent)−base (free nitrogen site of CN<sup>−</sup> ligand) interaction. With a more acidic solvent, the redox potential appears at a more positive potential region, as the π-back-bonding is more favorable to make the metal center more difficult to oxidize. In a poorly acidic solvent such as CH<sub>2</sub>Cl<sub>2</sub>, the π-back-bonding is less favorable and even a simple π-donation is possible. π-Donor−acceptor properties of the metal−CN<sup>−</sup> bond should also depend on the type of metal center; the π-back-donation is less significant with a lesser number of d electrons.<sup>53</sup> Thus, it is expected that the CN<sup>−</sup> ligands coordinated to poor π-donating Ru <sub>2</sub>(μ-O)(μ-CH <sub>3</sub>COO)<sub>2</sub> and Ru<sub>3</sub>(μ<sub>3</sub>-O)(μ-CH<sub>3</sub> COO)<sub>6</sub> (vide infra) cores are less favorable for π-back-donation.
