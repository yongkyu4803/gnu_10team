ABSTRACT: The novel supramolecular complexes, which are composed of an [Os(5dmb) <sub>2</sub>(BL)]<sup>2+</sup>-type complex (5dmb = 5,5′-dimethyl-2,2′-bipyridine; BL

= 1,2-bis(4′-methyl-[2,2′-bipyridin]-4-yl)ethane) as a photosensitizer and cis,trans-[Re(BL)(CO)<sub>2</sub>{P(p-X-C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>4</sub>)<sub>3</sub>}<sub>2</sub>]<sup>+</sup>-type complexes (X = F, Cl) as a catalyst, have been synthesized. They photocatalyzed selective reduction of

CO <sub>2</sub> to CO under red-light irradiation (λ > 620 nm). The photocatalytic abilities were affected by the phosphine ligands on the Re unit, and the supramolecule with P(p-Cl-C <sub>6</sub>H<sub>4</sub>)<sub>3</sub> ligands exhibited better photocatalysis (Φ<sub>CO</sub> = 0.12, TON<sub>CO</sub> = 1138, TOF<sub>CO</sub> = 3.3 min<sup>−1</sup>). The detailed studies clarified the electron balance and material balance; i.e., one molecule of the sacrificial electron donor (1,3-dimethyl-2-phenyl-2,3-dihydro-1H-benzo[d]- imidazole (BIH)) donated two electrons, one molecule of CO<sub>2</sub> accepted the two electrons, and another CO<sub>2</sub> molecule served as an “O<sup>2−</sup>” acceptor to give each molecule of the two-electron oxidized compound of BIH, CO, and HCO<sub>3</sub><sup>−</sup>.

### ■ CONCLUSION

We successfully developed red-light-driven supramolecular photocatalysts composed with an Os(II) tris-diimine complex as a photosensitizer and a Re(I) complex as a catalyst. Irradiation of the photocatalysts in the presence of BIH as a reductant using light at λ > 620 nm caused selective reduction of CO<sub>2</sub> to CO. The phosphine ligands on the Re catalyst unit affected its photocatalytic abilities, and the photocatalyst with P(p-Cl-C <sub>6</sub>H<sub>4</sub>)<sub>3</sub> ligands exhibited better photocatalytic activity (Φ<sub>CO</sub> = 0.12, TON<sub>CO</sub> = 1138, TOF<sub>CO</sub> = 3.3 min<sup>−1</sup>) than that with P(p-F-C<sub>6</sub>H <sub>4</sub>)<sub>3</sub> ligands. The photocatalytic reactions proceeded via (1) light-absorption by the Os unit, (2) electron transfer from BIH to the excited Os unit, (3) intramolecular electron transfer from OER species of the Os unit to the Re unit, and (4) CO<sub>2</sub> reduction on the Re unit giving CO and HCO<sub>3</sub><sup>−</sup>.
