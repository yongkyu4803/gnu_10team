The reaction of [Ru<sup>VI</sup>(N)(L)(MeOH)\](PF <sub>6</sub>) [1; L = N,N<sup>0</sup>-bis(salicylidene)-o-cyclohexyldiamine dianion] with a stoichiometric amount of RSH in CH<sub>3</sub>CN gives the corresponding (salen)ruthenium(IV) sulfilamido species [Ru<sup>IV</sup>{N(H)SR}(L)(NCCH<sub>3</sub>)\](PF <sub>6</sub>) (2a, R = <sup>t</sup>Bu; 2b, R = Ph). Metathesis of 2a with NaN<sub>3</sub> in methanol affords [Ru<sup>IV</sup>{N(H)S<sup>t</sup>Bu}(L)(N<sub>3</sub>)] (2c). 2a undergoes further reaction with 1 equiv of RSH to afford a (salen)ruthenium(III) sulfilamine species, [Ru<sup>III</sup>{N(H)<sub>2</sub>S<sup>t</sup>Bu}(L)(NCCH<sub>3</sub>)\](PF <sub>6</sub>) (3). On the other hand, 2b reacts with 2 equiv of PhSH to give a (salen)ruthenium(III) ammine species [Ru<sup>III</sup> (NH<sub>3</sub>)(L)(NCCH<sub>3</sub>)\](PF <sub>6</sub>) (4); this species can also be prepared by treatment of 1 with 3 equiv of PhSH. The X-ray structures of 2c and 4 have been determined. Kinetic studies of the reaction of 1 with excess RSH indicate the following schemes: 1 f 2a f 3 (R = <sup>t</sup>Bu), 1 f 2b f 4 (R = Ph). The conversion of 1 to 2 probably involves nucleophilic attack of RSH at the nitrido ligand, followed by a proton shift. The conversions of 2a to 3 and 2b to 4 are proposed to involve rate-limiting H-atom abstraction from RSH by 2a or 2b. 2a and 2b are also able to abstract H atoms from hydrocarbons with weak C-H bonds. These reactions occur with large deuterium isotope effects; the kinetic isotope effect values for the oxidation of 9,10-dihydroanthracene, 1,4-cyclohexadiene, and fluorene by 2a are 51, 56, and 11, respectively.

Materials. The complex [Ru<sup>VI</sup>(N)(L)(MeOH)\](PF<sub>6</sub>) (1) was prepared by a literature method.<sup>8</sup> The Schiff base ligand H<sub>2</sub>L [L=N,N<sup>0</sup>-bis(salicylidene)-o-cyclohexylenediamine dianion] was synthesized by condensation of salicylaldehyde with trans-1,2-cyclohexyldiamine in refluxing ethanol. Thiols (Aldrich) were freshly distilled over anhydrous CaCl<sub>2</sub> before use. tBuSD and PhSD (>95% D by 1H NMR) were prepared by the direct deuterium exchange of tBuSH and PhSH with 99.8% D<sub>2</sub>O (ARMAR AG), and the processes were repeated three times.<sup>11</sup> 9,10-Dihydroanthracene (DHA; Aldrich, 97%), xanthene (Aldrich, 99%), fluorene (Aldrich, 98%), diphenylmethane (Aldrich, 99%), and triphenylmethane (Aldrich, 99%) were re- crystallized twice from ethanol. 1,4-Cyclohexadiene (CHD) was dried over CaH<sub>2</sub> and then distilled under dinitrogen. DHA-d<sub>4</sub> and CHD-d<sub>8</sub> [both >99% D by 1H NMR and gas chromatogra- phy-mass spectrometry (GC-MS)] were prepared by a literature method.<sup>12</sup> <sup>n</sup>Bu<sub>4</sub>NPF<sub>6</sub> (Aldrich) for electrochemistry was recrys- tallized three times from boiling ethanol and dried in vacuo at 120 °C for 24 h. Acetonitrile (Aldrich) for electrochemistry was distilled over calcium hydride. All other chemicals were of reagent grade and were used without further purification.

Instrumentation. IR spectra were recorded as KBr pellets on a Nicolet Avatar 360 FT-IR spectrophotometer at 4 cm<sup>-1</sup> resolu- tion. Elemental analyses were done on an Elementar Vario EL analyzer. <sup>1</sup>H NMR spectra were recorded on a Bruker (400 MHz) FT-NMR spectrometer. The chemical shifts (δ, ppm) were reported with reference to tetramethylsilane. UV/vis spectra were recorded with a Perkin-Elmer Lamda 19 spectrophotometer in 1 cm quartz cuvettes. Magnetic measurement (solid sample, Gouy method) was performed at 20 °C using a Sherwood magnetic balance (Mark II). Electrospray ionization mass spec- trometry (ESI/MS) spectra were obtained on a PE SCIEX API 365 mass spectrometer. The analyte solution was continuously infused with a syringe pump at a constant flow rate of 5 μL min<sup>-1</sup> into the pneumatically assisted electrospray probe, with nitrogen as the nebulizing gas. The declustering potential was typically set at 10-20 V. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) was performed with a PAR model 273 potentiostat using a glassy carbon working electrode, a Ag/AgNO<sub>3</sub> (0.1 M in CH<sub>3</sub>CN) reference electrode, and a Pt wire counter electrode with ferrocene (Cp<sub>2</sub>Fe) as the internal standard. GC analyses were performed on a HP 7890 GC/FID equipped with a HP-5MS (30 m 0.25 mm i.d.) column. GC-MS measurements were carried out on a HP 6890 gas chromatograph interfaced to a HP 5975 mass-selective detector.

## Concluding Remarks

The electrophilic (salen)ruthenium(VI) nitrido species 1 undergoes stepwise reactions with RSH to produce the first examples of sulfilamido and sulfilamine complexes of ruthenium. The direct reduction of 1 to [Ru<sup>III</sup>(NH<sub>3</sub>)(L)-(CH<sub>3</sub>CN)]<sup>þ</sup> with PhSH also represents a new method for the conversion of a nitrido species to an ammine. The ruthenium(IV) sulfilamido species, generated from the addi- tion of 1 equiv of RSH to 1, is able to abstract H atoms from hydrocarbons containing weak C-H bonds. Our preliminary results show that similar ruthenium(IV) species, generated from the addition of 1 equiv of various other nucleophiles such as PPh<sub>3</sub> or 2,3-dimethyl-2-butene,<sup>9</sup> are also able to abstract H atoms from hydrocarbons. Thus, a new class of C-H bond-activating reagents can be readily obtained by the treatment of Ru<sup>VI</sup>tN with a suitable nucleophile.
