# Fluoride: Solution- and Solid-State Structural Binding Probe

### Sung Ok Kang, Victor W. Day, and Kristin Bowman-James\*

Department of Chemistry, University of Kansas, Lawrence, Kansas 66045

kbjames@ku.edu

Received July 26, 2009

Solid-state and solution studies were performed to determine if F<sup>-</sup> is encapsulated by anion hosts in

both media. X-ray crystal structure determinations were compared with both <sup>1</sup>H and <sup>19</sup> F solution

NMR data. Three hosts were studied: (1) two polyamide hosts, one with isophthaloyl spacers and

the other with pyridine spacers, and (2) a polythioamide host with pyridine spacers. Binding

studies showed that the pyridine-containing amide cryptand shows the highest affinity (K<sub>a</sub> > 10<sup>5</sup>

in DMSO-d<sub>6</sub>), with the other hosts at least a factor of 10 lower. All of the cryptands appear to

encapsulate F<sup>-</sup> in solution, where a deuterium-exchange reaction with DMSO-d<sub>6</sub> can be monitored

by <sup>19</sup> F NMR. Four crystal structures are reported and compared: two for the pyridine-containing

free base hosts and two for encapsulated F<sup>-</sup> complexes of the two amide-based cryptands.

Introduction

The binding of anions with supramolecular hosts is a fascinating and growing field.<sup>1,2</sup> Researchers have relied on a variety of methods to investigate structural interactions and binding propensities. X-ray crystallography has histori- cally been the method of choice for definitive structural answers because it gives an atomic-level view of host/guest interactions, albeit in the solid state. A variety of titration techniques have been used to determine anion-binding con- stants in solution. 1H NMR Studies. The <sup>1</sup>H NMR spectra of F<sup>-</sup> with the cryptands L1-L3 in DMSO-d<sub>6</sub> indicate proton coupling with the internally held I = 1/2F<sup>-</sup>. A new doublet amide NH signal at 11.85 ppm appears upon addition of F<sup>-</sup> to L1 (J<sub>HF</sub> = 27 Hz) (Figure 1a), and a similar pattern is observed for the thioamide cryptand L3. Both the amide and iso- phthaloyl H atoms in L2 couple with F<sup>-</sup> (J<sub>HF</sub> = 14 Hz) (Figure 1b). Similar coupling of pyrrole NH with bound F<sup>-</sup> has also been observed by Sessler and Gale.<sup>10,11</sup>

19F NMR Studies. The F<sup>-</sup> signal shifts upfield from free F<sup>-</sup> at -96.7 to bound F<sup>-</sup> at -111.7 ppm upon addition of L1 to a solution of F<sup>-</sup> in DMSO-d<sub>6</sub>. The new signal appears as a septet (J<sub>HF</sub> = 27 Hz) due to coupling of F<sup>-</sup> with the six equivalent amide H atoms (no. of multiplets = 2nI þ 1, where I = nuclear spin and n = number of atoms) (Figure 2a,b). These findings provide further confirmation that F<sup>-</sup> is present as an inclusion complex in solution. A similar upfield shift is observed with the thioamide L3. However, in addition to the septet (J<sub>HF</sub> = 25 Hz), the spectrum consists of a series of multiplets (Figure 2d), which are the result of deuterium exchange as described below.

This multiplet was subse- quently determined to be an unresolved dectet that resulted from F<sup>-</sup> coupling with the six amide and three phenyl H atoms. The upfield chemical shifts of the F<sup>-</sup> signal with L1 and L3 can probably be attributed to the presence of the lone pair of electrons on the pyridine N atoms, which tends to shield the F<sup>-</sup>; the downfield shift observed for L2 is most likely the result of the ring current of aromatic ring. Similar H-bonding coupling of bound F<sup>-</sup> in 19F NMR has previously been observed with pyrrole NH protons in calix[4]pyrrole-fluoride complexes.<sup>11</sup>

Fluoride Binding Studies. All three hosts display high affinity for F<sup>-</sup>, as anticipated by the strong H-bonding tendency of this ion. L1, L2, and L3 all showed slow exchange in DMSO-d<sub>6</sub> upon addition of up to 1 equiv of [n-Bu<sub>4</sub>N<sup>þ</sup>][F<sup>-</sup>] at room temperature. Calculations using EQNMR<sup>13</sup> indicate very high binding for L1 at the limits of NMR detectability (K = 8.1 10<sup>5</sup>) and binding slightly more than a factor of 10 lower for L2 (K = 3.3 10<sup>4</sup>) and L3 (K = 3.2 10<sup>4</sup>). In general, for simple amido-based mono- cycles, the pyridine analogue displays significantly higher binding than the isophthaloyl derivative, so the superior binding of L1 is as anticipated. We and others have attribu- ted this to the preorganization effect of the pyridine N atom on the nearby amide H atoms.<sup>14,15</sup>

The lessened affinity for L3 for F<sup>-</sup>, however, is not easily explained. In the monocycles, the thioamides show heigh- tened binding for anions as we communicated earlier.<sup>9</sup> Indeed, the rationale for exploring thioamides was for their enhanced H-bonding capability that we predicted would result in higher anion affinities.<sup>16</sup> The crystal structures of the two free bases, described below, do not shed light on the situation, since both the amido- and thioamido- cryptand are structurally very similar and almost superimposable. The only real experimental difference between the two hosts, then, is in the <sup>19</sup> F NMR, and involves the exchange process. As seen in Figure 2d, the thioamide L3 undergoes amide H atom exchange when immediately placed in solution, while the exchange process for the corollary amides L1 and L2 is slower, occurring over days. It is possible that the enhanced acidity of the thioamide group works against binding, since in a dissociative mechanism for D exchange the host would bear a negative or repelling charge to an incoming F<sup>-</sup>during any given instance in the rapid exchange process.

## Conclusions

Bicyclic cryptand hosts with differing H-bond donor functional groups (amide vs thioamide) and spacers (pyri- dine vs isophthaloyl) have been synthesized by simple one- or two-step procedures. The similar cryptand frameworks have allowed for an evaluation of anion-binding propensities and structural influences. The effect of preorganization has also been assessed by comparing L1 hosts containing pyridine (prone to preorganize adjacent amides) and L2 hosts containing isophthaloyl (no preorganization of adjacent amides) spacers. Binding studies indicated that the preorganized pyridine host L1 showed superiority in F<sup>-</sup> binding.

The encapsulation of the F<sup>-</sup> in L1 and L2 was confirmed by crystal structures as well as solution <sup>19</sup> F NMR studies. Coupling between the F<sup>-</sup> and the amide H atoms as well as the isophthaloyl H atoms in L2 was observed. The strong H-bonding interactions between amides and the bound F<sup>-</sup> facilitated an interesting deuterium exchange of amide NHs in solution. The crystal structures of the two solvated free bases, the amide and thioamide, were virtually superimpo- sable. Both showed a stacking of two of the pyridine arms, indicating a preference for this interaction and the resulting conformation. In the two F<sup>-</sup>complexes the crystal structures showed that macrocycles were pulled in to surround the strongly bound F<sup>-</sup> ion.

These studies emphasize the utility of F<sup>-</sup>ion as a probe for both solid-state and solution studies of anion binding. They also settle the question of whether the anion remains en- capsulated in solution and reveal some unanticipated chem- istry, the D exchange reaction. Finally, these findings add to the growing information base and understanding of the aptitude of amide- and thioamide-based cryptands as hosts for anions.
