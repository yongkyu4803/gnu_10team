## Electrocatalytic Activity of Gold and Gold Nanoparticles Improved by Electrochemical

## Pretreatment

Jun Ho Shim, Jiyoung Kim, Chongmok Lee,\* and Youngmi Lee\*

Department of Chemistry and Nano Science, Ewha Womans UniVersity, Seoul 120-750, Korea

ReceiVed: July 20, 2010; ReVised Manuscript ReceiVed: NoVember 4, 2010

The electrocatalytic activity of bulk Au and Au nanoparticles (AuNPs) toward the oxygen reduction reaction

(ORR), before and after the electrochemical pretreatment, was investigated in a 0.05 M phosphate buffer

solution (pH ) 7.4). Both the Au and AuNPs were pretreated by repetitive potential cycling between -0.2

and +1.0 V (vs SCE). Rotating disk electrode (RDE) voltammetric studies showed that a more favorable

ORR occurred at the AuNPs than at the Au. Interestingly, increased number of electrons (n) transferred in

the ORR (∼4) and more positive ORR onset potential were observed at both the Au and AuNPs after the

pretreatment. In particular, for the bulk Au, a simple electrochemical pretreatment produced remarkably

improved ORR activity, i.e., a considerable positive shift in the ORR onset potential by ∼300 mV as well as

a significant increase in the n number (from <2 to ∼4). The greatest catalytic activity was observed at the

pretreated AuNPs. The enhanced catalytic activity by the electrochemical pretreatment was attributed to the

formation of thick Au oxide layers, which was also demonstrated with a thermally pretreated Au microwire

electrode. The pretreated Au and AuNPs also showed higher activity toward the oxidation of hydrogen peroxide

than the corresponding untreated ones.

Introduction

Noble metals are used widely in a range of electrochemical applications, such as electrocatalysis, electrochemical analysis, electrochemical synthesis, etc., owing to their inertness and catalytic properties, generally through chemisorption on the surface. The electrocatalytic activity of the metals is of great interest, particularly in the field of fuel cells. Pt is the most commonly used cathode catalyst in fuel cells, as it shows high activity toward a sluggish oxygen reduction reaction (ORR). Recently, there have been an increasing number of studies searching for more efficient electrocatalysts as a replacement for the expensive and rare Pt.

In general, Au is regarded as a poor catalyst for ORR due to the weak chemisorption properties caused by the filled d-band.<sup>1-3</sup> To improve the activity of Au, there have been a series of efforts using the following approaches: (i) increasing the surface area using nanoparticles (NPs) <sup>4-7</sup> or nanoporous structures,<sup>6</sup> (ii) employing a single crystalline structure,<sup>4-10</sup> (iii) modification with second metals,<sup>5,10</sup> and (iv) a combination of these methods. Some examples of these efforts are as follows. In fact, the Au(100) facet<sup>4,5</sup> and Au(100)-like AuNPs <sup>6</sup> were reported to show high catalytic activity toward the ORR. The particle-size dependent ORR activity of AuNPs was studied. <sup>7</sup> Single crystal Au(111) exhibited an enhanced positive catalytic effect for the ORR by surface modification with Cu and Ag ad-layers. <sup>8</sup>

The effective ORR via the four-electron-transfer pathway to form water was confirmed at nanoporous Au made by dealloying Ag/Au alloys.<sup>9</sup> More recently, the catalytic activity of Au-based bimetallic NPs was reported to be superior to that of each separate metal. <sup>10</sup> The effect of the particle size of AuNPs on the kinetics of the ORR in acidic medium was examined, and greater catalytic activity was confirmed on the smaller AuNPs.<sup>11</sup> However, the approaches described here are rather limited

because they include difficult and complicated fabrication and modification processes.

The electrocatalytic activity of noble metals can be altered dramatically by surface pretreatments: the first discovery was the enhancement of Pt activity by pulsing between the anodic and cathodic potentials.<sup>12,13</sup> With regard to Au, activation with electrochemical,<sup>14-17</sup> thermal,<sup>16-18</sup> and chemical pretreatments<sup>19,20</sup> was reported. Indeed, the catalytic effects of activated Au were investigated mostly for oxidation reactions rather than reduction reactions. The oxidation of hydrogen,<sup>14</sup> hydroxide,<sup>15</sup> some organic substances (diethyl amine, hydrazine, glyoxal),<sup>17</sup> and hydrogen peroxide<sup>20</sup> has been reported. On the other hand, there are relatively few reports on the application of activated Au for reduction reactions, such as nitrobenzene<sup>18</sup> and dichromate.<sup>21</sup> However, none of these were for the ORR. This paper reports the significantly enhanced catalytic activity of Au, which was activated electrochemically by repetitive potential cycling, toward the ORR as well as the oxidation of H<sub>2</sub> O2 under neutral conditions, which are important in the area of biofuel cells and biosensors.

Experimental Methods

Chemicals and Materials. Hydrogen tetrachloroaurate tri- hydrate (HAuCl <sub>4</sub> · 3H<sub>2</sub>O), sodium citrate (Na<sub>3</sub>C <sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>O<sub>7</sub> · 2H<sub>2</sub>O), NaBH <sub>4</sub>, sodium phosphate monobasic (NaH<sub>2</sub>PO<sub>4</sub> , g 99%), sodium phosphate dibasic (Na<sub>2</sub>HPO<sub>4</sub>, g 99%), hexaammineru- thenium(III) chloride (Ru(NH<sub>3</sub>)6Cl<sub>3</sub>), and chitosan (MW ∼1 × 10<sup>6</sup> Da; ∼80% deacetylation) were purchased from Sigma- Aldrich (St. Louis, MO, USA). AuNP Synthesis. AuNPs (∼10 nm diameter) were synthe- sized using a similar method to the one previously described.<sup>22</sup> First, 20 mL of 1.0 mM HAuCl<sub>4</sub> and 2 mL of 38.8 mM sodium citrate aqueous solutions were mixed with magnetic stirring. Subsequently, 50 μL of NaBH <sub>4</sub> (0.75%) in water was added slowly to this solution until the solution color changed. Magnetic stirring was stopped once the color of the solution turned a deep red color. The prepared solution containing AuNPs was stored under refrigerated dark conditions before and after use. The sizes of the AuNPs were confirmed by ultraviolet/visible spectroscopy (UV-vis, HP 8453, Hewlett-Packard Wilmington, DE, USA) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM, JEM 1230, JEOL, Japan) operated at 80 kV. The field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM) images of the surface of a AuNP-loaded GC electrode were obtained using a JSM-6700 (JEOL, Japan). The phase structures were examined by X-ray diffraction (XRD; Siemens D500, with a Cu KR radiation) for bulk Au and AuNPs. Fabrication of Au Microwire Electrode. Au microwire (∼30-mm long and 12.5 μm diameter) was contacted electrically to a tungsten rod using silver conductive epoxy. The Au/tungsten rod was inserted with caution into a ∼5 cm long tapered glass capillary leaving out a ∼10 mm long Au wire from the end of the capillary. The surface of the Au microwire electrode was heated electrically with nichrome coiled wire (∼800 °C) for 60 s and allowed to cool to room temperature. The heating process also caused the glass capillary in which the Au wire was sealed to melt with a 10 mm length protruding from the glass capillary end.

Electrodes and Electrochemical Pretreatments. Au and glassy carbon (GC) rotating disk electrodes (BASi, A ) 0.071 cm<sup>2</sup>), which served as the working electrode, were polished to a mirror finish using 0.3 and 0.05 μm alumina slurries. After alumina polishing, the electrodes were sonicated in a water bath for 10 min. Five microliters of the prepared AuNP colloidal solution was loaded onto the clean surface of a GC electrode and then dried at room temperature. The loading/drying processes were repeated five times. Subsequently, 10 μL of a chitosan solution (0.01 wt %) was transferred to a AuNP-loaded GC electrode to adhere the AuNPs to the GC substrate electrode. The chitosan solution was prepared by dissolving chitosan flakes in acetic acid (0.5%, v/v) followed by filtration through a syringe filter (0.45 μm Millex-HA, Millipore) to remove the coarse undissolved flakes and impurities. The Au disk and AuNP- modified GC (AuNP/GC) electrodes were pretreated electro- chemically by potential scanning between -0.2 and +1.0 V (vs saturated calomel reference electrode, SCE) for 200 cycles at a scan rate of 50 mV s<sup>-1</sup> with a Pt counter electrode (coiled). Electrochemical Measurements. RDE voltammetry and cyclic voltammetry (CV) were carried out using a RDE-1 rotor/ Epsilon electrochemical analyzer (Bioanalytical Systems Inc., IN, USA) and a 1000A multipotentiostat (CH Instruments TX, USA), respectively. A Pt coil counter electrode and SCE reference electrode were used in all electrochemical measure- ments. ORR was examined in an O<sub>2</sub>-saturated 0.05 M phosphate buffer solution (pH 7.4) prepared with triple-distilled water. The solution was purged with O<sub>2</sub> gas for 30 min before each measurement, and O <sub>2</sub> gas was flowed over the solution during the experiments.

Characterization of the AuNPs. The synthesized AuNPs were characterized in terms of their size distribution by TEM and UV-vis spectroscopy. Electrochemical Characterization of the Au Catalysts. As described in the Experimental Section, the Au disk and AuNP/GC electrodes were pretreated electrochemically by repetitive potential cycling. Meanwhile, the results show that the pretreatment process affects the ORR catalytic activity of Au and AuNPs. In fact, after the pretreatment, the onset of ORR at the tAu was shifted significantly to a more positive potential (∼300 mV) and became comparable to one of the uAuNPs. Indeed, the beneficial effects (in terms of onset potential shift, increased current densities) were observed almost identically for the Au catalysts pretreated with Au counter electrode instead of Pt, indicating the improved activity is not caused by the catalyst contamination with Pt.

Thermal Pretreatment. The experimental results show that the catalytic activity of the Au and AuNPs was improved by the electrochemical pretreatment. The improved activity might be induced by the formation of a thick Au oxide layer, as evidenced by the CV characterization of the Au catalysts before and after the pretreatment.

The ORR activity of Au on which the thick Au oxide layer was grown by the thermal pretreatment was examined to confirm the effect of the Au oxide on the ORR. Indeed, linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) was carried out with a Au microwire electrode (ca. 10 mm long and 12.5 μm diameter), which was fabricated as described in the Experimental Section, before and after the thermal pretreatment. The Au microwire electrode was pretreated thermally by heating electrically in a nichrome coiled wire (ca. 800 °C) for 60 s and allowed to cool to room temperature. The thermally pretreated Au microwire electrode was moved from air to an O<sub>2</sub>-saturated solution until the end of the Au microwire first touched the air/solution interface using a micromanipulator. Electrode movement was then stopped, and LSV was run. Figure 5 shows the LSV results. In the O<sub>2</sub>-saturated solution, the ORR onset potential of the Au microwire electrode exhibited a great positive shift after the thermal pretreatment (i.e., -0.2 V vs +0.04 V, before vs after the treatment, respectively, a Au wire after the thermal pre- treatment is denoted as thAu). A similar positive shift in the ORR onset potential was also observed at the Au disk and AuNPs after the electrochemical pretreatment (repetitive po- tential cycling). The observed thermal pretreatment effect on the ORR activity of the Au also shows that the Au oxide layer plays a key role in the ORR. The XPS spectra of Au also support the formation of Au oxide on the Au surface after the pretreatment. (See the Supporting Information.) Indeed, the Au oxide layer presumably enables O <sub>2</sub> to be chemisorped more favorably than the bare Au surface.

Conclusions

This study examined the effect of the electrochemical pretreatment by repetitive potential cycling on the electrocata- lytic activity of bulk Au and AuNPs under neutral conditions. The RDE voltammetric studies showed that the ORR became more favorable and efficient at both the Au and AuNPs after the pretreatment. Indeed, more positive onset potentials, higher current densities, and an increase in the n value involved (nearly to 4) were observed at the tAu and the tAuNPs compared to the corresponding untreated ones. In particular, for the bulk Au, a considerable positive shift in the ORR onset potential (∼ 300 mV) and a great increase in the n value (<2 to ∼4) were obtained using a simple electrochemical pretreatment. Considering all

the experimental results, the observed catalytic activity was in the order of tAuNPs > uAuNPs > tAu . uAu. The increase in the ORR, catalytic activity by the electrochemical pretreat- ment was presumably due to the formation of thick Au oxide layers. The tAu and tAuNPs also exhibited an improved activity for the oxidation of hydrogen peroxide.
