# Mechanism of O<sub>2</sub> Production from Water Splitting: Nature of Charge Carriers in Nitrogen Doped Nanocrystalline TiO<sub>2</sub> Films

and Factors Limiting O<sub>2</sub> Production

## Junwang Tang,\*<sup>,†,§</sup> Alexander J. Cowan,<sup>†</sup>

†

## † and David R. Klug<sup>†</sup>

## James R. Durrant,

Department of Chemistry, Imperial College London, Exhibition Road, London SW7 2AZ, United Kingdom

bS Supporting Information

|  | ABSTRACT: The low efficiency of the extensively investigated visible light photocatalyst N-TiO<sub>2</sub> has been widely assumed to be determined by the dynamics of the charge carriers. The nature of the photoelectrons and photoholes produced on the nanostruc- tured (nc) N-TiO<sub>2</sub> film has been systematically investigated in this work by the use of time-resolved absorption spectroscopy. Here the fingerprints of the two distinct photohole populations on nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> films are reported and the reaction between these photoholes and water has been examined. The origin of the low efficiency of the visible-driven material for water oxidation was explored and rapid electron hole decay following visible excitation is believed to be a key factor. Pt deposition on nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> resulted in an 80% enhancement of the quantum yield for O<sub>2</sub> production under UV light. Finally, it has been summarized that the oxygen production on the nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> film requires photoholes with lifetimes of ∼0.4s. |

The amount of solar energy incident on the earth’s surface in a single hour is in excess of that generated annually by fossil fuel consumption, nuclear power, and other renewable energy tech- nologies.<sup>1</sup> However to harness this abundant energy supply, it is necessary to convert and store the solar energy, ideally into a chemical fuel. The photochemical splitting of water can produce hydrogen, which could be utilized directly as a fuel and coupled to other chemical processes such as CO<sub>2</sub> reduction to produce carbon-based fuels such as methanol in artificial photosynthesis. Pioneering research performed by Fujishima et al. demonstrated that UV light driven photocatalytic water oxidation can occur on single crystals of TiO<sub>2</sub> photoannodes, with hydrogen production occurring at a platinum counter electrode.<sup>2</sup> Since this initial report, there has been intense research into developing new effi- cient semiconductor photocatalysts for the oxidation of water.<sup>3</sup> While considerable numbers of new materials have been devel- oped for photochemical water splitting, to date there is not a material that meets the proposed minimum commercial require- ments.<sup>4</sup> Mechanistic research of current materials offers a route to obtain important design parameters for the next generation of water photo-oxidation catalysts.<sup>5</sup>

TiO<sub>2</sub> has been widely studied as a model system for a range of photocatalytic processes; however, the large band gap of TiO<sub>2</sub> limits its photoactivity to the UV region of the solar spectrum. A clear way to enhance the efficiency of TiO<sub>2</sub> would be to extend its photoactivity into the visible region of the spectrum. Over the past twenty years, there has been extensive

also been no reported measurement of O<sub>2</sub> production by photocatalytic water splitting over N-TiO<sub>2</sub> under visible illumination, although incident photon to current efficiency (IPCE) measurements have indicated this may be occurring under visible illumination with a very low efficiency.<sup>8</sup> A systematic and fundamental investigation into the poor activity of this low-cost, nontoxic and visible-light-driven material has implications for both the study of organic degradation reactions and for the photolysis of water under visible irradiation.

In this work, we examine the lack of visible light driven water splitting on a nanocrystalline (nc) N-TiO<sub>2</sub> by using transient absorption spectroscopy (TAS) to monitor the dynamics of the photoelectrons and photoholes produced under both UV and visible illumination. TAS has been previously used to study nanocrystalline (nc) TiO<sub>2</sub> . Following UV excitation initial trap- ping of photoelectrons and photoholes at the surface of the nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> particles occurs in less than 500 ps.<sup>21-23</sup> The trapped charge carriers can then participate in reduction and oxidation chemistry such as water oxidation, oxygen reduction, or organic material oxidation. We have recently reported that the rate of water oxidation by photoholes on nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> is very slow at neutral pH’s (∼0.3 s)<sup>5</sup> and that electron-hole recombination occurs very rapidly with large losses occurring on the submillisecond time scale, even in positively biased nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> films,<sup>24</sup> leading to low efficiencies for photocatalytic water oxidation on nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> . Nakamura et al., investigated the activity of the visible light generated photoholes on N-TiO<sub>2</sub> using IPCE measurements and concluded that the photoholes were trapped at N-induced mid gap level from which direct oxidation of organic materials such as methanol could not occur.<sup>8</sup> Instead, it was proposed that the observed organic degradation occurred via reactions with either reduced oxygen or surface intermediates from the photo-oxidation of water. Tachikawa et al. have examined the activity of visible light generated photoholes on doped TiO<sub>2</sub> powders<sup>25</sup> and have examined the activity of N-TiO<sub>2</sub> toward ethylene glycol oxida- tion using TAS.<sup>26</sup> TAS spectra recorded in acetonitrile in the presence and absence of ethylene glycol were only slightly different and a lack of change in the photoelectron dynamics led the authors to conclude that although trapped photoholes were generated under visible light these were not able to directly oxidize the ethylene glycol. Instead, it was proposed that the mechanism of ethylene glycol degradation was also via reaction with reduced oxygen or water splitting intermediates, in agree- ment with the work of Nakamura et al.<sup>8</sup> Despite the importance of the potential role of water oxidation in the activity of N-TiO<sub>2</sub> for both organic decomposition and solar hydrogen production, the reaction of visible light excited photoholes with water has not been directly investigated yet.

In this paper, the charge carriers’dynamics on the synthesized nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> films are measured and compared to the undoped nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> films in an attempt to explore the nature of the factors limiting the efficiency of photochemical processes. By corre- lating the activity and lifetime of the observed photoholes to measured O<sub>2</sub> production yields over nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> important conclusions regarding the photocatalytic oxidation of both organic materials and water on nc-N-TiO <sub>2</sub> can be drawn. Furthermore, we demonstrate how these mechanistic findings can be used to make a simple material modification that leads to a large enhancement in photocatalytic water oxidation efficiency on nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> .

## ’ EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

Preparation and Physical Characterization of nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> Films. nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> films were made by high-temperature nitridi- zation of nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> films. nc-TiO <sub>2</sub> suspensions were prepared by aqueous hydrolysis of titanium isopropoxide as previously des- cribed.<sup>5,27</sup> All regents were commercial grade and used as recei- ved from Sigma-Aldrich or their subsidiaries, unless stated other- wise. Microscope glass slides, used as the substrate for these films, were cleaned by sonication in acetone for 30 min, then in de- ionized water for 30 min. nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> films were prepared by doctor blading the prepared TiO<sub>2</sub> paste using “magic tape” (3M) as spacers. Following initial drying in the air for 20 min, the films were sintered at 723 K for 30 min. The resulting films were placed into a tubular furnace and calcined at a range of different temper- atures (723-873 K) for 30 min under an ammonia gas flow (BOC, flow rate 60 mL/min,) and then cooled to 673 K under ammonia prior to being cooled to room temperature under a nitrogen gas flow. The crystal structure of the sample was deter- mined by X-ray diffraction (JEOL JDX-3500 Tokyo, Japan). UV-visible diffuse reflectance spectra of the samples were measured with a Shimadzu UV-1601. The morphology of the samples was observed with a Field Emission Scanning Electron Microscope (FE-SEM) operated at 15 kV. For all studies under UV irradiation, the films were made by one layer of precursor paste, which produced a film of ∼4 μm thickness that was measured by a profilometer (Alpha-Step 200, Tencor Instru- ments). For oxygen production studies under visible light exci- tation, two layers of precursor paste were used to produce ∼8 μm films. The glass slides were cut into 1.5 2.7 cm<sup>2</sup> pieces with a TiO<sub>2</sub> coated area of 1 1.5 cm<sup>2</sup> in the slide center. Prior to each measurement, all samples were pretreated at 673 K for a further 30 min in air to remove any absorbed organic substances.

Time-Resolved Absorption Spectroscopy. Time-resolved absorption spectroscopy (TAS) was utilized to directly observe the dynamics of electrons and holes in doped nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> films. Details of the TAS system employed in this work have been pre- viously reported and we include only a brief description here.<sup>24</sup> The third harmonic of a Nd:YAG laser (Continuum, Surelite I-10) was employed as the UV excitation source (355 nm, ∼200 μJ/cm<sup>2</sup> /pulse, typically 1 Hz repetition rate and 6 ns pulse width). An OPO (Surelite OPO PLUS) pumped by the third harmonic of a Nd:YAG laser was utilized as the visible light exci- tation source (420 nm, ∼318 μJ/cm<sup>2</sup>/pulse, 1 Hz repetition rate). A 75 W Xe lamp (Hamamatsu) provided the probe light. A liquid light guide was utilized to transmit the laser pulse to the sample. The wavelength of the probe light was controlled by monochromators before and after the sample. The light intensity was measured using a Si-PIN photodiode (Hamamatsu), and these data were transferred for computer analysis in custom built software (LabVIEW, National Instruments). All TAS traces were obtained following averaging of 200-500 pulses.

Pt and 2 mM aqueous AgNO <sub>3</sub> solutions were employed (sepa- rately) to scavenge photoelectrons while ethanol was used to scavenge photoholes. Pt nanoparticles were coated onto the TiO<sub>2</sub> films in the manner previously reported.<sup>5</sup> A detailed discus- sion on the choice of chemical scavengers employed can be found in the Supporting Information. TAS studies were carried out in sealed quartz cuvettes and solutions and samples were thor- oughly degassed with argon prior to use.

## ’ RESULTS

The nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> films were synthesized by thermal treatment of the prepared nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> films under an ammonia flow at a range of temperatures (723-823 K). The doped samples reveal a visible absorption that appears as a shoulder in the UV-vis spectrum extending to 500 nm and the strongest visible absorp- tion can be seen in the sample prepared at 823 K. The increased level of doping at higher temperatures is in good agreement with many previous publications.<sup>9,20</sup> Above 823 K, glass substrate deformation occurs, therefore samples prepared at 823 K are used in all further studies (Figure S1, of the Supporting In- formation). All of the doped samples show identical XRD pat- terns as the precursor anatase nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> films and the morphol- ogies of the nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> and nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> films observed by FE-SEM are similar with an average particle size of 15-20 nm, (Figure S7, of the Supporting Information).

Oxygen Evolution under Visible and UV Light. In the absence of a suitable electron scavenger or an applied bias, water oxidation on nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> does not occur due to rapid electron-hole recombination.<sup>5,23,24</sup> Therefore, in order to assess the activity of the nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> and nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> samples toward photochemical water oxidation we have examined the activity of our samples in the presence of Ag<sup>þ</sup> ions, a widely used, efficient, electron scavenger.<sup>28-32</sup> Quantitative measurement for O <sub>2</sub> evolution using a controlled photon flux emitted by a pulsed 355 nm laser system (1 Hz, 200 μJ cm<sup>-2</sup>) have been carried out, Figure 1a. The dissolved O<sub>2</sub> concentration is seen to increase for both the doped and undoped samples under UV laser illumination. When

the laser light is turned off, the oxygen concentration stabilizes, demonstrating that the rise in dissolved oxygen concentration is due to photochemical water oxidation and not from leaks from the surrounding atmosphere. Despite having a slightly lower optical absorption at 355 nm (Figure S2, of the Supporting Infor- mation), nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> has a much higher rate of oxygen production and this is reflected in it is higher quantum yield (QY) for O<sub>2</sub> production than nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> (nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> = 16%, nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> = 5.5%), which was calculated using the procedure described in a previous paper.<sup>5</sup> In an attempt to enhance the low quantum yield for oxygen production over nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub>, we have examined the effect of the addition of Pt to the surface of the nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub>. Pt is known to act as an electron sink lowering electron-hole recombination and its addition results in a ∼80% increase in O<sub>2</sub> production under UV irradiation when compared to the unmodified nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> , Figure 1a. The potential mechanistic implications of this significant enhancement are discussed in the latter sections of this work.

The potential evolution of O<sub>2</sub> over N-doped TiO<sub>2</sub> was also measured under visible light irradiation (Xe lamp λ > 395 nm) in 2 mM AgNO<sub>3</sub> aqueous solution, Figure 1b. To improve the possibility of observing any visible light driven water oxidation, we prepared films of 8 μm thick nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> for this measure- ment. The lack of an intrinsic optical absorption in the visible region means that no noticeable O<sub>2</sub> consumption or production occurs over an 8 μm nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> film (S3, of the Supporting Infor- mation). Irradiation of the nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> film in AgNO <sub>3</sub> solution with visible light actually leads to a lowering of the dissolved oxygen concentration, which is assigned to the reduction of oxygen by photoelectrons, a previously observed phenomena over TiO<sub>2</sub> photocatalysts under UV illumination.<sup>5</sup> The addition of Pt to the nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> surface is seen to prevent the previously observed decrease in oxygen concentration; however, no clear increase in dissolved oxygen was detected. Figure 1b clearly de- monstrates that although the nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> film can absorb visible light to create active charge carriers, net production of oxygen through water oxidation is not observed.

A superposition of the TAS spectrum of the photoholes on UV excited nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> and of visible excited nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> approximately reproduces the photohole spectra observed following the UV excitation of nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> . However, the limited resolution of this data prevents definitive assignment. A more definitive assign- ment of the TAS spectrum in Figure 2(c) to the formation of holes at trap states introduced by nitrogen doping is achieved in the following section by analysis of the recombination kinetics of the photoholes on nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> .

This is in agreement with previous research on nitrogen doped nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> powders,<sup>26</sup> which reported a red shifting in the broad hole absorption spectrum upon doping, although a distinct second hole population was not reported. In the presence of eth- anol, a hole scavenger, no clear TAS signals were observed between 550 and 800 nm on nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> following visible light excitation, confirming our assignment of the photohole spec- trum. Scavenging of the photoholes would be expected to lead to long-lived photoelectron signals. Visible excitation of N-nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> generates a much lower concentration of charge carriers than UV excitation due to the lower absorption of the sample at visible wavelengths; therefore, it is possible that we are unable to detect the potentially weak absorption spectrum of the visible light generated photoelectrons. The lack of a TAS signal for the visible light generated phototelectrons is discussed fully in the following sections.

Dynamics of Electron-hole Recombination. When experi- ments are carried out under argon in the absence of any chemical scavengers, the transient absorption decays on both doped and undoped TiO<sub>2</sub> are attributed to electron-hole recombination. The decay of the signal at 465 nm on nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> following UV excitation (355 nm, 200 μJ cm<sup>-2</sup>) is clearly slower (t<sub>50%</sub> = 220 μs)<sup>35</sup> than that on undoped nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> (t<sub>50%</sub> = 40 μs), Figure 3.

This difference in the hole lifetime is further investigated by comparing the TAS decay traces at wavelengths where only the photoholes absorb (420-460 nm) to 800 nm, a wavelength where trapped photoelectrons absorb, Figure 4. It should be noted that in the absence of chemical scavengers, the TAS signal at 800 nm may have a contribution from the tail of the photohole absorption; however, by measuring the differences between these two spectral regions we can evaluate the differences in the rates of decays of photoelectrons and photoholes. On nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> , the tran- sient absorption signal decay is independent of probe wave- length, indicating that the charge carrier populations probed are directly recombining with each other. We also observe an inde- pendence of the rate of decay of the TAS traces on the wave- length studied for nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> samples that have been thermally treated to 823 K in the absence of ammonia (Figure S6, of the Supporting Information). On nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> , the TAS decay at shor- ter wavelengths (420-460 nm), is slower than that measured at 800 nm, and the decay rate in this region varies with the wave- length probed, Figure 4.

## ’ DISCUSSION

Since the initial reports of visible light photocatalytic activity for nitrogen-doped TiO<sub>2</sub>, there has been considerable interest in the nature of the effect of the dopant on the band structure of TiO<sub>2</sub> and the origin of the low efficiency. The visible light absorption has been proposed to be due to either a localized N 2p level at higher energy to the TiO<sub>2</sub> VB (O 2p)<sup>8,9,17</sup> or to be from the formation a delocalized O2pþN2p level.<sup>6</sup> Our TAS results support the conclusion that there are two distinct states available for the photoholes (Scheme 1), with absorption max- ima at 460 and 550 nm, in the nitrogen-doped TiO<sub>2</sub> film. A broadening of the hole transient absorption spectrum of nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> has been previously reported by Tachikawa et al.,<sup>26</sup> but this is the first time that the kinetics of the visible light generated holes have been directly monitored.

Experiments in the presence of a sacrificial electron acceptor (Ag<sup>þ</sup>) have demonstrated that the QY for O<sub>2</sub> production is sig- nificantly lower on nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> than nc-TiO <sub>2</sub> under UV illumina- tion. Under visible illumination O<sub>2</sub> is not produced over nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> despite the formation of electron-hole pairs. TAS experiments have demonstrated that the photohole population that is trapped at nitrogen doping induced sites (absorption maximum at 550 nm) do not oxidize water in the presence of Ag<sup>þ</sup>. This gives rise to the

decreased QY for O<sub>2</sub> production as a significant population of photoholes centered at 550 nm are formed following excitation of UV light. Three main reasons for the lack of activity of these photoholes toward water oxidation might be envisaged: (i) deep trapping in the bulk material; (ii) a reduced oxidative power leading to a lack of thermodynamic driving force; and (iii) kinetic com- petition preventing a slow water oxidation process from occurring, and these will be discussed in detail below.

In order to investigate if the visible light generated holes are able to oxidize surface species, we have examined the effect of ethanol on the photohole kinetics. It is known that ethanol oxi- dation occurs very rapidly (<1 ns) on nc-TiO<sub>2</sub>, making it an ideal process to monitor the surface availability of the visible light photoholes.<sup>22</sup> Figure 5 clearly shows that the addition of ethanol to the sample quenches the TAS signal of the visible light gene- rated holes, therefore these holes that are trapped at the nitrogen- induced states are able to reach the nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> surface within the time resolution of the experiment (<10 μs). It has been pre- viously reported that the photoholes produced by visible light are not active for alcohol oxidation.<sup>8,26</sup> However, these reports were utilizing different alcohols in the presence of acetonitrile, which can have significant effects on the rate of photocatalytic oxi- dation;<sup>22</sup> additionally, these studies did not directly probe the dynamics of the photohole population. Instead several alternative mechanisms for the observed alcohol degradation under visible light have been proposed, which include degradation by photo- reduced oxygen (O<sub>2</sub><sup>-</sup>) and reaction between alcohols and the proposed intermediates of the water oxidation mechanism.<sup>8,26</sup> However, the TAS experiments outlined above strongly suggest that in the materials we have examined, the oxidation of ethanol by the trapped holes with an absorption maximum of 550 nm can occur.

Our TAS experiments instead show that the lack of water oxidation by visible light generated holes on nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> is either due to a lack of thermodynamic driving force for water oxidation or kinetic competition for the photohole reaction, or due to a combination of both factors. As the visible light generated photo- holes on nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> would be expected to be of lower energy than those generated by UV excitation of nc-TiO<sub>2</sub>, it is possible that these visible light holes no longer have sufficient oxidative power to react directly with water or surface hydroxyls. In our TAS and O<sub>2</sub> yield experiments, we have not observed any evidence for the reaction between visible light generated photo- holes on nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> and water. However, the experiments de- scribed here alone do not allow us to rule out the possibility that

there is significant thermodynamic driving force and previous IPCE measurements<sup>8</sup> have suggested that the oxidation of water under visible light by nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> may be thermodynamically possible. In a previous study, we have demonstrated that the hole reaction with water is very slow at neutral pH’s (∼0.3 s) on UV excited nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> in the presence of Ag<sup>þ</sup>.<sup>5</sup> The lifetime of the photoholes localized at the nitrogen doping levels is significantly shorter (∼ 0.04 s) than the lifetime of UV driven water oxidation on nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> , Figure 6c. As the visible light generated holes on nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> are expected to have a lower thermodynamic driving force for water oxidation than the UV generated holes on nc-TiO<sub>2</sub>, it seems unlikely that the rate of water oxidation on the doped material would be significantly (∼8-10 times) faster. Therefore, an alternative decay mechanism for the visible light generated photoholes appears to be occurring, which is most likely electron-hole recombination, even in the presence of an electron scavenger.

It is therefore highly desirable to directly observe the visible light generated photoelectrons on nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> . Following initial thermal relaxation (<100 fs), deep trapping of photoelectrons on nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> has been reported to occur rapidly (200-500 ps).<sup>36</sup> These deep-trapped electrons recombine very slowly with a power law dependence and the electron lifetimes extend into the milliseconds time scales, and it has been shown that the recombination is “trap-limited”, indicating that the time scale of detrapping is slow, even compared to the time scales of these measurements.<sup>5,37</sup> Therefore, it is feasible that two distinct deeply trapped photoelectron populations can exist on the time scale of these TAS measurements. In the TAS experiments des- cribed above, no distinct photoelectron signal could be observed following the visible excitation of the nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub>, even when the photoholes were efficiently scavenged in the presence of ethanol. It is possible that the low concentration of visible light photo- electrons prevents their observation. Alternatively, it is known that the photoelectron absorption spectrum on nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> inc- reases with wavelength and we cannot rule out that this species absorbs at wavelengths greater than we are able to monitor with our current apparatus. The formation of new photoelectron traps on TiO<sub>2</sub> following carbon doping has been previously reported, which would lead to the generation of a second photoelectron population with potentially different spectroscopic features.<sup>38</sup> We should also consider that the photoelectrons produced following visible excitation of nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> may be scavenged by surface species (e.g., O<sub>2</sub> ) on a submicrosecond time scale preven- ting direct observation by TAS. However, the TAS results recorded under argon, which show that the hole signal decays even in the absence of any permanent chemical scavengers, suggest that any scavenging must be reversible, allowing for indirect electron-hole recombination to occur.

We have attempted to enhance the efficiency of water oxidation on nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> by depositing Pt on the photocatalyst surface. The deposition of Pt was carried out to increase the efficiency of electron scavenging by Ag<sup>þ</sup>, which in turn would be expected to lead to higher O<sub>2</sub> yields by preventing charge carrier recombination. Under visible illumination, the addition of Pt did prevent the reduction of oxygen in solution by pho- toelectrons, but no clear rise in the dissolved oxygen concen- tration was measured. Under UV light, this leads to an 80% increase in QY, which indicates that enhancing electron scavenging can significantly improve the QY for O<sub>2</sub> production over these materials.

In summary, the lack of photocatalytic water oxidation under visible light and smaller efficiency for O<sub>2</sub> production over nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> under UV light is not due to surface availability of visible light generated photoholes. The lack of activity is due to the rapid decay of the visible light generated photoholes, which occurs on a much faster time scale than that required for water oxidation on the related material, nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> . The decay mechanism of the visible light generated photoholes on nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> can be assigned to either direct or indirect recombination with photoelectrons even in the presence of an electron scavenger.

## ’ CONCLUSIONS

This mechanistic research on the widely used visible light-driven photocatalyst nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> has identified two distinct photohole populations on nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> . For the first time, it has been found that the photohole population that is trapped at the nitrogen doping induced states (peaked at 550 nm) is active for ethanol oxidation. The low efficiency of nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> toward photo-oxidation reactions is not due to the deep trapping of photoholes in the bulk material. Instead, the lack of water oxida- tion is assigned to be due to electron-hole recombination be- tween the charge carriers trapped at doping-induced states and possibly due to the reduced oxidative power of the photoholes generated under visible light.

This work confirms that UV driven O<sub>2</sub> production from water over nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> is a slow process, requiring photohole lifetimes of ∼0.4 s, consistent with findings on undoped nc-TiO<sub>2</sub> films. Rapid electron-hole recombination occurs even in the presence of an efficient electron scavenger suggests that the visible light generated photoelectrons are not able to efficiently reduce the Ag<sup>þ</sup> ions. Therefore, in order to achieve an improvement in effi- ciency, enhancing the separation of the photoelectrons from the photoholes is expected to be essential on doped materials. Pt modification of nc-N-TiO<sub>2</sub> has been observed to dramatically enhance O<sub>2</sub> production under UV light in the presence of elec- tron scavenger. It is believed that these research results can provide useful information for O<sub>2</sub> production on the other anion- doped photocatalysts.
