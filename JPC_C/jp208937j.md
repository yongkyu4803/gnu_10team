# TiO<sub>2</sub> Surface Treatment Effects by Mg<sup>2+</sup>, Ba<sup>2+</sup>, and Al<sup>3+</sup> on Sb<sub>2</sub>S<sub>3</sub> Extremely Thin Absorber Solar Cells

ABSTRACT: Sb<sub>2</sub>S<sub>3</sub>-based extremely thin absorber (ETA) solar cells of structure, \<F- doped tin oxide (FTO)/TiO<sub>2</sub> (compact)/porous TiO <sub>2</sub>/Sb <sub>2</sub>S<sub>3</sub>/CuSCN/Au>, was fabricated with porous TiO<sub>2</sub> surface treating by Mg<sup>2+</sup>, Ba<sup>2+</sup>, and Al<sup>3+</sup> to be full-inorganic printed solar cells. The fabrication parameters of the ETA structure were optimized and the photovoltaic characteristics were improved significantly when double treatment in Mg<sup>2+</sup> and Ba<sup>2+</sup> were introduced. Best cells obtained using double treatment in Mg<sup>2+</sup> and Ba<sup>2+</sup> attained a short-circuit photocurrent of 13.4 mA/cm<sup>2</sup>, a open-circuit voltage of 0.584 V, a fill factor of 0.525, and a conversion efficiency 4.10% at 1 sun irradiation.

## 2. EXPERIMENTAL DETAILS

Ethanol (Kanto Chemical Co., Inc.), acetone (Kanto Chemical Co., Inc.), SbCl <sub>3</sub> (Kishida Chemical Co., Ltd.), Na<sub>2</sub> S<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> (Sigma-Aldrich), CuSCN (Mitsuwa Chemicals Co., Ltd.), AlCl <sub>3</sub> (Kanto Chemical Co., Inc.), Ba(NO<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub> (Kanto Chemical Co., Inc.), MgCl <sub>2</sub> (Kanto Chemical Co., Inc.), screen-printing TiO <sub>2</sub> pastes (d = 20 nm, PST-18NR, JGC-CCIC; d = 400 nm, PST-400C, JGC-CCIC), and TiO <sub>2</sub> particles (d = 90 nm, F-1, Showa Denko; d = 200 nm, ST-41, Ishihara Sangyo) were used as received. TiO <sub>2</sub> particles (d = 32 nm, calculated by BET) were synthesized by the hydrothermal crystallization method.<sup>27</sup> The TiO <sub>2</sub> nanoparticle was converted in screen-printing paste for nanocrystalline-TiO <sub>2</sub> electrode.<sup>28</sup> The final screen-printing pastes correspond to 18 wt % TiO <sub>2</sub>, 9 wt % ethyl cellulose, and 73 wt % α-terpineol.

A TiO <sub>2</sub> compact layer with thickness around 100 nm was deposited by the spray pyrolysis deposition (SPD) method onto a F-doped tin oxide transparent conducting glass substrate (FTO, Nippon Sheet Glass, Japan).<sup>29</sup> The deposition temper- ature of TiO <sub>2</sub> compact layer was 450 °C. Nanocrystalline-TiO<sub>2</sub> electrodes were coated using screen-printing method and subsequently sintered at 325 °C for 5 min, at 375 °C for 5 min, at 450 °C for 15 min, and finally at 500 °C for 15 min;<sup>28</sup> the film thickness was controlled to be about 2.3−2.5 μm.

The conversion efficiency of solar cells was improved after TiO <sub>2</sub> surface treatment. In the present work, we used three kinds of materials for TiO <sub>2</sub> surface treatment: BaTiO<sub>3</sub>, MgO, and Al <sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>. To coat BaTiO<sub>3</sub>, MgO, and Al<sub>2</sub>O <sub>3</sub>, nanocrystalline-TiO <sub>2</sub> electrodes were soaked in Ba(NO<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub> (saturated),<sup>30</sup> MgCl<sub>2</sub> (0.15 M),<sup>31−33</sup> and AlCl<sub>3</sub> (0.15 M) solution in ion-exchange water at room temperature, rinsed with ion-exchange water, and sintered at 550 °C for 30 min, respectively. Each soaking time has been optimized using conversion efficiency of resulting solar cells. All of the coating procedures were carried out in ambient air.

Sb<sub>2</sub> S<sub>3</sub> was then deposited on the nanocrystalline-TiO<sub>2</sub> electrodes with treatment by chemical bath deposition (CBD) method from a solution of SbCl<sub>3</sub> and Na<sub>2</sub>S<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>.<sup>34</sup> The as-deposited orange films of amorphous Sb<sub>2</sub>S<sub>3</sub> were annealed under N<sub>2</sub> at 320 °C for 30 min to give dark-brown crystalline stibnite. The samples were removed from the oven immediately after annealing and were allowed to cool in air.

The sample was then dipped in the 0.5 M aqueous KSCN solution for 5 min in place of LiSCN.<sup>14</sup> The excess solution was wicked off and dried at 80 °C. In this work, a solution of 0.05 M CuSCN in di-n-propyl sulfide was spread on the Sb<sub>2</sub>S<sub>3</sub>-deposited nanocrystalline-TiO<sub>2</sub> electrode while keeping it at 80 °C and by moving the glass rod forward and backward several times. Figure 1 shows deposition method of CuSCN.<sup>5</sup> Finally, Au back contact electrodes were deposited by vacuum evaporation.

The thickness of porous TiO <sub>2</sub> and CuSCN was viewed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM; JSM-6510, JEOL, Japan). The distribution of CuSCN in porous Sb<sub>2</sub>S<sub>3</sub> /TiO<sub>2</sub> was confirmed by an electron probe microanalyzer (EPMA; JXA8500-F). The coverage on nanocrystalline TiO<sub>2</sub> and crystallinity of Sb<sub>2</sub>S<sub>3</sub> were determined by high resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM; JEM 2100F). Absorption spectra were measured using an ultraviolet−visible spectroscopy (Lambda 750 UV/vis spectrometer, Perkin-

Photovoltaic measurements employed an AM 1.5 solar simulator equipped with a xenon lamp (YSS-100A, Yamashita Denso, Japan). In order to improve the photovoltaic effects, the possibility of combination of the treatments (to be a “double treatment”) was considered from the cell parameters (Table 1). The short-circuit current density was improved with Mg<sup>2+</sup>. FF was improved with Ba<sup>2+</sup>. The open-circuit voltage was improved with both of Al<sup>3+</sup> and Ba<sup>2+</sup>. However, the starting material for Al<sup>3+</sup> treatment, AlCl<sub>3</sub>, was quite unstable, resulting in the inconstant results. Excluding Al<sup>3+</sup>, therefore, Mg<sup>2+</sup> and Ba<sup>2+</sup> were chosen for the materials of double treatment. To fabricate a double coating, first nanocrystalline-TiO <sub>2</sub> electrodes were soaked in Ba(NO<sub>3</sub> )<sub>2</sub> (saturated) solution (ion-exchanged water as solvent) at room temperature for 30 s, then rinsed with ion-exchanged water, and sintered at 550 °C for 30 min. Subsequently, nanocrystalline-TiO<sub>2</sub> electrodes with Ba<sup>2+</sup> coat- ing were soaked in MgCl <sub>2</sub> (0.15M) solution (ion-exchanged water as solvent) at room temperature for 2.5−20 min, then rinsed with ion-exchanged water, and sintered at 550 °C for 30 min. All of the coating procedures were carried out in ambient air.

4. CONCLUSIONS

The fabrication methods of Sb<sub>2</sub> S<sub>3</sub> -based ETA nanocrystalline-TiO<sub>2</sub> solar cells were optimized, and the photovoltaic effects using various treatment materials including Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>, MgO, and BaTiO<sub>3</sub> as well as double treatment (MgO/BaTiO<sub>3</sub>) for TiO<sub>2</sub> surface were characterized. Among these treatment materials, BaTiO<sub>3</sub> showed the best cell parameters with J<sub>sc</sub> = 12.20 mA/cm<sup>2</sup>, V<sub>oc</sub> = 0.53 V, FF = 0.54, and η = 3.5%. The conversion efficiency was increased as double treatment was applied. The best efficiency for BaTiO <sub>3</sub>/MgO double treatment was obtained to be 4.1% at 1 sun. So far, the best published efficiency of Sb<sub>2</sub>S<sub>3</sub> ETA solar cells was 6.2%,<sup>40</sup> which utilized organic polymer as the hole conductor. Although the best efficiency of this report was just 4.1%, this work is about “full-inorganic” Sb<sub>2</sub>S<sub>3</sub>-based ETA solar cells using CuSCN as p-type semiconductor, which is one of the best efficiency for “full-inorganic” Sb<sub>2</sub>S<sub>3</sub>-based ETA nanocrystalline-TiO<sub>2</sub> solar cells up to this time. EPMA mapping color showed that the distribution of CuSCN in porous Sb<sub>2</sub>S<sub>3</sub>/TiO<sub>2</sub> is quite uniform. HRTEM indicated a thin layer (0.5−2 nm) of Sb <sub>2</sub>S<sub>3</sub> coated on porous TiO<sub>2</sub> and the low crystallinity of Sb<sub>2</sub>S<sub>3</sub>. The electrical functions of surface treatment have been studied and will be discussed in our forthcoming paper. At this stage we are unable provide a concrete reason as to why the double barrier layers are superior. Possibly a graded barrier differentiates forward and backward electron transfer, more effectively suppressing recombination. Larramona et al. published 0.65 V open-circuit photovoltage,<sup>41</sup> but the experimental details were not clear because it was just the conference A4 one-page paper. After the publication from them, the efficiencies of “full-inorganic” Sb<sub>2</sub>S<sub>3</sub> ETA solar cells will be significantly improved with our showing Mg<sup>2+</sup>/Ba<sup>2+</sup> double treatment effects.
