# Fabrication Pathways of p−n Cu<sub>2</sub>O Homojunction Films by Electrochemical Deposition Processing

## Leo Chau-Kuang Liau,\* Yu-Chieh Lin, and Yong-Jie Peng

Department of Chemical Engineering and Materials Science, Yuan Ze University, Chung-Li 32003, Taiwan

ABSTRACT: Fabrication of p−n Cu<sub>2</sub>O homojunction films was demonstrated by multiple steps of electrochemical deposition (ECD) processing with the adjustment of the deposition periods. The deposited Cu<sub>2</sub>O films, p-type and n-type Cu<sub>2</sub>O films, using ECD processing were, respectively, characterized using several analytical tests. Fabrication strategies of p−n Cu <sub>2</sub>O homojunction films were proposed using the sequential deposition steps of p-Cu<sub>2</sub>O and n-Cu<sub>2</sub>O films. Results showed that the prepared Cu<sub>2</sub> O films were influenced by the ECD conditions and displayed different semiconductor, crystal, and morphological properties. A p-type Cu<sub>2</sub>O (p-Cu<sub>2</sub>O) film can be transformed into a n-type Cu<sub>2</sub>O (n- Cu <sub>2</sub>O) film by the control of the ECD deposition periods. Different positions of p- and n-Cu <sub>2</sub>O layers located in the p−n homojunction devices can be produced by the adjustment of the deposition periods in the ECD processing steps. The interaction of n-Cu <sub>2</sub>O grown on a p-Cu <sub>2</sub>O film during the ECD processing was revealed to interpret the formation of the p−n homojunction devices. The design and fabrication of the p−n homojunction films were discussed by the fabrication strategy using the multiple ECD processing steps.

## 2. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

2.1. Deposition of Cu<sub>2</sub>O Films. The deposition of Cu<sub>2</sub>O films used an electrochemical analyzer type 6081 C with a three-electrode provided by CH Instruments in the ECD system. The deposited Cu<sub>2</sub>O films were carried out using indium doped tin oxide (ITO) as the working electrode. ITO glass is a commercial product and was purchased from AimCore Technology Co., Taiwan. The composition of the ITO film is 90% indium oxide and 10% tin oxide, and the sheet resistance is 7 Ω/square. The counter electrode consisted of platinum, and the reference electrode was an Ag/AgCl electrode in 3 M KCl solution. The potential value of the reference Ag/AgCl vs NHE is 0.198 V.

Two types of semiconductor films, p- and n-Cu<sub>2</sub>O, were produced using the ECD system and previously reported by McShane and Choi.<sup>4</sup> The deposition of p-Cu<sub>2</sub>O films was prepared by the ECD system using a solution, mixed of copper(II) acetate (Cu(OOCCH <sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>·H<sub>2</sub>O) and lactic acid. The concentration of Cu(OOCCH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub> is 0.02 M for all the fabrication processes in the work. The ECD system of p-Cu <sub>2</sub>O preparation was setup to pH = 11 by the adjustment of NaOH (2 M) and maintained at 60 °C. The deposition potential was constantly kept at −0.4 V. For the n-Cu<sub>2</sub>O deposition, copper(II) acetate was mixed with acetic acid under the control of pH = 4.9 for n-Cu <sub>2</sub>O preparation. The deposition of n-Cu<sub>2</sub>O films was prepared using a constant potential of +0.02 V. The temperature of the deposition bath was maintained at 70 °C for the production of n-Cu<sub>2</sub>O films. All the chemicals were obtained from J. T. Baker Corp.

The possible deposition mechanism of this case is the n-Cu <sub>2</sub>O grown on top of the p-Cu<sub>2</sub>O film instead of inside the film for the last ECD processing step. The electrochemical reaction of n-Cu<sub>2</sub>O formation occurs in the region between the p-Cu <sub>2</sub>O film surface and the liquid phase. This is because the preparation of the p-Cu<sub>2</sub>O film was carried out for two consecutive ECD processings of p-Cu<sub>2</sub>O. The fabricated p-Cu <sub>2</sub>O film was thicker and denser to resist the transportation of the Cu ions from the liquid to the solid film. The formation of the n-Cu<sub>2</sub>O growth inside the p-Cu<sub>2</sub>O film is difficult to perform due to the high diffusion resistance. Therefore, the n-Cu <sub>2</sub>O layer is formed on top of the p-Cu<sub>2</sub>O film due to the diffusion effect.

From the results of the above cases, the ECD pathways of p−n Cu <sub>2</sub>O homojunction films are greatly affected by the processing conditions, such as deposition steps and periods. If the p-Cu <sub>2</sub>O film was produced in the first ECD step, the deposition period of the n-type Cu<sub>2</sub>O processing significantly influenced the formation of p−n Cu<sub>2</sub>O homojunction films. For longer deposition period of the n-type Cu<sub>2</sub> O deposition step, the p-Cu<sub>2</sub>O film can be completely transformed to n-Cu<sub>2</sub>O semiconductor discussed in Case (1). However, p−n Cu<sub>2</sub>O homojunction films cannot be made using this fabrication approach. A p−n Cu<sub>2</sub>O homojunction film can be produced if shorter deposition period of the n-type Cu<sub>2</sub>O processing step is adopted as explained in Case (3). The n-Cu <sub>2</sub>O layer is formed in the bottom of the p-Cu<sub>2</sub>O layer using this fabrication strategy. Moreover, p−n Cu<sub>2</sub>O homojunction devices can also be produced by two consecutive ECD processings to prepare a thicker p-Cu<sub>2</sub>O film, following by the ECD processing of the n-Cu <sub>2</sub>O grown on top of the p-Cu<sub>2</sub>O film.
