1. INTRODUCTION

Hematite (α-Fe<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>) has emerged as a promising photocatalyst

for solar water splitting due to its favorable optical band gap (∼2.2 eV), excellent chemical stability in a wide pH range (pH

> 3), earth abundance, and low cost.<sup>1−10</sup> The band gap gives a theoretical upper limit of solar-to-hydrogen efficiency of 16.8%

and a photocurrent of 12.6 mA cm<sup>−2</sup> under the standard AM 1.5G 1 sun illumination.<sup>4</sup> Therefore, enormous efforts have been focused on improving the performance of α-Fe<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> photoanodes in photoelectrochemical (PEC) water splitting

by employing various strategies, such as nanostructure engineering via different synthesis methods, doping, or forming

a heterojunction.<sup>11</sup> The preparation of α-Fe<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> nanowires,<sup>6,12</sup> nanotubes,<sup>13</sup> nanopetals,<sup>11</sup> nanonets,<sup>14</sup> and dendritic nano- cauliflowers<sup>15</sup> are examples of successful attempts that achieved improvement in the light harvesting and charge carrier separation to enhance the PEC performance. Several physical

and chemical methods have been developed to obtain such nanostructures of α-Fe<sub>2</sub>O <sub>3</sub>. Among these, pulse reverse electrodeposition (PRED)<sup>11</sup> has been relatively less employed despite having several potential advantages as it serves a potential strategy to tailor both the chemical composition and

the structure of materials including metallic films and alloys. It perturbs the electrocrystallization process to change the

© 2015 American Chemical Society	5281

morphology, texture, and properties of the electrodeposits. In PRED, a periodic waveform consisting of a cathodic pulse and anodic pulse is applied to deposit the desired material. The mechanism of PRED is simply based on the adsorption or desorption phenomena at the electrode−electrolyte interface. The nucleation and growth of the deposited material depends on the adsorption−desorption rate of the species in the electrolyte, controlled by PRED. Although a simple method, PRED requires careful attention for optimization of the parameters such as cathodic current density, anodic current density, duty cycle, pulse period, and the deposition time. It can be employed to obtain nanostructures with greater control over the grain size and hardness through variation of such process parameters. The schematic of potential pulse reverse waveform for electrodeposition of iron oxide is shown in Scheme 1.

The use of PRED for synthesizing α-Fe<sub>2</sub>O <sub>3</sub> electrodes is rather a new approach, and the influence of the PRED process parameters for the performance improvement has not yet been investigated. ## 2. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

2.1. Materials Synthesis. Pulse reverse electrodeposition was carried out in a two-electrode configuration cell. Trans- parent conducting glass (fluorine doped tin oxide, FTO, 10−15 Ω cm<sup>−1</sup>) substrate was used as a working electrode, and convoluted platinum wire as a counter electrode. The distance between the two electrodes was ∼1 cm. Prior to deposition of iron oxide films, FTO substrates (1 cm × 2.5 cm) were successively cleaned by ultrasonic degreasing in acetone, ethanol, and deionized water (each for 10 min) then dried using a nitrogen stream. The electrolyte consisted of 6 g of ferrous sulfate, 0.15 g of ascorbic acid, 0.05 g of amidosulfonic acid, and 1.5 g of boric acid in 0.1 L of deionized water (pH = 5.71). The pH of the resulting electrolyte solution was 2.6. The chemicals were purchased from Alfa Aesar and Kanto Chemicals and were used without further treatment. Potential control was provided by means of a function generator. The PRED of Fe<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> films was carried out by keeping the amplitude (peak-to-peak voltage) of the square-wave pulse fixed at 10 V (−6/+4 V). The other deposition parameters such as duty cycle (20%), pulse period (frequency), and the total deposition time were varied systematically. At least three electrodes of each condition were prepared. As-grown films were dark grayish in

color and reflected the light. Films were rinsed several times in deionized water and dried using nitrogen stream. The two-step annealing method was followed to obtain highly photoactive photoanodes.<sup>16</sup> The as-grown films were heated to 550 °C at the rate of 5 °C min<sup>−1</sup> and baked there for 4 h. Finally, the films were annealed at 800 °C briefly for 15 min and subsequently transferred to a forced-convection oven preheated at 100 °C for 10 min to avoid breaking of the electrodes and the supporting ceramic crucibles. The sintered films appeared reddish or dark brown in color, depending on the film thickness.

The photo- activity of the synthesized α-Fe<sub>2</sub> O<sub>3</sub> photoanodes was measured in a PEC cell consisting of a three-arm glass compartment with a circular quartz window for light illumination. The PEC cell comprised Fe<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>/FTO photoanode as the working electrode, Pt wire as the counter electrode, Ag/AgCl (saturated with KCl) as the reference electrode, and 1 M NaOH as an electrolyte. All potentials were measured relative to the Ag/AgCl electrode and were converted to the reversible hydrogen electrode (RHE) scale using the Nernst eq 1.<sup>15</sup>

The α-Fe<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> has a short hole diffusion length (2−4 nm) and a light penetration depth (1/α) of around 118 nm at the wavelength of 550 nm. To ensure optimum absorption (>90%) of the incident light, the film thickness must be greater than 2.3 times the value of 1/α.<sup>18</sup> The films should have an enough thickness that would offer sufficient amounts of light absorption, but not too thick that would hold electrons too long in the film and expose them to charge recombination. Hence, the film thickness effect on photocurrent response was studied by varying the PRED time. The pulse period (10 ms), the duty cycle (20%), and the pulse voltage (−6/+4 V) were kept constant during PRED time variation. The thinner films of α-Fe<sub>2</sub>O <sub>3</sub> (with PRED time <30 s) were also prepared for comparison since most literature studies were focused on thinner hematite (20−60 nm) photoanodes for water splitting applications.<sup>19−25</sup> Figure S2 shows the J−V curves of thinner α-Fe <sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> films prepared by PRED for the deposition times of 5, 10, and 20 s. The shift of onset potential for the water oxidation reaction results from the energy that is absorbed in semiconductor electrodes by photoexcitation to increase the concentration of interfacial holes in the valence band. The reason for cathodic shift in onset

potential could be attributed to surface passivation effect owing to the decrease in charge transfer resistance from surface states.<sup>29</sup> The onset potential of a pure Fe<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> photoanode can be lowered through passivating the surface states by means of aging the photoanodes in saturated NaCl solution.<sup>30</sup> However, the α-Fe<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> photoanodes in the present study were fabricated by PRED without inducing any oxygen evolution reaction catalyst or passivation layer. In this context, we believe that the synthesis conditions of PRED method help passivate the surface states. However, it necessitates further study to assert these findings.

3.2. Structural Studies by XRD and XAFS. Figure 3a shows the XRD patterns recorded in the diffraction angle (2θ) range of 20°−70° for the as-grown and annealed (at 550 °C) iron oxide films prepared by PRED for a deposition time of 45 s. The peaks marked by an asterisk in the figure are due to contribution from the FTO substrate (ICDD #00-077-0452). The as-grown film revealed an Fe peak (110) at a diffraction angle of 44.75°. This indicates that the as-grown films were metallic. After annealing at 550 °C for 4 h, the metallic peak disappeared, indicating a transformation to iron oxide. In the case of annealed electrode, the major Bragg diffraction peaks could be indexed as those of the hematite (α-Fe<sub>2</sub> O<sub>3</sub>) phase of iron oxide (ICDD #00-087-1165, space group: R−3c(167)). The presence of other nonhematite phases could be ruled out, as no diffraction peaks corresponding to maghemite (γ-Fe<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>), magnetite (Fe <sub>3</sub>O<sub>4</sub>), and wustite (FeO) were observed. Hence, the synthesis of phase-pure α-Fe<sub>2</sub>O <sub>3</sub> hematite structure was confirmed. The crystallite size was determined to be 54 nm from the high intensity α-Fe<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> peak (104) using the Scherer formula.

The electrodes prepared at different pulse periods were further annealed at 800 °C for 15 min after being sintered at 550 °C for 4 h. Figure 3b shows the XRD patterns of the Fe <sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> /FTO electrodes prepared by PRED for the different pulse periods. All annealed α-Fe<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> films were polycrystalline. No significant changes in the crystallite size (ca. ∼62 nm) were observed with variation in the pulse period from 0.01 to 10 ms; however, slight increments to ca. 64 and 72 nm were observed for the pulse periods of 100 and 1000 ms, respectively. Figure 3c shows the XRD patterns of Fe<sub>2</sub> O<sub>3</sub>/FTO electrodes prepared by PRED with different deposition times. The intensity of the α-Fe<sub>2</sub>O <sub>3</sub> peaks increased progressively with increase in the deposition time, which is an indication of improvement in the crystallinity as well as the film thickness. The crystallite size determined from the (104) peak showed an increasing trend, except for the thicker α-Fe <sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> film. The crystallite sizes were 62, 73, 74, 75, 79, and 58 nm for the deposition times of 30, 45, 60, 90, 120, and 300 s, respectively. The crystallite size determined for other diffraction peaks varied from 58 to 104 nm.

X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS) is a powerful site- specific local structural probe.<sup>31</sup> Both synchrotron-based XANES (X-ray absorption near-edge structure) and EXAFS (extended X-ray absorption fine structure) in XAS are well suited for determining local structures of amorphous and polycrystalline materials. Figure 4 shows the XANES and EXAFS spectra revealing the local structure of iron in the as-grown and annealed (550 °C + 800 °C) samples. Growth Mechanism. In general, the growth mechanism of materials by the electrodeposition method is quite a complex process due to the possible influence of many different parameters such as deposit−substrate interactions, structure and structural orientation of the substrate, current density, electrode potential, electrolyte composition, mass transport, temperature, pH, etc. Like any other electrochemical synthesis method, PRED controls the electrocrystallization via two fundamental electrokinetic phenomena: nucleation and growth of crystal grains. In this work, we studied the growth rate and morphology of prepared iron oxide films with respect to conditions of the pulse reverse electrodeposition synthesis and the high temperature annealing treatment of the electrodes. In the present study, PRED involved the application of cathodic and anodic pulses switched at −6 and +4 V over a short duration by varying the pulse polarization time from 0.001 to 1 ms. In PRED, uniform deposition is induced by partial dissolution of the deposited species during a slight anodic polarization pulse, E<sub>a</sub>, after interruption of the cathodic deposition pulse, E<sub>c</sub> (see Scheme 2). During the reduction stage of PRED, the Fe ions solvated in the electrolyte arrive at the surface of the electrode, promoting a diffusion motion, a stimulated migration, or even a convective motion depending on the experimental conditions. As soon as the cathodic pulse is applied to the electrode (FTO substrate), the reduction process starts. The reduced iron species are then adsorbed and migrate to an energetically favorable site at a brisk rate. In potential controlled experiments like in this study, the continuity of this mechanism over the entire electrode surface leads to the formation of a density of nuclei and the establishment of a macroscopic electrical current flowing through the electro- chemical cell. The current can be directly related to the formation of the deposits. Because the applied cathodic pulse potential is more negative than both the potentials of iron deposition and hydrogen ion reduction, the deposition of iron species and hydrogen evolution takes place simultaneously. Moreover, oxygen evolution and bubble formation also occur at the counter electrode. The evolved hydrogen gas molecules tend to form bubbles on the electrode surface. These bubbles, after reaching some critical size, detach from the electrode and disappear into the electrolyte/air interface. Hence, PRED replenishes the diffusion layer and allows for the selective dissolution of electrode protrusions. Such selective dissolution

## 4. CONCLUSIONS

High-quality hematite (α-Fe <sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>) photoanodes with nano- structured morphology were successfully prepared by a facile pulse reverse electrodeposition (PRED) method. The process parameters were optimized via photoelectrochemical perform- ance of the photoanodes under 1 sun illumination. The morphology of the α-Fe<sub>2</sub>O <sub>3</sub> prepared by PRED was affected by both the process parameters and the high-temperature annealing. The films were nonporous, granular, and nano- crystalline. Optimum photocurrent performance was noted for the α-Fe<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> electrodes prepared with the duty cycle of 20%, pulse period of 10 ms, and deposition time of 45 s (200 nm film thickness). The enhancement in photocurrent density was attributed to an increase in the charge carrier density, as estimated from Mott−Schottky analysis. Moreover, synthesis conditions of PRED method help in passivating the surface states, thereby shifting the onset of photocurrent in order to accelerate the water oxidation kinetics. This is the first systematic study of α-Fe<sub>2</sub>O <sub>3</sub> electrodes prepared using PRED with regard to optimization of the process parameters and their application in photoelectrochemical water splitting.
