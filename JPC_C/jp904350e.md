## Xu Zong, Guopeng Wu, Hongjian Yan, Guijun Ma, Jingying Shi, Fuyu Wen, Lu Wang, and

Can Li\*

457 Zhongshan Road, Dalian, 116023 China

## ReceiVed: May 11, 2009; ReVised Manuscript ReceiVed: December 23, 2009

Photocatalytic H<sub>2</sub> production on MoS<sub>2</sub>/CdS photocatalysts in the presence of different sacrificial reagents

under visible light (λ > 420 nm) has been investigated. The transformation process of the Mo species loaded

on CdS, together with the junctions formed between MoS<sub>2</sub> and CdS, was clearly demonstrated with X-ray

photoelectron spectroscopy and transmission electron microscopy. Photocatalytic H<sub>2</sub> evolution was optimized

for MoS<sub>2</sub>/CdS catalysts. The 0.2 wt % MoS<sub>2</sub>/CdS catalyst calcined at 573 K achieves the highest overall

activity for H <sub>2</sub> evolution, and the 0.2 wt % MoS<sub>2</sub>/CdS catalyst demonstrates even higher activity than the 0.2

wt % Pt/CdS, irrespective of different sacrificial reagents used. The junctions formed between MoS <sub>2</sub> and CdS

play an important role in enhancing the photocatalytic activity of MoS<sub>2</sub>/CdS catalysts. Electrochemical

measurements indicate that MoS<sub>2</sub> is an excellent H<sub>2</sub> evolution catalyst, which is another very important factor

responsible for the enhancement of the photocatalytic activity of MoS<sub>2</sub>/CdS catalysts.

## 1. Introduction

Photocatalytic H<sub>2</sub> production with heterogeneous photocata- lysts has received much attention due to its attractive potential of supplying H<sub>2</sub> utilizing solar energy.<sup>1-5</sup> For the purpose of producing H<sub>2</sub> with high efficiency, much effort has been devoted to the exploration of novel semiconductor catalysts or new photocatalytic reaction systems.<sup>6-17</sup> Up to now, a lot of photocatalysts, including (oxy)nitrides, such as TaON,<sup>6</sup> Ta<sub>3</sub>N<sub>5</sub>,<sup>7</sup> Y Ta O5N2,<sup>8</sup> (Ga Zn )(N O ),<sup>9</sup> and (Zn Ge)(N O ),<sup>10</sup> and

In addition to the semiconductor materials, surface modification of photo- catalysts with cocatalysts is also crucial for photocatalytic H<sub>2</sub> production reactions. Cocatalysts can offer the low activation potentials for H<sub>2</sub> or O<sub>2</sub> evolution and act as active sites for H<sub>2</sub> or O<sub>2</sub> formation.<sup>18-25</sup> Moreover, cocatalysts could also suppress the recombination of photogenerated charges by the efficient separation of charges on different sites. However, for most photocatalysts reported so far, mainly noble metals or their oxides are used as the cocatalysts,<sup>26-28</sup> while little work was done on using inexpensive cocatalysts as the substitutes for noble metals.

In our recent work, MoS<sub>2</sub> was found to be an efficient cocatalyst for CdS in the photocatalytic H<sub>2</sub> production reac- tions,<sup>29</sup> while factors influencing the photocatalytic activity of MoS2/CdS and the reasons for the enhanced activity of MoS<sub>2</sub>/CdS are not clear yet. In the present work, we focused on these two aspects and found that the rate of H<sub>2</sub> evolution on MoS<sub>2</sub>/CdS was influenced by the chemical state of the Mo species loaded on CdS, the junctions formed between CdS and MoS<sub>2</sub>, and the surface area and the crystallinity of the catalyst. ## 2. Experimental Section

2.1. Preparation of Catalysts. The CdS powder was ob- tained commercially, and (NH<sub>4</sub> )2MoS<sub>4</sub> was prepared according to the literature.<sup>30</sup> The preparation procedure of the catalyst is shown in Scheme 1. CdS was impregnated with ammonia solution containing different amounts of (NH<sub>4</sub>)2MoS4 and then dried with a water bath. The resulting (NH<sub>4</sub>)<sub>2</sub>MoS<sub>4</sub>/CdS precur- sor powder was calcined at temperatures from 423 to 773 K for 2 h in H<sub>2</sub>S to obtain the MoS<sub>2</sub>/CdS catalyst. In most cases, commercial CdS was directly used as the substrate to be loaded with MoS <sub>2</sub>, and the resulting sample was denoted as MoS<sub>2</sub>/CdS. In some cases, CdS was first calcined at 773 K for 5 h in H<sub>2</sub>S, and the resulting CdS, which is denoted as CdS(HT), was used as the substrate. The corresponding catalyst was denoted as MoS <sub>2</sub>/CdS(HT). MoS<sub>2</sub> powder was obtained by calcining (NH<sub>4</sub>)2 MoS<sub>4</sub> at temperatures from 573 to 773 K in H<sub>2</sub>S for 2 h.

2.2. Characterization of Catalysts. The 0.2 wt % MoS<sub>2</sub>/CdS catalyst was demonstrated to be the most active catalyst among the MoS<sub>2</sub>/CdS with varied MoS<sub>2</sub> loadings.<sup>29</sup> So, in most cases, the 0.2 wt % MoS<sub>2</sub>/CdS catalyst was specially character- ized. In some cases, for the purpose of XPS and TEM characterizations, the 1 wt % MoS<sub>2</sub>/CdS catalyst was used. The prepared samples were characterized by X-ray powder diffrac- tion (XRD) on a Rigaku MiniFlex powder diffractometer. Each sample powder was scanned through a 2θ range of 20-60° using Cu KR radiation with an operating voltage of 40 kV and an operating current of 100 mA. X-ray photoelectron spectra (XPS) were recorded on an Amicus X-ray photoelectron spectrometer, using Mg KR radiation. The morphology and particle size of MoS<sub>2</sub>/CdS were examined by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images taken with a Quanta 200FEG scanning electron microscope. TEM micrographs were taken using a Tecnai G2 Spirit (FEI Company) transmission electron microscope. The Brunauer-Emmett-Teller (BET) surface areas were measured on a Micromeritics ASAP 2000 system at liquid nitrogen temperature.

2.3. Photocatalytic Reactions. The photocatalytic reactions were carried out in a Pyrex reaction cell connected to a closed gas circulation and evacuation system. A 0.1 g portion of catalyst was suspended in 200 mL of aqueous solution containing 20 mL of different organic reagents as sacrificial electron donors. Pt was loaded on CdS by a photoreduction method using H<sub>2</sub>PtCl<sub>6</sub> aqueous solution. The suspension was then thoroughly degassed and irradiated by a Xe lamp (300 W) equipped with an optical cutoff filter (λ > 420 nm) to eliminate ultraviolet light and a water filter to remove infrared light. The temperature of the reactant solution was maintained at 283 ( 5 K by a flow of cooling water during the reaction. The amount of H<sub>2</sub> produced was analyzed using an online gas chromatography. The activities of different catalysts were compared by the average rate of H<sub>2</sub> evolution in the first 5 h. The apparent quantum efficiency (Φ) was estimated by the method reported in the literature<sup>31</sup> and calculated using the following equation: Φ(%) ) (2 × R/I) × 100, where R and I represent the number of evolved H<sub>2</sub> molecules and the number of incident photons, respectively. Here, Φ is quantum efficiency, where it is assumed that all incident photons are absorbed by the photocatalyst. The number of incident photons was measured using a calibrated Si photodiode.

2.4. Electrochemical Measurements. The H<sub>2</sub> evolution property of MoS<sub>2</sub> was investigated by electrochemical measure- ments on two electrodes: bare FTO and MoS<sub>2</sub>/FTO electrodes. The MoS<sub>2</sub>/FTO electrode was prepared by dropping a small amount of (NH<sub>4</sub>)2MoS<sub>4</sub> methanol solution onto the FTO glass using a pipet, followed by a calcination at 573 K for 2 h in H<sub>2</sub>S to convert (NH<sub>4</sub>)2MoS<sub>4</sub> to MoS<sub>2</sub>. Because treatment with temperatures higher than 673 K in H<sub>2</sub>S will damage the FTO surface, electrochemical measurement were done on the MoS<sub>2</sub>/FTO electrode prepared at 573 K. Electrochemical experiments were performed in a three-electrode cell made of quartz. A Pt plate and a saturated calomel electrode (SCE) were employed as the counter and reference electrodes, and the as-prepared electrodes were used as the working electrodes. A 0.5 M Na<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> solution was used as the electrolyte. Current-voltage curves were recorded by an electrochemical workstation (CHI660A, Shanghai Chenhua Instruments, China).

3.3. Effect of Junctions Formed between CdS and MoS<sub>2</sub> on the Photocatalytic Activity. From the HRTEM images of MoS<sub>2</sub>/CdS, the intimate junction formed between CdS and MoS<sub>2</sub> is clearly observed (Figure 4). To investigate the role that junctions between MoS<sub>2</sub> and CdS played on the photocatalytic activity of MoS<sub>2</sub>/CdS photocatalysts, a mechanical mixture of MoS<sub>2</sub> and CdS was made. MoS<sub>2</sub> powder was obtained by calcining (NH<sub>4</sub>)<sub>2</sub>MoS<sub>4</sub> in H<sub>2</sub>S at different temperatures. The as- prepared MoS <sub>2</sub> powder was simply mixed with CdS at room temperature and used as catalyst in the photocatalytic reactions. For the sake of comparison, the CdS catalyst obtained by calcining commercial CdS at 573 K for 2 h was used for the mixed catalyst. As shown in table 1, CdS alone demonstrates only a little activity and MoS<sub>2</sub> alone is nearly inactive for the production of H<sub>2</sub>. However, when MoS<sub>2</sub> powder (0.01 g) prepared by calcining (NH<sub>4</sub>)2MoS4 at 573 K was employed in the mixture, the rate of H <sub>2</sub> evolution on the mixture of CdS and MoS<sub>2</sub> was greatly enhanced compared with CdS alone (Table 1, entry 1). The activity of the mixed catalyst tends to decrease when a smaller amount of MoS<sub>2</sub> was used.<sup>29</sup> If MoS<sub>2</sub> was directly loaded on CdS by calcining the (NH<sub>4</sub>)2MoS<sub>4</sub>/CdS at 573 K, the resulting MoS <sub>2</sub> (0.0002 g)/CdS (0.1 g) catalyst (Table 1, entry 4) shows even higher activity than the mixture of CdS (0.1 g) and MoS<sub>2</sub> (0.01 g), although a much lesser amount of MoS <sub>2</sub> was used. Therefore, the intimate junctions formed between CdS and MoS<sub>2</sub> are crucial for the interelectron transfer between the two components. When MoS<sub>2</sub> prepared by calcining (NH<sub>4</sub>)2MoS<sub>4</sub> at 673 and 773 K was used in the mixtures, the rate of H<sub>2</sub> evolution on the mixtures was further decreased compared with that on the mixture of CdS and MoS<sub>2</sub> prepared at 573 K (Table 1, entries 2 and 3). Therefore, the catalytic efficiency of MoS<sub>2</sub> is supposed to be decreased with the increase of the calcination temperature of MoS<sub>2</sub> from 573 to 773 K.

## 4. Conclusions

The photocatalytic H<sub>2</sub> production on CdS under visible light (λ > 420 nm) can be significantly enhanced by loading MoS<sub>2</sub> as a cocatalyst using different organic sacrificial reagents. The optimized 0.2 wt % MoS<sub>2</sub>/CdS catalyst demonstrated even higher activity than the 0.2 wt % Pt/CdS catalyst, regardless of the different sacrificial reagents used. It is found that the excellent catalytic property of MoS<sub>2</sub>, together with the junctions formed between MoS<sub>2</sub> and CdS, is mainly responsible for the greatly enhanced activity of MoS<sub>2</sub>/CdS in the photocatalytic H<sub>2</sub> production.
