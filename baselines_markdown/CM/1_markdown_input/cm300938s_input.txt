1-001	The field of organic electronic devices, such as organic light- emitting diodes (OLEDs), thin-film transistors (OTFTs), and photovoltaic cells (OPVs), is characterized by fast-paced progress and mainly driven by the discovery and availability
1-002	of key chromophores as building blocks.
1-003	Diketopyrrolo[3,4- c]pyrrole (DPP) is present as a key structural unit in an important class of red pigments that has exceptional light, weather, and heat stability,[1] and is widely used as a building block in the construction of a variety of compounds and
1-004	based on DPP-fluorene copolymers emit orange light with an external quantum efficiency of 0.45%.[3]
1-005	Bulk heterojunction solar cells showing a power conversion efficiency of 4.4% for DPP oligomers and 5.5% for DPP polymers have been reported,[6,8] which is comparable to the one based on regioregular poly(3-hexylthiophene).[13]
1-006	The hole mobility up
1-007	to 1.95 cm2 V−1 s−1 for the DPP polymer-based transistor has
1-008	been achieved.[5]
1-009	In looking for other champion materials, structural modifications on DPP derivatives have been done mainly at
1-010	the 3,6-positions and the nitrogen atom of the lactam moiety.

1-012	The N-alkylation can improve solubility and alter molecular stacking in the solid state.
1-013	Thiophene,[4] furan,[14,15] benzene,[3,16] thienothiophene,[5] fluorene,[17] carbazole,[18] cyclopenta[2,1-b:3,4-b′]dithiophene,[17] dithieno[3,2-b:2′,3′-d]pyrrole,[19] and other groups have been introduced at the 3,6-positions, affording many compounds and polymers with different band gaps and some unique properties.
1-014	However, a decrease in the band gap of DPP compounds is always accompanied along by an increase in the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) level.[17]
1-015	For solar cell applications, donor materials with a narrow band gap and low-lying HOMO level are desirable because of their contribution to an increase in short-circuit current (Jsc) and open-circuit voltage (Voc),[20,21] and improvement of the long- term stability of organic optoelectronic devices.
1-016	For photo- detector applications, narrow-band-gap polymers can extend photocurrent spectral response to the near infrared (NIR) region, and low-lying HOMO level could lead to a high detectivity by decreasing the dark current.[22]
1-017	However, as in the case of the development of DPP-based optoelectronic

1-022	DPP has a planar, conjugated 8π-electron bicyclic ring and contains the indigo-like, cross-conjugated vinylogous amidic and vinylogous hydrazine units.
1-023	Its lactim form has a bicyclic 2,5-diazapentalene (DAP) unit (Figure 1).
1-024	By chemical
1-025	transformation of the lactam group in DPP to the lactim derivatives, the two chromophores PPO (pyrrolo[3,4-c]pyrrol-1(2H)-one) and DAPO[25] could be produced.
1-026	By analogy, dithioketopyrrolopyrrole (DTPP), derived from DPP by thionation,[26] could also yield the two new chromophores, PPT (pyrrolo[3,4-c]pyrrol-1(2H)-thione) and DAP (Figure 1).
1-027	However, due to relative reactivity S-alkylation > N-alkylation > O-alkylation, DPP, PPO, and DAP are preferably produced during the alkylation of DPP and DTPP, respectively.
1-028	Therefore, PPO and DAP are potentially useful building blocks for a variety of new optoelectronic compounds and polymers that may show some interesting properties.
1-029	The computational calculation was carried out on the three chromophores DPP, PPO, and DAP.
1-030	The energy gap of three chromophores shows the order DPP > PPO > DAP, and their HOMO levels gradually decease in order from DPP to PPO to DAP (Figure 2).
1-031	In comparison with DPP, lack of the donation from lone-pair electrons on the nitrogen to the π-orbit of the diene moiety may contribute to lowering the HOMO levels in PPO and DAP.
1-032	The calculation results imply that DAP-
1-033	containing compounds and polymers could have a low band gap and also low-lying HOMO levels.
1-034	Encouraged by this predication from calculation results, several representative compounds and polymers (Figure 3) containing PPO and DAP were designed, synthesized, and characterized.
1-035	By studying the chemical and photophysical properties, the unique halochromic and halofluoric properties of these new chromophores and polymers were also revealed.
1-036	Furthermore, the NIR photodetectors with photocurrent spectral response up to 1000 nm were fabricated and characterized.

3-002	Design and Synthesis.
3-003	The chemical structures of the PPO and DAP-based compounds and polymers are illustrated in Figure 3, and the synthetic routes are outlined in Scheme 1.
3-004	As a result of competitive N- and O-alkylations, PPO 2 was obtained in 14% yield along with N-alkylated DPP 1 (Scheme
3-005	1).
3-006	Bromination of 2 yielded only monobrominated PPO, and subsequent Suzuki cross-coupling reaction afforded three PPO compounds 4a−c.
3-007	A large family of DAP-containing compounds can be readily derived in high yields from the corresponding DTPP compounds.
3-008	As an example, alkylation of DTPP gave exclusively DAP 3.
3-009	Bromination at the two terminal thiophene rings of 3 yielded a dibromo compound that is useful as a building block for constructing a variety of DAP-containing compounds (e.g., 5) and as a monomer for making many conjugated polymers (e.g., 6a−d) by the Suzuki or Stille cross-coupling reaction.
3-010	The fluorene, carbazole, and other electron- donating moieties were introduced in compounds 4 and polymers 6 in order to tune the optical and electrochemical properties.
3-011	Thermal and Optical Properties.

3-068	Photodetector and Detectivity.
3-069	To demonstrate the potential applications of DAP-based materials, polymer 6d with a narrow band gap of 1.19 eV was used to fabricate polymer photodetector devices.
3-070	The device configuration is ITO/ PEDOT:PSS (35 nm)/active layer (100 nm)/Al (100 nm), the active layer comprises a blend of polymer 6d and [6,6]-phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PC61BM) in a weight ratio of 1:3.
3-071	The dark current density was 3.27 × 10−9 (A/cm[2]) at bias of −0.1 V (Figure S10, Supporting Information) and even lower than that of the reported high-detectivity photo- detector based on a narrow-band gap polymer,[22] which may be attributed to, at least in part, the lower HOMO level of polymer 6d.
3-072	The photocurrent spectral response covered a wide range from 400 nm to 1000 nm, matching well with the absorption profile of the film of polymer 6d and PC61BM mixture (Figure
3-073	7).
3-074	The EQE and detectivity were calculated at 800 nm (points A and B in Figure 7d) as 4.0% and 1.6 × 1011 Jones, respectively.
3-075	The relatively low EQE value may be due to the large domain size of the active layer (about 100 nm, Figure S10, Supporting Information) and the close LUMO levels of 6d and PC 61BM, which could lead to insufficient exciton dissociation.
3-076	At the wavelengths from 400 to 850 nm, the device exhibited a photodetectivity greater than 1011 Jones, being comparable to previously reported polymer photodetectors.[46,47]
3-077	In conclusion, by simple chemical transformation of readily available DPP derivatives, a new family of DAP chromophores and polymers are obtained.
3-078	DAP-based materials have smaller energy gaps and lower HOMO levels relative to DPP analogs and display unique acid-induced halochromic fluorescence in the NIR spectral region.
3-079	Further band gap tuning is likely to afford suitable DAP polymers for applications in NIR optoelectronic devices such as NIR photodetectors and NIR OLEDs.
