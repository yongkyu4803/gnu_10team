1-002	Over the past several years, the field of organic electronics has received intense research attention.[1]
1-003	Striking new materials systems have been developed to fulfill the requirements of organic field-effect transistors (OFETs)[2] and organic photo- voltaic (OPV) cells.[3]
1-004	Organic semiconductors represent an attractive, low cost alternative to amorphous silicon for numerous devices, and in fact, some materials already out- perform it in terms of carrier mobility.
1-005	Moreover, the use of organic molecules paves the way for an array of new applications where low cost, minimal weight, and mechanical flexibility will be required.[4]

3-002	Small Molecule Synthesis.
3-003	The synthesis of the present new materials is straightforward, as shown in Scheme 1.
3-004	First, 3-
3-005	bromo-2-iodothiophene and 3-bromo-2-ethynylthiophene were prepared according to previously published procedures.[30]
3-006	These two compounds were used to perform a Sonogashira coupling,[34] which afforded compound 1 in good yield (82%).
3-007	The subsequent step was another Sonogashira coupling in order to introduce an additional alkyne chain having different aliphatic substituents to tune the solubility and thin-film organization of the resulting materials.
3-008	Specifically, three different side chains were investigated in this study, 1-octyne, 1-hexadecyne, and 4-ethyloct-1-yne; the latter is not commercially available and was synthesized following the procedure of Janssen et al.[30] While smaller side chains were synthesized, solubility issues prohibited their use in this study.
3-009	Note that, for the synthesis of compound 1, a Pd(II) catalyst [PdCl 2(PPh3)2] was effective.
3-010	However, the same catalyst gave poor yields in the syntheses of compounds 2, 3, and 4, and therefore the Pd(0) source [Pd(PPh3)4] was used.
3-011	The reaction yields varied from 67% to 90% depending on the substituent type.
3-012	The final step in the synthesis of the new building blocks is the stannylation of the terminal thiophene positions via double lithiation, followed by treatment with Me3SnCl which yields compounds 5, 6, and 7 in 95, 88, and 71% yields, respectively.
3-013	Simple bromination using N- bromosuccinimide (NBS) was also attempted with compound
3-014	6.
3-015	Unfortunately, the NBS reaction at room temperature in the dark for 1 h yields a mixture of numerous side products as assessed by TLC.
3-016	This latter result indicates that not only are the thiophene 5 positions reactive but the triple bonds are also subject to reaction.
3-017	The lithiation reaction does not evidence any side products and is very clean since only a quick precipitation in methanol is required for purification.
3-018	This versatile route to these compounds opens the door to future small molecule and polymer syntheses using this building block.
3-019	The final step in these syntheses is Stille cross-coupling[35] between a monobromo DPP, which was synthesized according to previously published procedure and compounds 5, 6, and 7.

3-047	Thermal Properties.
3-048	Thermal analysis of the present EBT- based small molecules was carried out by differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) at a temperature ramp rate of 10 °C/min.
3-049	As shown in Figure S1 (Supporting Information), no obvious thermal features can be detected in 8, 9, or 10, and heating beyond 250 °C risks thermal decomposition.
3-050	Small molecules exhibit sharp thermal transitions compared to polymers;[38] however, considering the size and bulkiness of the substituents in the present small molecules, there may be too much disorder in the molecular packing to exhibit significant crystallinity.
3-051	The presence of thermal transitions is, of course, a useful guide for determining appropriate thin film annealing temperatures for OFET and OPV processing.
3-052	Redox Properties.
3-053	Cyclic voltammetry was performed on all materials in order to evaluate their electrochemical properties such as the oxidation and reduction potentials, which yield HOMO and LUMO (lowest unoccupied molecular orbital) energies as well as electrochemical band gaps.
3-054	Data are compiled in Table 1, and representative voltammograms are shown in Figure 4.
3-055	The curves shown are the first scan for each material.
3-056	Note that alkyne-containing molecule electrochemical behavior varies widely; however, most recent studies report quasi-reversible or even completely reversible redox proper- ties.[24]
3-057	Nevertheless, there are still some examples showing no
3-058	redox reversibility.[39]
3-059	The curves recorded in this study show that the three materials display nearly complete reductive reversibility, whereas oxidation is only quasi-reversible.
3-060	Solid state cyclic voltammetry was also performed on thin films of these materials; however, the results were substantially different, as shown in Figure S2 (Supporting Information).
3-061	The reversibility of film reduction and oxidation was not evident, which may be due to dewetting of the films from the platinum electrode or oxidation of the EBT unit to unidentified species.
3-062	Possible oxidation processes that seem most plausible were demonstrated by Bildstein et al.[40] Figure S3 (Supporting Information) shows how a radical cation might be stabilized.
3-063	The cyclic voltammetry enables estimation of the HOMO and LUMO energies of the present EBT derivatives.
3-064	The oxidation onsets of compounds 8, 9, and 10 are very similar (0.34−0.42 V vs Fc/Fc+) giving HOMO energies of 5.44−5.52 eV.
3-065	Such low-lying HOMOs are common for small conjugate molecules.
3-066	Conjugated polymers, especially DPP-containing macromolecules, are known to have higher-lying HOMOs (around 5.00−5.20 eV), which is expected for structures having more extensive conjugation.[29a,b] Note that low-lying HOMOs generally correlate with large open-circuit voltages (VOC) in bulk heterojunction (BHJ) OPVs.
3-067	This small molecule characteristic could, in principle, be advantageous in OPVs because, while polymers can exhibit very good field-effect mobilities (p- and n-type) and very good short-circuit currents (JSC), VOC values can be lower than in small molecules.[41]
3-068	Similarly, reduction potentials are used here to estimate the LUMO energies of compounds 8−10.
3-069	Once again, the values are similar, ranging from 3.64 to 3.78 eV (−1.32 to −1.46 V vs Fc/Fc+).
3-070	In general, bulky side chains are only expected to alter redox properties if they alter the conjugation length by twisting aromatic groups out of coplanarity.
3-071	In this case, the EBT triple bond should act as a spacer, preserving the molecular planarity even with 2-ethylhexyl substituents.

3-073	Electronic Structure Analysis.
3-074	Density functional theory (DFT) calculations on (TDPP)2-EBT containing methyl substituents were carried out at the DFT level.[42]
3-075	Methyl side chains were chosen to make the calculations more tractable.
3-076	Beckes three-parameter exchange functional combined with the LYP correlation functional (B3LYP)[43] was employed with the standard 6-31G basis set.[44]
3-077	The molecular geometries of the neutral and charged species were first optimized.
3-078	In addition, to
3-079	the frontier molecular orbitals of methylated (TDPP) 2-EBT.
3-080	better understand the electrical performance within this semiconductor series, intramolecular reorganization energies for both hole and electrons were also estimated theoretically using the DFT formalism.
3-081	The intramolecular reorganization energy (λ) considers the molecular structure reorganization required to accommodate added charge as a prerequisite for efficient transport.
3-082	It consists of two terms associated with the geometric relaxation energies upon going from the neutral to the charged species and vice versa, and these are computed directly from the adiabatic potential energy surfaces, as described previously.[45]
3-083	As it can be seen in Figure 5, molecule 8 is planar with skeletal distortions of less than 10°, which indicates an effective π-conjugation.
3-084	Furthermore, the topologies of the HOMO and LUMO indicate delocalization of both orbitals throughout the entire π-conjugated skeleton, which is desirable for a greater π−π interaction and thus enhanced electrical properties.
3-085	In addition, reorganization energies for both holes and electrons are estimated to be 0.15 eV.
3-086	These values are quite low and, in fact, are comparable to those reported for pentacene[46] (0.097 eV for holes) and other fused benzene-thiophene structures[47] (0.118−0.204 eV for holes), which exhibit high performance in OFETs.

4-002	In this contribution we show that alkyne-functionalized electron donor building blocks can be very efficiently synthesized via Sonogoshira coupling.
4-003	The new EBT core was prepared with various alkyl substituents, which play a significant role not only in the resulting physiochemical properties of the materials but also in the performances of TFT and OPV devices incorporating them.
4-004	The three new EBT derivatives flanked by the DPP π-acceptor unit afford low band gaps and low-lying HOMOs as indicated by optical spectros- copy and cyclic voltammetry.
4-005	Good hole mobilities as high as 0.17 cm2/(V s) for compound 8 with ION/IOFF greater than 104 are measured even under ambient conditions.
4-006	XRD and AFM characterization of the spin-coated films reveal modest crystallinity but good homogeneity, affording very uniform/ reproducible device performance.
4-007	Furthermore, DFT calcu- lations indicate low reorganization energies for both holes and electrons, which is desirable for efficient charge transport.
4-008	BHJ solar cells fabricated with these small molecules yield PCEs close to 2%.
4-009	Thus, EBT-related cores show promise for developing new organic opto-electronic materials.
