1-001	■
1-002	Ambipolar OTFTs based on polymeric semiconductors with high and balanced electron and hole mobilities are currently of great interest,[1] due to their applications in complementary-like circuits[2] and in light-emitting transistors.[3]
1-003	Effective methods developed thus far for fabricating ambipolar OTFTs include blending of unipolar p- and n-channel materials[3b,4] or using a single-component layer but with asymmetric electrodes of different work functions.[5]
1-004	These methods, however, signifi- cantly increase the device complexity and therefore the manufacturing cost.
1-005	Covalently linked donor−acceptor (D-A) copolymers provide an effective strategy to develop single- component ambipolar semiconductors,[6] because fine-tuning of energy levels through appropriate combination of D−A building blocks makes it possible for efficient charge injection of both holes and electrons from the same-type electrodes.
1-006	Proper selection of acceptor units is a key step for ambipolar D−A polymers, including diketopyrrolopyrrole (DPP),[7] naphthalenediimide (NDI),[8] and benzothiadiazole (BZ).[9]
1-007	Recently, improved mobilities of both holes and electrons (over 1 cm2 V−1 s −1) have been obtained for DPP-based copolymers.[10]
1-008	However, most of them were achieved under nitrogen, and few are known to operate in ambient conditions.
1-009	Development of air-stable ambipolar polymeric semiconductors is of crucial importance for practical applications, especially, for utilizing the superior rheological properties of polymer materials in the printing process.
1-010	© 2013 American Chemical Society
1-011	For achieving high performance air-stable ambipolar polymers, three key criteria need to be met: (i) low-lying lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO < −4.0 eV) for air-stable electron injection and conduction; (ii) appropriate highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) ensuring low- energy bandgap (<1.8 eV) for both hole and electron injection from the same-type electrodes; (iii) structural planarity of the polymer backbone for tight solid-state packing and therefore efficient charge delocalization.
1-012	Optimizing these criteria requires concurrent up- and/or down-movement of both HOMO and LUMO energy levels of D−A polymers.
1-013	This, however, is not an easy task with respect to molecular design, because a limited box of available acceptors makes it difficult to systematically modify the LUMO levels of single-type-acceptor copolymers.
1-014	For example, due to its highly electron-deficient character of NDI acceptors, NDI-based D−A copolymers are air-stable but inherently favored for electron transport.[11,12]
1-015	Although introduction of strong electron-donating groups, such as dialkoxybithiophene, can lead to ambipolar NDI-based D−A polymers, the hole-mobility (maximum 10−3 cm 2 V−1 s −1) is an order of magnitude lower than the electron-mobility.[2b,13]
1-016	Herein, we developed a novel strategy to construct ambipolar copolymers, in which NDI moieties are covalently connected with BZ units through thiophene (T) linkers, namely, PNDI-

1-020	mT(BZ)mT (m = 1, 2).
1-021	BZ groups are conventional acceptors widely used in D−A polymers for organic photovoltaics (OPVs)[14] and OTFTs.[3d,15] However, in PNDI-mT(BZ)mT polymers, the coplanarity of BZ core with flanking thiophenes makes mT(BZ)mT a whole as electron donors rather than acceptors.
1-022	We found that decreasing the number of thiophene linkers from m = 2 to 1 lowers both LUMO and HOMO energy levels.
1-023	Under ambient conditions, PNDI-2T(BZ)2T exhibits unipolar p-type transport with hole mobility up to 0.07 ± 0.02 cm2 V−1 s−1, whereas PNDI-T(BZ)T presents balanced ambipolar transport with electron and hole mobilities up to 0.05 ± 0.02 and 0.1 ± 0.03 cm2 V −1 s−1, respectively.
1-024	Furthermore, OTFT devices based on both polymers are air-stable and exhibit negligible variations when stored in ambient over 3 months.
1-025	Our results provide an example for rational design and synthesis of air-stable ambipolar polymeric semi- conductors.

2-048	Device Fabrication and Characterization.
2-049	Top-contact/bottom- gate OTFT devices were fabricated using n++-Si/SiO2 substrates where n++-Si and SiO2 were used as the gate electrode and gate dielectric, respectively.
2-050	The substrates were subjected to cleaning using ultrasonication in acetone, ethanol, and deionized water.
2-051	After cleaning the SiO 2/Si wafer with piranha solution (7:3 mixture of H2SO4 and H2O2 by volume ratio) and UV-ozone plasma treatment, the SiO 2 surface was modified with n-octadecyltrimethoxysilane (OTS) self- assembled monolayer (SAM) as reported.[18]
2-052	The thin films of the polymers were deposited on the treated substrates by spin-coating using a polymer solution (8−10 mg/mL) at 1500 rpm for 60 s, optionally followed by thermal annealing at 120 °C, 160 °C, 200 °C, and 240 °C under ambient condition.
2-053	After organic thin-film deposition, about 60-nm thick gold thin films were deposited as source and drain contacts using a shadow mask.
2-054	The OTFT devices had a channel length (L) of 200 (or 30) μm and a channel width (W) of 500 μm.
2-055	The evaluations of the OTFTs were carried out in atmosphere using a Keithley 4200 parameter analyzer on a probe stage.
2-056	The carrier mobility, μ, was calculated from the data in the saturated regime according to the equation ISD = (W/2L)Ciμ(VG − VT)[2], where ISD is the drain current in the saturated regime.
2-057	W and L are, respectively, the semiconductor channel width and length, Ci (Ci = 10 nF) is the capacitance per unit area of the gate dielectric layer, and VG and VT are the gate voltage and threshold voltage.
2-058	VG − VT of the device was determined from the relationship between the square root of ISD and VG at the saturated regime.

PNDI-T(BZ)T (31.0 kDa) showed a higher Mn than PNDI-2T(BZ)2T (11.5 kDa) as well as a slightly lower polydispersity index (PDI: 2.4 vs 2.2) (Figure S1).

3-008	The thermal gravimetric analysis (TGA) revealed that both polymers exhibited good thermal stability: PNDI-T(BZ)T with 5% weight loss at 454 °C and PNDI-2T(BZ)2T with 5% weight loss at 288 °C (Figure S2).
3-009	A differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) thermograph of PNDI-T(BZ)T showed endothermic and exothermic peaks at 362 and 297 °C, respectively, while only endothermic transition was observed at 232 °C for PNDI-2T(BZ)2T (Figure S3).
3-010	DFT-Calculated Molecular Geometries and Orbitals.
3-011	To predict the minimum-energy conformation of the polymer backbone and the LUMO/HOMO, we performed DFT calculations (B3LYP/6-31G(d)) for trimers of NDI-mT(BZ)mT and m = 1, 2 (Figure 1).
3-012	In order to reduce the calculation time, long alkyl chains were replaced by methyl groups in the calculations.
3-013	The dihedral angles between the BZ core and the neighboring thiophene units are calculated to be 4° and 2° in NDI-T(BZ)T and NDI-2T(BZ)2T trimers, respectively, much smaller than that typically in oligothiophenes (12°).[19]
3-014	The distance between the nitrogen atom of BZ core and the β-hydrogen atom of neighboring thiophene, i.e., short contacts of N···H (indicated by red-dashed lines), is calculated to be ca. 2.32 Å, much shorter than the sum of the van der Waals radii (2.59 Å).[20]
3-015	It is these short contact heteroatom interactions
3-016	that improve the coplanarity of mT(BZ)mT.
3-017	Because of the existence of dihedral angle 12° between two thiophene rings in PNDI-2T(BZ)2T, the planarity of PNDI-T(BZ)T backbone should be even better than that of PNDI-2T(BZ)2T (Figure S4).
3-018	Most importantly, in both polymers, the HOMOs are mainly located in the mT(BZ)mT moieties, while the LUMOs concentrate on NDI parts.
3-019	Moreover, the better planarity of PNDI-T(BZ)T enhances the delocalization of both LUMO and HOMO along the backbone.
3-020	In any event, well-planar mT(BZ)mT moieties taken as a whole serve as donors in our PNDI-mT(BZ)mT polymers, though the electron deficient BZ core is usually the acceptor part in D−A polymers.

4-001	■
4-002	We have reported the design, synthesis, and characterization of two polymers PNDI-mT(BZ)mT (m = 1, 2).
4-003	Unipolar p-type transport (μh = 0.07 ± 0.02 cm2 V−1 s −1) was observed for OTFTs based on PNDI-2T(BZ)2T. By simply reducing the thiophene number from 2 to 1 in the repeat unit, balanced ambipolarity was achieved in PNDI-T(BZ)T with electron and hole mobilities up to 0.05 ± 0.02 and 0.1 ± 0.03 cm2 V −1 s −1, respectively.
4-004	Our results demonstrated an efficient approach to enhance the hole mobilities of NDI-based polymers without sacrificing the electron mobilities.
4-005	The good air-stability was evidenced by the trifling variations in charge mobilities of OTFTs of both polymers when stored in ambient over 3 months.
