1-016	Very recently, n-channel semiconductors have been finding applications in another type of optoelectronic device, the organic light-emitting transistors (OLETs).[35−40] In this device the switching function of an OFET and the light-emitting capabilities of an OLED are combined, possibly enabling the realization of display using a far more simple architecture.[41−43] OLETs can be fabricated using different structures, including a single semiconductor layer, a p-/n-bilayer, and, more recently, a p-/emissive/n-trilayer architecture (Figure 1B).[44−47] The advantage of the trilayer structure is that the hole/electron charge transport and light emission are physically decoupled and occur primarily in the p-/n-channel and emissive layers, respectively.
1-017	In the first exploitation of this architecture, the n-channel layer was DFH-4T, while the p-channel and emissive layers were DH-4T and Alq3:DCM, respectively.
1-018	Red-emissive OLET devices with considerable external quantum efficiencies (EQEs) of 5% were demonstrated.[44]
1-019	In a recent study, a nonplanar electrode geometry was used in trilayer OLET devices in order to enhance the key parameters of quantum efficiency, brightness, switching, and mobility across the red, green, and blue colors range.[48]
1-020	In these devices, PBTTT polymer was used as p-channel layer and DFH-4T was used as n-channel layer with various polymeric emissive layers, and
1-021	EQEs of up to 1.9% were achieved.
1-022	Due to the great promise of molecular n-channel semiconductors in trilayer OLET devices, realizing new molecular semiconductors with relatively high LUMO level and efficient electron transport characteristics is very important for the further development of device performances and full color ranges.
1-023	Among the n-channel semiconductor classes studied to date, thiophene-based oligomers have by far attracted the greatest attention due to their chemical and electrochemical stability, synthetic accessibility, straightforward chemical modification, and remarkable device performances.[49,50]
1-024	Although oligo- and polythiophenes were originally developed as p-type semi- conductors,[51−53] we demonstrated that electron transport is feasible after core functionalization with strong electron-withdrawing substituents.[54,55]
1-025	In the early 2000s, DFH-4T, DFH-6T, and FTTTTF (Figure 1) were introduced as the first examples of n-channel oligothiophene semiconductors exhibit- ing electron mobilities (μe) of 0.02−0.4 cm2/(V·s).
1-026	In these structures relatively π-electron-rich quaterthiophene (4T) and sexithiophene (6T) cores were functionalized with perfluor- oalkyl (−n-C6F13 ) and perfluoroarene (−C6 F4− and −C6F5 ) substituents.[56]
1-027	Following these initial reports, over the past decade, several oligothiophenes have been functionalized with acyl (RCO−), perfluoroacyl (R FCO−), perfluorobenzoyl (Ph FCO−), and cyano (−CN) substitutents, and a few of these systems exhibit impressive device performances (μe ≥ 0.5 cm2/(V·s)).[57,58]
1-028	For these structures the charge carrier polarity inversion from p- to n-type has been studied systematically, and
1-029	it was found to be due the combination of reduced (stabilized) LUMO energy level, enhanced electron delocalization along the molecular π-system, efficient LUMO···LUMO intermolecular electronic coupling (bandwidth), proper molecular regiochem- istry, and suitable thin-film morphology/microstructure.[59]
1-030	Recently, another approach for designing n-channel oligothio- phenes includes core functionalization with nitrogen-contain- ing, electron-poor five- and six-membered heterocycles such as thiazole, pyridine, pyridazine, and pyrimidine.[60]
1-031	In these structures, electron-withdrawing imine/azo nitrogen(s) (CHN or NN) within the cyclo increases the electron-deficiency of these cores resulting in lower HOMO and LUMO energy levels compared to their corresponding π-isoelectronic ethenyl (CHCH) structures.[61,62]
1-032	Among the resulting heterocycles, thiazole is an important five- membered analogue of thiophene where the “CH” group at the 3 position is replaced with “N”.[63−65] From a molecular design point of view, thiazole offers several key characteristics as a promising building block for n-channel semiconductors: (i) low LUMO energy level for enhanced electron injection/ stabilization/transport (ΔLUMO = 0.2−0.3 eV compared to thiophene),[66] (ii) large effective dipole moment (μexp = 1.61 D for thiazole vs 0.52 D for thiophene)[67,68] to facilitate molecular alignment/packing, (iii) nonbonded interactions (“N···S” and “N···HC”) to enhance structural planarity and intermolecular interactions,[62] and (iv) reduced steric congestion-induced π-backbone twisting by eliminating repulsive CH···HC interactions when linked to arene counits.[66,69,70]
1-033	Despite the large potential of thiazole-based oligomers in a broad range of
1-034	applications as efficient electron transporting materials, their development has lagged behind that of other π-conjugated classes, and up to date only a limited number of families have been studied.[71−74] Some examples of thiazole−thiophene semiconductors terminated with trifluoromethylphenyl groups (DCF3-P(2,2′)Tz2P2, DCF3-P(5,5′)Tz2P2, and DCF3 -TzTzT2P2) are shown in Figure 1.
1-035	The limited number of thiazole-based semiconductors is mainly due to more challenging/less developed synthesis of (bi)thiazole-based building blocks.[75,76]
1-036	Considering all the aforementioned properties we believe that continuous efforts to design and synthesize new thiazole-based n-channel semiconductors is very crucial to further expand their scope as n-channel semi- conductors and to better understand structure−property relationships.
1-037	In this contribution, we demonstrate the molecular design, synthesis, characterization, and OTFT device performance of four new regioselectively linked thiazole−thiophene oligomers DFH-T-5,5′Tz2-T (1), DFH-T-2,2′Tz2-T (2), DFH-T-5,5′Tz4-T (3), and DFH-T-2,2′Tz4-T (4) (Figure 2).
1-038	These oligomers are functionalized with strong electron-withdrawing perfluorohexyl (−n-C6 F13) substituents at α,ω-positions, and their nitrogen regiochemistry is controlled via 2,2′ and 5,5′ inter-ring linkages.
1-039	Top-contact/bottom-gate OTFT devices fabricated via vapor deposition of these materials indicate that 1−4 are excellent n-channel semiconductors with electron mobilities of 0.1−1.3 cm2/(V·s).
1-040	The highest mobility (μe = 1.3 cm2/(V·s)) is achieved for room-temperature deposited thin- films of compound 2.

2-161	The absence of first order reflections is consistent with previously reported thin-film XRD spectra of several thiophene and thiophene−thiazole oligomers, resulting from their crystal structure belonging to the C2/c space group, which does not allow first-order reflections.[56,71−74] The d-spacings obtained from the second order reflection peaks are 2.62 nm for 1, 2.67 nm for 2, 3.21 nm for 3, and 3.27 nm for 4, which for 1 and 2 are consistent with the terrace heights measured by AFM images (vide supra).
2-162	Since the molecular lengths obtained from the optimized geometries are ∼2.7 nm for 1 and 2 and ∼3.4 nm for 3 and 4, the present semiconductors have molecular orientations on the dielectric surface which are almost perpendicular (for 1 and 2) or only slightly tilted (∼19° versus the surface normal for 3 and 4).
2-163	The preferential edge- on molecular orientations relative to the substrate and highly interconnected grain based morphology for all semiconductors ensures an efficient electron transport in the plane of the film benefiting from efficient π−π stacking.
2-164	Note, the broad peaks observed for all of the films centered at 21° are assigned to be a reflection coming from the underneath PMMA layer, which is confirmed by the θ−2θ scan of PMMA/SiO2 substrates (Supporting Information Figure S5E).

2-166	Trilayer Light-Emitting Transistor Device Charac- terization.
2-167	The relatively high LUMO energy level (−3.2 eV) of semiconductor 2, coupled with the high electron mobility (1.3 cm2/(V·s)), makes it a good candidate for heterostructure OLETs.[44]
2-168	As the preliminary study, single-layer OFETs based on 2 are fabricated on glass/ITO/PMMA substrates (Au contacts), which is the gate-dielectric platform for trilayer OLET devices, to understand its charge transport behavior on only PMMA dielectric.
2-169	Semiconductor 2 exhibits very high electron mobility of 1.3 cm2/(V·s) on PMMA (Supporting
2-170	Information Figure S7), which is 2−3 times larger than that of DFH-4T (μe = 0.5 cm2/(V·s)) on the same substrate.[44]
2-171	The trilayer heterostructure OLETs (Figure 8, OLET-a and OLET-b) used in this study are fabricated by vacuum- depositing the active organic layers (semiconductor (p- channel)/emissive/semiconductor (n-channel)) and gold con- tacts on a transparent substrate, composed of 450 nm of PMMA spin-coated on glass/indium tin oxide (ITO, 150 nm).
2-172	The active region consists of three organic layers, i.e., top and bottom semiconductor layers which transport different types of charge carriers (electrons and holes) and a middle light-emitting layer where the charge recombination and light emission occur.
2-173	In this study, DH4T and tris(8-hydroxyquino- line) aluminum (Alq3) are used as the p-channel semi- conductor layer and the emitting layer, respectively, since their molecular energy levels are compatible with 2.
2-174	In the first type of OLET configuration (Figure 8B, OLET-a), the first, in contact with PMMA dielectric, and the third layers are p-channel (DH-4T, 15 nm) and n-channel (2, 30 nm) semiconductors, respectively, whereas the middle layer is Alq3 (15 nm).
2-175	In the second type of OLET (Figure 8C, OLET-b), the active region layer configuration is reversed.
2-176	In both types of device configurations gold contacts (70 nm, source-drain) are deposited on top of the organic stack to form channels with length and width of 70 μm and 12 mm, respectively.
2-177	The operation principle of a trilayer OLET relies on the vertical charge diffusion process, which includes first the percolation of electrons and holes from the semiconductor layers to the emissive layer and then the confinement of charge carriers in the emissive layer to enable an efficient recombination/light-emission.
2-178	Therefore, it is very crucial to have compatible molecular energetics for these three layers.
2-179	As shown in Figure 8A, the LUMO level of semiconductor 2 (−3.2 eV) matches well with the LUMO level of Alq3 with no charge injection barrier.
2-180	Additionally, the low HOMO level (−6.0 eV) of 2 compared to that of Alq3 (−5.8 eV) creates an intrinsic hole-blocking barrier to prevent any undesired hole transport

2-197	This shows that the excitons are only formed in the emissive Alq3 layer, indicating favorable charge carrier percolation from the semiconductors to the emissive layer.
2-198	The emitted optical powers are found to be comparable in both device configurations (241 nW for OLET-a and 342 nW for OLET-b).
2-199	A difference in the electron mobility and threshold voltage of 2 is observed when it is deposited directly on the PMMA dielectric (OLET-b, μe = 0.23 cm2/(V·s); Vth = +24.9 V) versus on PMMA/DH-4T/Alq3 (OLET-a, μe = 0.01 cm2/(V·s); Vth = +48.4 V).
2-200	This difference becomes more significant for the p-channel semiconductor DH-4T (μh = 0.04 cm2/(V·s) on PMMA vs μh = 2.4 × 10−5 cm2/(V·s) on Alq3).
2-201	These observations are consistent with our previous results on the DFH-4T/Alq3:DCM/DH-4T trilayer system,[44] and it can be ascribed to different surface roughnesses and morphologies of the underneath films (PMMA vs Alq3), which strongly affects the transport properties of the top semiconductor layer.
2-202	Devices with the OLET-a configuration exhibit typical ambipolar characteristics with well-balanced p- and n-channel mobilities, clearly visible “V” shaped transfer plot, and nonlinear increases of ISD at high VSD in the output curves.
2-203	However, it is noteworthy that the differences of gate threshold voltages between the p-channel (Vth = −29.5 V) and the n-channel (Vth = +48.4 V) transport layers are such that the overall ambipolar electrical behavior is still not perfectly balanced.
2-204	The marked ambipolar behavior of devices with the OLET-a configuration leads to steeper electroluminescence increases at lower biases in the output curves compared to the OLET-b configuration.
2-205	This indicates that, although threshold voltages in OLET-b are lower than those of OLET-a, the electroluminescence behavior is mainly dominated by the electrical current of the top transport layer.
2-206	As expected, the external quantum efficiency (EQE) of the OLET devices with the OLET-a configuration (EQE = 0.15%) is higher than that of devices with the OLET-b configuration (EQE = 0.03%).
2-207	Note that for all devices EQEs are calculated from the ratio between the total emitted photons and the flowing charges (OLET drain current) without any device-geometry corrections to avoid any risks of over- estimating the EQE.
2-208	Our results are also consistent with the previous trilayer OLET device configurations, where well-balanced ambipolarity leads to higher EQE devices.
2-209	Addition- ally, bidirectional transfer plots reveal small, comparable hysteresis for both OLET device configurations (Figure 9A,C), indicating minimal density of charge traps.
2-210	In general, charge traps in multilayered organic semiconductor devices may originate from energetic mismatch between different materials at the interface.
2-211	Considering that the semiconductors in this study are optimized with respect to their molecular energy levels, we believe that the present charge traps are more likely to originate at the polymer dielectric−semiconductor interface.
2-212	We also fabricated trilayer OLETs in the OLET-a configuration by using DFH-4T as the top n-channel layer instead of 2.
2-213	These devices showed somewhat better EQEs of up to 0.3% with maximum optical power of 838 nW, which is probably due to enhanced charge transport characteristics of this stack (μh = 0.19 cm2/(V·s); μe = 0.07 cm2/(V·s)) with respect to DH-4T/Alq3/2.
2-214	Although this initial OLET performance of the new thiazole−thiophene semiconductor 2 is somewhat lower than that of DFH-4T, it should be noted that our devices have not yet been optimized in terms of device architecture, fabrication condition, and p-channel/emissive material structures to maximize light outcoupling.
2-215	Previously, through extensive device optimizations, we showed that the EQEs of DFH-4T- based trilayer OLETs can increase up to ∼5%.
2-216	Nevertheless, our results show the compatibility of semiconductor 2 with the
2-217	trilayer OLET configuration and demonstrate its great potential in the integrated photonic field-effect technologies.
3-001	In summary, a series of new regioselectively synthesized thiazole−thiophene co-oligomers, 1−4, has been designed, synthesized, and fully characterized.
3-002	These new co-oligomers are end-functionalized with electron-withdrawing perfluorohex- yl chains to induce efficient electron-transport characteristics.
3-003	The effects of thiazole unit insertion and nitrogen-regiochem- istry (2,2′ versus 5,5′) on the physicochemical and optoelec- tronic properties of the current materials are studied.
3-004	Thiazole units are found to enhance intermolecular cohesions through dipole−dipole and π−π interactions, which results in more effective solid-state packings and substantially higher melting points compared to thiophene analogues.
3-005	The OTFT devices of the current thiazole−thiophene co-oligomers (1−4) exhibit excellent n-channel transport with carrier mobilities (μe) of 0.2−1.3 cm2/(V·s) and Ion/Ioff of 105−107.
3-006	Among the current semiconductors, room temperature deposited thin films of 2 yield very large unoptimized μe of 1.30 cm2/(V·s) with Ion/Ioff of 106−107 , which is one of the highest reported for a room temperature deposited film in the literature.
3-007	The impressive results obtained for semiconductor 2 indicate that 2,2′-bithiazole can be a very practical building block for high-performance n-channel semiconductors, which is in sharp contrast to the previous reports in the literature.
3-008	Additionally, we also show that significant charge transport improvements (Δμe > 100−1000×) occur upon thiazole insertion into a sexithiophene backbone (3 and 4 versus DFH-6T).
3-009	Analysis of the thin-film morphological and microstructural characteristics indicate preferential edge-on molecular orientations relative to the substrate and highly interconnected grain based morphol- ogies for all semiconductors, which ensures an efficient electron transport in the plane of the film benefiting from π−π stacking.
3-010	The results presented here suggest that the thiazole unit is a very promising building block for molecular n-channel semiconductors, and it can significantly enhance the electron transport characteristics of molecular semiconductors.
3-011	We have shown trilayer OLET devices successfully fabricated with semiconductor 2.
3-012	This compound demonstrated high compat- ibility with the trilayer OLET approach, by showing good electron mobility also when used as the top layer of the organic heterostructure.
3-013	This opens the perspective of a potential use of semiconductor 2 in future optoelectronic applications.
3-014	We believe that further design/synthesis studies on thiazole-based n-channel semiconductors must be pursued to enhance the current OTFT device performances, and also to realize high performance via solution processing in air.

4-171	Device Fabrication and Characterization.
4-172	Prime grade p- doped silicon wafers (100) having a 300 nm thermally grown oxide layer (Montco Silicon) were used as device substrates.
4-173	These were rinsed with water, methanol, and acetone before film deposition.
4-174	Next, ∼30 nm of PMMA layer is spin-coated from a polymer solution (PMMA, ∼20 mg mL−1 in ethyl lactate, 9000 rpm).
4-175	The dielectric film was dried at 110 °C overnight in a vacuum oven (D = 25 and 70 °C).
4-176	For TFT device
4-177	fabrication, top-contact electrodes (500 Å) were deposited by evaporating gold (pressure ∼10−6 Torr); channel dimensions were 25/50/100 μm (L) by 2000/1000/500 μm (W).
4-178	The capacitance of the insulator (PMMA (30 nm)/SiO2 (300 nm)) was 10.0 nF cm−2.
4-179	TFT device measurements were carried out in a customized vacuum probe station (pressure ∼ 10−6 Torr) at a scan rate of 300 ms/step.
4-180	Coaxial and/or triaxial shielding was incorporated into Signatone probes to minimize the noise level.
4-181	TFT characterization was performed with a Keithley 6430 subfemtoammeter and a Keithley 2400 source meter, operated by a locally written LabView program and GPIB communication.
4-182	Mobilities (μ) were calculated in the saturation regime by the standard relationship: μsat = (2IDSL)/[WCi(VG − VT)[2]], where IDS is the source−drain saturation current, Ci is the gate dielectric capacitance (per area), VG is the gate voltage, and VT is the threshold voltage.
4-183	The latter can be estimated as the x intercept of the linear section of the plot of VG vs (IDS)1/2.
4-184	Trilayer OLET devices were fabricated by vacuum-depositing the organic layers and the metal contacts on a transparent substrate, composed of 450 nm of PMMA spin-coated on glass/indium tin oxide (ITO) and baked in a vacuum oven at 110 °C (overnight baking).
4-185	The first organic layer deposited on the dielectric is for the field-effect transport of either the p- or n-type charge carriers, while the third layer is devoted to the transport of the charge carriers of opposite sign.
4-186	For p-type, we deposited 15 nm of dihexyl-quaterthiophene (DH4T) at 0.1 Å/s, while for the n-type we deposited 30 nm of semiconductor 2 at 0.1 Å/s.
4-187	The layer in between of the two transport semiconductors is the recombination layer, where emission takes place, and it is made of 30 nm of tris(8-hydroxyquinolinato) aluminum (Alq3) sublimed at 5 Å/s.
4-188	Finally on top of the organic stack, 70 nm gold drain−source electrodes were deposited, forming a 70 μm long (CL) and 12 mm wide (CW) channel.
4-189	The capacitance of the insulator (450 nm of PMMA) was 7.58 nF cm−2.
4-190	OLETs opto-electrical characterization has been carried out with a commercial probe station from Suss connected through triaxial cables to an Agilent B1500A parametric analyzer.
4-191	Finally, the device electroluminescence was measured by using a calibrated photodiode (Hamamatsu S1337-1010BR) placed in contact with the glass substrate.
4-192	Thin films were analyzed by wide-angle X-ray film diffractometry (WAXRD) on a Rikagua ATX-G instrument with Cu Kα radiation and a monochromator using standard θ−2θ techniques.
4-193	AFM images were taken from a JEOL-SPM5200 with a Si cantilever.
4-194	Film thickness was determined by profilometry using a Veeco Dektak 150.
