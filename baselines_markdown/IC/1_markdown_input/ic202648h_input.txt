1-001	■
1-002	Over the past several decades, the field of ruthenium polypyridine chemistry has experienced phenomenal growth.
1-003	The mainstay ligands in this vast family of complexes are the bidentate 2,2′-bipyridine (bpy) and the tridentate 2,2′:6′,2″-terpyridine (tpy).[1]
1-004	These ligands have been elaborated in almost every way imaginable including the replacement of H atoms with other groups, the substitution of other elements for C atoms in the pyridine rings, and the incorporation of aza- aromatic rings other than pyridine.
1-005	Ruthenium(II) complexes of bpy and tpy have fundamental structural and photophysical differences.
1-006	The tris-bidentate complex [Ru(bpy)3]2+ exists as a pair of enantiomers, while the bis-tridentate complex [Ru(tpy)2]2+
1-007	Recently, we prepared a new ligand in this series, 2,9-di(pyrid-
1-008	2′-yl)-1,10-phenanthroline (dpp).[5,6]
1-009	The central phenanthroline
1-010	© 2012 American Chemical Society 3179
1-011	ring restricts the conformational freedom of the ligand and thus prevents it from binding as a bis-bidentate bridging ligand.
1-012	Instead, all four pyridines bind to RuII to form surprisingly stable tetradentate complexes that can accommodate two monodentate ligands in the remaining axial positions (1a−1h).
1-013	One of the interesting properties of these RuII(dpp) complexes is that they all appear to be active water oxidation catalysts, and we are carrying out a thorough investigation of this important feature.
1-014	In our initial reports on these dpp complexes, we prepare them in a “one-pot” synthesis, wherein dpp is treated with [RuCl3·3H2O] in aqueous ethanol (EtOH), followed by the addition of excess monodentate ligand and triethylamine to complete the coordination sphere.
1-015	In this process, some dpp binds as a tridentate, and thus [Ru(dpp) 2]2+ is formed as a byproduct.
1-016	In this report, we isolate and characterize the intermediate trans-[RuII (dpp)Cl2], study its reaction with various ligands, and explore the novel RuII(dpp-N,N′,N″) complexes, where dpp binds to the metal in a tridentate fashion.

2-043	However, the reaction was slow and, using 70 equiv of PPh3 , only 75% conversion was observed after 2 days at reflux.

2-047	However, in the presence of a stronger chelating ligand, such as bpy or tpy, ligand substitution for the weakly bonded distal pyridine on dpp is observed, forcing dpp to bind in a tridentate fashion.

2-068	Electronic Absorption and Emission Properties.
2-069	In our earlier communication, we observed that the metal-to-ligand charge transfer (MLCT) absorbance of complexes 1a−1c (Table 2) could be tuned by the 4 substituent on the axial monodentate pyridines.[5]
2-070	Four absorption maxima were observed in the visible region and shifted to lower energy as the substituent was changed from electron-withdrawing (CF3) to electron-donating (NMe2).
2-071	The low-energy MLCT band at 541 nm for VP complex 1d resembles the 544 nm band found for its 4-methylpyridine analogue (1b).
2-072	Unlike complexes 1a−1d, where a 4-substituted pyridine occupies the axial sites, the CH3CN (1f) and PPh3 (1h) complexes show blue-shifted absorptions at 474 and 488 nm, respectively (Figure 2).

2-119	The heteroleptic complex 3 bearing one uncoordinated pyridine oxidizes at slightly higher potential than 2, which bears two uncoordinated pyridines, but their first reduction potentials are nearly the same.
2-120	For the heteroleptic complexes 3 and 4, the first reduction is dpp-based and the second reduction is attributed to a tpy/bpy-based process.
2-121	As expected, the presence of an anionic ligand stabilizes the higher oxidation state of ruthenium and lowers the oxidation potential by more than 0.4 V. The coordinated chloride, being cis to the pyridyl−phenanthroline plane, also makes the dpp-based reduction more negative.
3-001	The complex trans-[RuII(dpp)Cl2] serves as a convenient reagent for the preparation of a series of trans-Ru(dpp) complexes 1a−1h, where dpp is a planar tetradentate ligand.
3-002	Axial ligands exert significant effects on the absorption and redox properties of the complexes.
3-003	It is of interest to find that trans-[RuII(dpp)Cl2] functions as the source for a tridentate dpp complex of ruthenium.
3-004	The reaction of trans-[RuII(dpp)Cl2] with dpp, tpy, or bpy produces ruthenium(II) complexes (2−4), where one of the distal pyridines on dpp was replaced by the other polydentate ligand.
3-005	The X-ray crystal structure of [Ru(dpp- N,N′,N‴)(bpy)Cl\](PF6)·CH3CN revealed that the uncoordi- nated pyridine experiences π−π interaction with bpy and the uncoordinated N atom is positioned cis to the chloride.
3-006	The uncoordinated pyridine, covalently bound to a tridentate ligand, tends to lower the oxidation potential of the ruthenium(II) complex, slightly influences the reduction potentials, and red- shifts the absorption maxima by a few nanometers.

4-075	X-ray Structure Determination of 4·CH3CN.
4-076	All measurements were made with a Siemens SMART platform diffractometer equipped with a 4K CCD APEX II detector.
4-077	A hemisphere of data (1271 frames at a 6 cm detector distance) was collected using a narrow-frame algorithm with scan widths of 0.30° in ω and an exposure time of 35 s/frame.
4-078	The data were integrated using the Bruker-Nonius SAINT program, with the intensities corrected for Lorentz factor, polarization, air absorption, and absorption due to variation in the path length through the detector faceplate.
4-079	A ψ-scan absorption correction was applied based on the entire data set.
4-080	Redundant reflections were averaged.
4-081	Final cell constants were refined using 8155 reflections having I > 10σ(I), and these, along with other information pertinent to data collection and refinement, are listed in Table S1 in the Supporting Information.
4-082	The Laue symmetry was determined to be mmm, and from the systematic absences noted, the space group was shown unambiguously to be Pbca.
4-083	Both the anion and the CH3CN solvent molecule were found to be massively disordered, having three slightly different orientations.
4-084	This was treated by refinement of ideal rigid bodies at each site, with occupancies estimated based on a comparison of the isotropic displacement parameters involved.
4-085	No attempt was made to locate or refine the disordered solvent H atoms.
