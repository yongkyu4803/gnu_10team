1-001	■
1-002	Over the past decade, cationic iridium(III) complexes have received increasing attention because of their use as luminophoric materials in a wide variety of applications including light-emitting electrochemical cells (LEECs),[1] biolabeling agents,[2] chemosensors,[3] and as photocatalysts for solar fuels[4] and in organic transformations.[5]
1-003	In the great majority of these cases, the luminophore used is a mononuclear heteroleptic complex.
1-004	Far less explored are materials based on discrete oligohomonuclear complexes, most of the examples of which link the metal centers through a polyacene-type bridge.[6]
1-005	In fact, to the best of our knowledge only a single example of a dinuclear cationic complex incorporating a π-conjugated bridging ligand, a 3,8-dipyridyl-4,7-phenanthroline,[6d] has been reported.
1-006	The ligand 2,5-dipyridylpyrazine (2,5-dpp) has had a long
1-007	and storied past as a strong π-acceptor σ-donating bis(chelate), particularly involving Ru(II).[7]
1-008	Indeed as a result of their properties, the use of 2,5-dpp and other related diazine ligands results in more photostable complexes.[8]
1-009	Given our interest in
1-010	the optoelectronic properties of multimetallic cationic iridium- (III) complexes,6m,t we targeted complexes 1 and 2 for study,
1-011	the first examples of iridium(III) complexes incorporating a 2,5-
1-012	dpp ancillary ligand (Chart 1).
1-013	We report here the improved synthesis of 2,5-dpp along with 1 and 2, X-ray structures of the complexes, and their optoelectronic characterization.

3-002	Ligand and Complex Synthesis.
3-003	Bridging 2,5-dipyridyl- pyrazine (2,5-dpp) was obtained efficiently in 55% yield in a single-step through a Negishi cross coupling of 2-bromopyr- idine and 2,5-dibromopyrazine (2,5-diBrpyz), Scheme 1.
3-004	This simple synthesis contrasts will the multistep and low-yielding condensation strategy commonly used in the literature.[7a,22]
3-005	Complexes 1 and 2 were obtained through a one-pot two- step reaction protocol previously used by us[23] wherein the [(ppy)2 Ir(μ-Cl)]2 dimer was not isolated.
3-006	Mononuclear complex 1 was obtained in very good yield when an excess of 2,5-dpp was employed.
3-007	Dinuclear complex 2 was obtained in good yield as a mixture of diastereomers when 2,5-dpp was used as the limiting reagent.
3-008	Complexes 1 and 2 were
3-009	characterized by 1H and 19F NMR, Mp, HPLC and HRMS analyses, and the X-ray structures of each were solved.
3-010	X-ray Structures.
3-011	Complexes 1 and 2 each crystallized from slow diffusion of iPr2 O into an ACN solution of the complex.
3-012	Suitable single crystals for X-ray analysis were obtained for 1 and 2, and their molecular structures are shown in Figure 1 while refinement parameters are available in
3-013	Supporting Information, Table S1.
3-014	Both complexes crystallized within the P1 space group and are triclinic.
3-015	Complex 1 crystallized as dark purple plates as an ACN solvate in the asymmetric unit while 2 crystallized as dark green prisms also as an ACN solvate.
3-016	In each case, the coordination environment of the iridium is that of a distorted octahedron with a mutually trans-configuration of the nitrogen atoms of the two pyridine rings of the ppy ligands.
3-017	Selected bond lengths and bond and torsional angles are collected in Supporting Information, Tables S2−S3 and are similar to those of other heteroleptic iridium complexes such as [Ir(ppy) 2(bpy)]PF6, where bpy = 2,2′-bipyridine.[24]
3-018	In 1, there is a small torsion between the coordinating pyridine and pyrazine rings (interplanar angle of 8.4°) as well as
3-019	a twisting of the noncoordinating pyridine ring of the 2,5-dpp ligand with an interplanar angle of 12.8°).
3-020	Of note, the pyridine nitrogen atom is trans to the noncoordinating nitrogen atom of the pyrazine moiety, similar to that observed[9] for [ZnCl2(2,5-dpp)(DMF)] while in the crystal structure of [Ru(bpy)2(2,5-dpp)\](PF 6)2 the tethered pyridine ring is disordered and these two nitrogen atoms are in some instances disposed in an S-cis conformation.[25]
3-021	This deviation from planarity of the distal pyridine is probably due to the weak intermolecular π-stacking interaction between the noncoordinating pyridine rings in adjacent enantiomeric molecules evidenced by the slip-stack and face-to-face separation between their centroids of 3.806 Å (Supporting Information, Figure S1).
3-022	Both Δ and Λ isomers are present in the lattice.
3-023	Complex 2 is C2-symmetric, and crystallizes as the meso ΔΛ form, with the two iridium atoms separated by 6.996 Å and with each iridium atom located 0.118 Å above and below the mean plane of the 2,5-dpp ligand.
3-024	This stereochemical arrangement in a dinuclear iridium complex has been previously reported.[6d,26] The Ir1···Ir2 distance in 2 places the iridium subunits substantially further apart than the majority of the few other dinuclear and polynuclear iridium crystal structures reported to date.6r,26,27 Exceptionally, recently several crystal structures of dinuclear iridium complexes have been solved wherein the nonbonded Ir···Ir distance ranges from 7.467 Å to 10.007 Å.[6d,f,h,28] As with 1, there are distortions from planarity over the 2,5-dpp in 2 (interplanar angle between each of the pyridines and pyrazine of 8.14°).

3-110	We have performed a combined DFT and TDDFT study to better understand the origin of the optoelectronic properties of 1 and 2.[13a−c,14] We have previously shown that a similar computational methodology accurately predicts both ground and excited state properties for both mononuclear and dinuclear iridium complexes.6m,23 Selected geometric parameters for the optimized ground and excited state geometries of 1 and 2 are reported in Supporting Information, Tables S2−S3.

4-001	■
4-002	We presented the first examples of 2,5-dpp-containing iridium(III) complexes including their solid state structures.
4-003	We showed that the 2,5-dpp ligand promotes a large red-shift in the RT emission spectrum for mononuclear complex 1.
4-004	The RT luminescence of dinuclear analogue 2 was unfortunately completely quenched.
4-005	The deep-red emission and quasi- reversible electrochemical behavior in 1 makes it a promising candidate in red-emitting LEECs for flat panel lighting and visual displays.
