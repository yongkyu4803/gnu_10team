2-095	decrease of the 13CO 2 signal and an appearance of a new signal of relatively weak intensity, attributed to 13 CO at 185.2 ppm (Figure 4b).

2-097	A strong signal attributed to H13CO3− was also observed at 158.3 ppm after irradiation, whereas a signal with a much weaker peak intensity of H13CO3− was observed before irradiation.[9]

3-002	General Procedures.
3-003	UV−vis absorption spectra were measured with a JASCO V-670 or a Photal MCPD-9800 spectrophotometer.
3-004	1H NMR spectra were measured in acetone-d6 using a JEOL AL400 (400 MHz) or AL300 (300 MHz) system.
3-005	Residual protons of acetone-d6 were used as internal standards for the measurements.
3-006	IR spectra were measured with a JASCO FT/IR-610 spectrometer in a dichloro- methane or acetonitrile solution.
3-007	Electrospray ionization mass spectroscopy (ESI-MS) was performed using a Shimadzu LCMS-
3-008	2010A system with acetonitrile as a mobile phase.
3-009	Electrospray ionization time-of-flight mass spectroscopy (ESI-TOFMS) was undertaken with a Waters LCT Premier, with acetonitrile as a mobile phase.
3-010	Emission spectra were measured at 25 °C with a JASCO FP-8600 spectrofluorometer.
3-011	The emission quantum yields were evaluated by emission from [Ru(bpy)3\](PF6)2 (bpy = 2,2′-bipyridine) in degassed acetonitrile (Φem = 0.095).[15]
3-012	Emission lifetimes were obtained using a HORIBA TemPro fluorescence lifetime system with emission monochromator.
3-013	The excitation light source was a NanoLED-560 (559 nm, 1.5 ns).
3-014	The samples were degassed by the freeze−pump−thaw method before emission measurements.
3-015	Emission quenching experiments were performed using Ar-saturated solutions containing a complex and five different concentrations of BIH.
3-016	The redox potentials of the complexes were measured in a DMF solution containing Et4NBF 4 (0.1 M) as a supporting electrolyte by cyclic voltammetric techniques using an ALS/CHI CHI-720Dx electro- chemical analyzer with a glassy-carbon disk working electrode (3 mm diameter), a Ag/AgNO3 (0.01 M) reference electrode, and a Pt counterelectrode.
3-017	The supporting electrolyte was dried in a vacuum at 100 °C for 1 day prior to use.
3-018	The scan rate was 200 mV s−1.

In situ measurements of UV−vis absorption spectra were conducted using a Photal MCPD-9800 spectrophotometer during irradiation to determine the absorbed photon number.

3-109	Inorganic Chemistry Article
4-001	We successfully developed red-light-driven supramolecular photocatalysts composed with an Os(II) tris-diimine complex as a photosensitizer and a Re(I) complex as a catalyst.
4-002	Irradiation of the photocatalysts in the presence of BIH as a reductant using light at λ > 620 nm caused selective reduction of CO2 to CO.
4-003	The phosphine ligands on the Re catalyst unit affected its photocatalytic abilities, and the photocatalyst with P(p-Cl-C 6H4)3 ligands exhibited better photocatalytic activity (ΦCO = 0.12, TONCO = 1138, TOFCO = 3.3 min−1) than that with P(p-F-C6H 4)3 ligands.
4-004	The photocatalytic reactions proceeded via (1) light-absorption by the Os unit, (2) electron transfer from BIH to the excited Os unit, (3) intramolecular electron transfer from OER species of the Os unit to the Re unit, and (4) CO2 reduction on the Re unit giving CO and HCO3−.
