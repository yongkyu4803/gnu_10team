1-001	Today, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is one of the most powerful diagnostic methods in clinical medi- cine.
1-002	The intrinsically low sensitivity of MRI is often improved by the application of contrast agents (CA); about 40% of all MRI examinations are made after administration of a contrast medium.
1-003	These paramag- netic substances reduce the longitudinal (T1) and trans- verse (T2) relaxation times of water protons in the body tissue resulting in an increased image contrast.
1-004	Nowa- days, the majority of contrast agents used in the clinics are Gd3þ complexes that affect primarily the T1 relaxation

1-010	The efficiency of a CA is described by its proton relaxivity (r1) which is defined as the paramagnetic enhancement of the longitudinal water proton relaxation rate referred to 1 mM concentration of the agent.
1-011	The overall relaxivity is deter- mined by microscopic properties of the paramagnetic com- plex and originates from inner- and outer-sphere mecha- nisms.1The inner-sphere relaxivity contribution is due to the presence of water molecule(s) in the first coordination sphere of the paramagnetic ion that exchange with the surrounding water, thus transmitting the paramagnetic effect of the metal ion to the bulk.
1-012	Consequently, the inner-sphere relaxivity is linearly proportional to the number of coordinated water molecules (hydration number, q) and also depends on their exchange rate, kex.
1-013	The rotational motion of the chelate, described by the rotational correlation time, τr, as well as the
1-014	Article
1-015	Inorganic Chemistry, Vol. 49, No. 7, 2010 3225
1-016	relaxation rates of the electron spin of the metal ion, T1,2e, are other important parameters determining relaxivity.
1-017	The outer-sphere relaxivity term arises from the random translation diffusion of water protons in the proximity of the paramag- netic ion and contributes up to 50% to the overall relaxivity for small-molecular-weight chelates.
1-018	While Gd3þcomplexes have been extensively studied in the context of MRI, much less attention has been devoted to paramagnetic transition metals.
1-019	Among them, Mn2þ is the best candidate given its high spin (5 unpaired electrons) and slow electronic relaxation.
1-020	One advantage of Mn2þ com- plexes could be their fast water exchange, which is often in the optimal range to attain high relaxivities, while this factor can be highly limitative for Gd3þ-based agents.
1-021	Manganese is a biogenic element.
1-022	It is a cofactor in several enzymes such as manganese superoxide dismutase[4] or glut- amine synthetase.[5]
1-023	Due to their similar ionic radius, Mn2þ shows biological and chemical similarity to Ca2þ, enabling, e.g., calcium influx detection,6and has a high affinity for Ca2þ and Mg2þ binding sites in proteins[7] and nucleic acids.[8]
1-024	The free Mn2þ aqua ion shows toxicity only at higher concentra- tions.[9]
1-025	Nevertheless, the accumulation of Mn2þ has been
1-026	proven in the brain,[10] which can lead to neurological dis- orders resembling Parkinson’s disease.[11]
1-027	In the current medical practice, only one Mn2þ-based CA, [Mn(DPDP)]4-, is used in liver or cardiac imaging (DPDP6-= N,N0-dipyridoxylethylenediamine-N,N0-diacetate-5,50-bis- (phosphate); Chart 1).[12,13]
1-028	The complex itself has no coordi- nated water molecule, and the observed in vivo relaxation effect comes from free Mn2þ released from the chelate.
1-029	The presence of the ligand is only important to ensure a slow release of Mn2þ which prevents toxicity observed on the direct application of simple manganese(II) salts.
1-030	Indeed, a specific field of application of Mn2þ in MRI is manganese enhanced magnetic resonance imaging (MEMRI), where mostly manganese(II) chloride is used.[14]
1-031	The main draw- back of this method is manganese toxicity for concen- trations providing sufficient contrast; thus, MEMRI is limited to small animal imaging.
1-032	The design of ligands simultaneously satisfying stable Mn2þ complexation and good MRI efficiency remains a coordination che- mistry challenge.[15]
1-033	Indeed, the high complex stability and the presence of water molecule(s) directly coordinated to Mn2þ are often contradictory requirements.
1-034	Conse- quently, relatively few data have been reported on Mn2þ chelates in the context of MRI contrast agent applications.
1-035	The Mn2þ complexes studied so far involve chelates of
1-036	linear polydentate ligands, like EDTA4- 16-19 or DTPA5-derivatives,[20] or of macrocycle-based ligands, NOTA3- 21,22
1-037	4- 18,21,23,24
1-038	and DOTA (Chart 1).
1-039	These complexes have a maximum of one water molecule in the first coordination sphere.
1-040	Creating ligands that form bishydrated Mn2þ com- plexes of sufficient stability is a considerable problem and likely needs macrocyclic structures.
1-041	Due to the lack of crystal-field stabilization caused by the d[5] electron configuration of Mn2þ and a lower positive charge, Mn2þ complexes are, in general, thermodynamically less stable than their transition metal ion or Gd3þ analogues.
1-042	Therefore, their kinetic stability can play a very important role in prevent- ing in vivo toxicity.
1-043	So far, dissociation or transmetalation kinetic data for Mn2þ chelates has not been reported in the literature.
1-044	With the objective of exploring novel ligand systems that simultaneously ensure stable Mn2þ complexation and high efficiency in potential MRI applications, we have considered 15-membered aza-crown ethers.
1-045	Such systems have been exten- sively investigated as superoxide dismutase (SOD) mimics.[25,26]
1-046	We have synthesized pyridine-derivative macrocycles L1 and L2 (Chart 1) and investigated their Mn2þ complexes.
1-047	These ligands are expected to provide a structural motif ensuring balance between complex stability, the presence of coordinated water, and suitable microscopic parameters governing relaxivity.
1-048	The introduction of a pyridine ring into the macrocycle skeleton will rigidify the complex, which can contribute to an increased kinetic stability, as it has been observed for linear[27] or macro- cyclic[25,28] chelators.
1-049	The Mn2þ complexes are expected to be bishydrated, with all five ligand donor atoms lying in the equa- torial plane and two water molecules occupying the axial positions of the pentagonal bipyramid (overall coordination number of seven).
1-050	Such a coordination environment is com- monly found in solid-state structures of the complexes with 15-membered macrocyclic ligands; however, in these cases, some of the apical positions are usually occupied by anion(s).[25,29]
1-051	Nevertheless, bishydration was found in the solid state for the
1-052	Mn2þ complex of a C-substituted analogue of L2,[30] as well as for several complexes with related Schiff bases.[31]
1-053	Recently, the bishydration was confirmed also in solution for Mn2þ com- plexes of pentaaza macrocycles, including L2.[32]
1-054	Importantly, since the two inner-sphere water molecules are situated in the axial positions on two opposite sides of the macrocycle plane, the formation of ternary complexes and, thus, the replacement of these waters by endogenous bidentate anions should not be important.
1-055	Moreover, potentiometric data indicated reasonable stability for MnL2.[25,32]
1-056	Here, we report several crystal structures of the free ligands and of MnL1 and MnL2 complexes.
1-057	In solution, the ligand protonation constants and the thermodynamic stability con- stants of the complexes formed between L1 or L2 and various transition and alkaline earth metal ions were determined by pH- potentiometric titrations.
1-058	In order to gain insight into the disso- ciation kinetics behavior of the MnL2 complex, we studied its transmetalation with Zn2þ.
1-059	To the best of our knowledge, this is the first transmetalation study on a Mn2þ complex.
1-060	Finally, in order to assess the parameters governing proton relaxivity, variable-temperature 17O NMR and1H NMRD measurements were performed on both Mn2þ complexes.
1-061	The formation of ternary complexes in the presence of some endogenous small anions was also investigated by relaxometric titrations.

3-001	Syntheses.
3-002	The crucial precursor for the synthesis of L1, pyridine-2,6-dicarbaldehyde, was prepared from the com- mercially available pyridine-2,6-dicarboxylic acid by its esterification to diethyl ester,[34] subsequent reduction by NaBH4 to 2,6-bis(hydroxymethyl)pyridine,[35] and gentle selective oxidation employing MnO2.[36]
3-003	The last two reac- tion steps were slightly modified with respect to literature procedures (see the Supporting Information) where, in particular, the purification procedures were improved.
3-004	Ligand L1 was previously prepared by the template reaction on Mg2þ.[40]
3-005	To facilitate the procedure and, mainly, to avoid the high-dilution technique, the template reaction on Mn2þ was employed (Scheme 1).
3-006	The reaction conditions were similar to those previously described for the preparation of Mn2þ complexes with a Schiff-base derived from 2,6-diacetylpyridine and different amines or aminoethers.[41]
3-007	After reduction of the Mn2þ Schiff base complex (not isolated) with NaBH 4 and hydrolysis of the hydride excess, the manganese precipitated in the form of Mn(OH)2 and was further oxidized in the air allowing a simple demetalation to obtain the free ligand which was purified by column chromatography.
3-008	The additional
3-009	purification could be done by a high-vacuum distillation (<10-2 mbar).
3-010	The overall yield was 74%.
3-011	The manganese(II) complex of L2 (which could be demetalated as above) has also been prepared directly by the analogous template synthesis but in a rather low yield.[32]
3-012	In our hands, any attempts to reproduce the synthesis failed, giving only nonpurifiable reaction mix- tures.
3-013	Therefore, the general Atkins-Richman procedure was used for the synthesis of the macrocycle.[39]
3-014	The procedure was slightly modified (Supporting Informa- tion).
3-015	The MS spectrum after the deprotection showed only the molecular ion of the pure product, but there were two spots on TLC.
3-016	To ensure the purity of L2, column chromatography was performed, and the final product was obtained in a 42% overall yield as a light-yellow solid (Supporting Information).

3-217	Reaction with Endogenous Anions.
3-218	Small endogenous anions like carbonate, phosphate, citrate, etc. are known to interact with coordinatively unsaturated metal com- plexes by replacing the inner-sphere water molecule(s).
3-219	The formation of such ternary complexes can be strongly limitative for the in vivo relaxivity of the complex, as has been proved for Gd3þ-DO3A derivatives.[67]
3-220	In the case of
3-221	Gd3þ, ternary complex formation is typically observed for bishydrated chelates, provided the two inner-sphere water molecules occupy adjacent positions.
3-222	For MnL1 and MnL2, the two water molecules are in axial positions on the opposite side of the macrocycle plane, which should be unfavorable for anion binding.
3-223	To assess anion binding, proton relaxivities were re- corded at 0.5 MHz for MnL1 and MnL2 solutions in the presence of an increasing amount of phosphate (mixture of HPO42-/H2PO4- at pH 8.0), carbonate (CO32-/HCO 3-), or citrate (cit3-/Hcit2-; Figure 8).
3-224	Carbonate has no effect on either of the complexes.
3-225	For MnL1, the relaxivities increase upon the addition of phosphate or citrate.
3-226	The most evident reasons could be the release of free Mn2þ or aggregate formation.
3-227	The decomposition of the complex and release of free Mn2þ could be promoted by an interaction between the free ligand and phosphate (or citrate), as some polyazamacrocycles behave as effi- cient receptors of phosphate.[68]
3-228	This hypothesis was ex- cluded by the identical NMR spectra of the ligand in the presence and in the absence of phosphate (pH 2-12).
3-229	In addition, on the basis of the stability constants,[46] phos- phate and citrate cannot replace L1 in MnL1.
3-230	We have also recorded the 1H NMRD profile of MnL1 in the presence of 100 equiv of phosphate, which showed in- creased relaxivity all over the measured magnetic field range (see Figure S5, Supporting Information) without the low-field dispersion typical of free Mn2þ.
3-231	The 1H NMRD profile does not show the typical high field peak characteristic of slowly tumbling aggregates either, ex- cluding therefore the formation of large aggregates.

3-244	Our results correspond well to the data published previously for MnL2 in anhydrous MeOH (E1/2 ∼0.8 V)[25] and for [Mn(Me2-15-pyaneN5)]2þ in an aqueous HEPES buffer (HEPES = 4-(2-hydroxyethyl)-1-piperazineethanesulfo- nic acid).[30]
3-245	The values obtained are slightly higher than those for the [Mn(NOTA)]- and [Mn(EDTA)(H 2O)]2-complexes[69] and indicate a good stabilization of the divalent manganese in complexes with macrocycles L1 and L2.
3-246	The formal redox potentials show that both MnL1 and MnL2 complexes have a low tendency to be oxidized by air oxygen.
3-247	This confirms their stability toward oxidation under the experimental conditions used in the kinetic and NMR studies.
4-001	Two pyridine-based macrocycles, L1 and L2, were pre- pared and characterized.
4-002	Potentiometry was used to deter- mine the protonation constants of both ligands as well as the stability constants of metal complexes formed with Mn2þ, endogenously important (Ca2þ, Cu2þ, Zn2þ), and some other metal ions (Co2þ, Cd2þ, Mg2þ).
4-003	The incorporation of the pyridine into the macrocyclic scaffold increases the rigidity of the complexes and leads to a greater thermodynamic stabi- lity.
4-004	The substitution of oxygen with nitrogen in the macro- cycle results in a higher ligand basicity and, consequently, an increased stability of the complexes.
4-005	The solid-state struc- tures of MnL1 and MnL2 confirm the coordination number of 7 for Mn2þ and the presence of Cl- and H2O in axial
4-006	positions.
4-007	Electrochemical measurements yielded a formal redox potential of ∼1 V, which confirms the resistance of the divalent manganese in the complexes toward air-oxidation.
4-008	17O NMR and 1H NMRD measurements were performed on MnL1 and MnL2 to determine the microscopic parameters governing their relaxivity.
4-009	The water exchange rate for MnL1, kex[298] = 0.38 107 s-1, is the lowest value ever measured for a Mn2þ complex and is about 20-times lower than kex[298] = 6.89 107 s-1on the MnL2 complex.
4-010	Our results indicate that water exchange is highly sensitive even to minor changes in the ligand structure, similarly to Gd3þcomplexes.
4-011	Water exchange on MnL2 is fast enough to achieve high relaxivity upon conjugation of the complex to slowly tumbling, large mole- cules.
4-012	The proton/zinc(II)-catalyzed dissociation of MnL1 is too fast to be studied by conventional relaxivity measurements under pH 6.
4-013	For MnL2, the prime importance of the proton-assisted dissociation was evidenced; the Zn2þ catalysis is not important at physiological pH.
4-014	Relaxometric anion-binding studies revealed weak interactions between MnL2 and phos- phate or citrate, leading to the formation of monohydrated species.
4-015	MnL1 seems to form oligomeric species in the presence of phosphate, resulting in an increased relaxivity.
4-016	Carbonate shows no interaction with either of the complexes.
4-017	The inclusion of the pyridine in the macrocycle scaffold induces crucial changes in several aspects.
4-018	Most importantly, it increases the thermodynamic and the kinetic stability of the Mn2þ complexes.
4-019	The presence of the pyridine rigidifies the ligand backbone, which leads to different coordination numbers of the Mn2þ complexes (CN 7 for L2 with respect to 6 for 15-aneN5).
4-020	The relaxivity of the studied complexes is higher than those of typical low-molecular-weight Gd3þ chelates.
4-021	In general, the toxicity of manganese is much lower with respect to that of gadolinium; moreover, the kinetic inertness of these macrocyclic Mn2þ chelates can likely be further improved by rigidifying the macrocycle backbone.
4-022	The complexes presented here and their derivatives are known to be excellent SOD mimics, nearing the efficacy of
4-023	the enzyme itself.
4-024	Considering the concentrations that would be needed in vivo for successful contrast enhancement, the SOD activity would likely prevent any in vivo imaging application.
4-025	Nevertheless, the results obtained on these model compounds give insight into the relaxation behavior of Mn2þ complexes in general with respect to MRI contrast agent applications.
