1-001	The aza-Baylis-Hillman reaction has been recognized as a potentially useful reaction in organic synthesis because it provides a one-step preparation of R-methylene-β-amino esters from readily available R,β-unsaturated esters and imines.[1]
1-002	Recently, asymmetric syntheses of these compounds have been of interest.
1-003	Many methods on the catalytic asymmetric modification of the reaction have been published.2For example, chiral amines derived from quinine derivatives[2b,d-f] or chiral
1-004	2c,g-i,m,q-w
1-005	phosphines have been examined as good catalysts for the asymmetric aza-Baylis-Hillman reaction.
1-006	Most of the reports, however, are usually useful for aromatic imines; the use of aliphatic imines is very limited.
1-007	This is probably due to the instability of aliphatic imines under the reaction conditions.
1-008	To overcome this drawback, stable and readily accessible aliphatic imines are desired.
1-009	Chiral sulfinimines are frequently used as stable imines in organic synthesis.[3]
1-010	The sulfinyl group affords
1-011	sufficient steric bias in the nucleophilic addition to the imino group and is readily removed by acidic treatment.
1-012	Use of chiral sulfinimines in the aza-Baylis-Hillman reaction has been re- ported by Aggarwal, but only moderate levels of diastereomeric selectivity were observed.[4]
1-013	Recently, we have reported an alter- native method, in which we disclosed that a Michael/imino- aldol domino reaction of an acrylate and a chiral sulfinimine followed by a subsequent thermal elimination of sulfoxide gave aza-Baylis-Hillman products.[5]
1-014	This methodology enabled us to prepare aza-Baylis--Hillman adducts from aliphatic imines in high enantiomeric excesses, although the method requires a two-step sequence.
1-015	To the best of our knowledge, there are only a few practical methods to prepare aza-Baylis-Hillman adducts from aliphatic imines.[6]
1-016	To enhance the potential of this meth- odology in organic synthesis, we attempted to prepare chiral 2,5-dihydropyrroles, which are recognized as potential precur- sors for the preparation of aza-multicyclic organic compounds and often seen among natural products such as alkaloids.7With our method, the C2 aliphatic side chain is introduced with sufficient control of chirality.
1-017	We also examined the preparation of (-)-trachelanthamidine in highly optically pure form.

2-002	The ring-closure metathesis reaction of 3 proceeded smoothly to give 2,5-dihydropyr- roles 4 in the presence of Grubbs’ second-generation catalyst.[9]

2-005	Subsequent N-allylation progressed under the weak basic

2-008	We have also examined homoallyl bromide for the alkylation, but the alkylation progressed sluggishly and a trace amount of alkylated products was obtained.
2-009	The ring-closure metathesis reaction of compound 3a was examined.
2-010	We employed the Grubbs’ second-generation catalyst for the reaction.
2-011	The transformation to 2,5-dihy- dropyrrole 4a was achieved smoothly with an isolated yield of 97% (entry 1).
2-012	The optical purity of 4a was examined by HPLC analysis using a Chiral-Pak IC column, which revealed that the enantiomeric excess of 4a was 90%, the same value as the starting material 2a.
2-013	Thus, no significant loss of enantiomeric excess was observed during the three-step transformation.
2-014	Thus, we have successfully provided a new preparation of optically active 2-alkyl-substituted 2,5-dihy- dropyrroles from chiral aza-Baylis-Hillman adducts which are prepared in a few short steps from chiral sulfinimines.
2-015	Having established the methodology, we then examined the synthesis of a pyrrolizidine alkaloid.
2-016	Pyrrolizidine alka- loids are important class of natural products, and due to their unique biological activity, there have been many efforts to synthesize these compounds.[10]
2-017	Among these alkaloids, we chose (-)-trachelanthamidine as a synthetic target.[11]

2-030	HPLC analysis of 8 revealed that the diastereomeric ratio was 97:3.
2-031	Oxidation and N-allylation of compound 8 was carried out in a manner similar to that shown in Scheme 1, and compound 9 was isolated in 78% yield in two steps.
2-032	The ring-closure metath- esis was achieved using the Grubbs’ second-generation cat- alyst to afford chiral 2,5-dihydropyrrole 10 as a solid in 90% yield.
2-033	HPLC examination of compound 10 indicated that the enantiomeric excess of 10 was 94%.
2-034	Recrystallization of 10 enhanced the enantiomeric excess over 99%.
2-035	We next examined the reduction of compound 10 (Scheme 3).[13]
2-036	Hydrogenation of 10 in the presence of a
2-037	10% Pd/C catalyst gave 11 in 88% yield.
2-038	Unfortunately, 11 was isolated as a 1:1 mixture of diastereomers.
2-039	Thus, the hydrogenation of 10 took place in a nonstereoselective manner.
2-040	To improve the stereoselectivity of the reaction, we examined the hydrogenation of the deprotected com- pound 12.
2-041	The removal of the O-protecting group was accomplished quantitatively by acidic treatment to give alcohol 12.
2-042	No pyrrole formation was observed during the reaction.
2-043	Free alcohol 12 was then reduced under standard hydrogenation conditions, giving 13 in 98% yield over two steps.
2-044	Fortunately, the reduction of alcohol 12 progressed stereoselectively, and the diastereomeric ratio of compound 13 was estimated to be 86:14.
2-045	The major isomer was separated by flash chromatography, and it was used for the next reaction.
2-046	The configuration of compound 13 was determined in the following way (Scheme 4).
2-047	The major isomer of compound 13 was an oil and did not yield good crystals for X-ray analysis.
2-048	Then, we tried to isolate the minor isomer of 13 by using the 1:1 mixture of 11.
2-049	Compound 11 was desilylated by treatment with TBAF to give the mixture of isomers of compound 13 in 96% yield.
2-050	Careful flash chromatography of the mixture of 13 enabled the separation of the two diastereomers of 13.
2-051	The hydroxyl group of minor-13 was protected by a TBS group, and minor-11 was prepared in 92% yield.
2-052	Fortunately, minor-11 gave a good crystal that was suitable for X-ray crystallographic analysis.
2-053	The stere- ochemistry of minor-11 was unambiguously determined to be the 2,3-cis configuration.
2-054	Thus, the major-11 and major 13 were determined to be 2,3-trans.
2-055	As the absolute stereogenic center at C2 should be S,[5d] the absolute configuration of major-13 was determined to be 2S,3S.
2-056	According to the reports by Lamaty, the hydrogenation of a 2,5-dihydropyrrole was expected to take place in a trans- selective manner, although a N-SES group was necessary to achieve high selectivity.[14]
2-057	In our case, even starting with an N-tosylpyrrolidine, the high trans-selectivity in the hydro- genation are observed.
2-058	The stereoselectivity for the hydro- genation step was very sensitive to the presence or absence of an O-protecting group in the C2 aliphatic side chain.
2-059	Epi- merization at the C3 position during hydrogenation was less likely.[14]
2-060	Thus, we believe that the trans-selectivity is coming from the hydrogenation process itself.
2-061	We are unsure of precise origin of the trans-selectivity, but the free hydroxyl group might be interacting with Pd/C surface which may cause good steric bias to achieve the trans-selectivity during the hydrogenation of dihydropyrrole.
2-062	To complete the total synthesis of (-)-trchelanthamidine, obtained alcohol 13 was exposed to PDC to give the corre- sponding carboxylic acid;[15] subsequent esterification gave diester 14 in 75% yield over two steps (Scheme 5).
2-063	In the esterification stage, the tert-butyl ester was also hydrolyzed under the acidic conditions and converted to methyl ester in
2-064	14.
2-065	The N-tosyl protecting group was removed on treatment with magnesium metal in MeOH.
2-066	Recently, this method has been widely used for the deprotection of the tosyl group because of the mild conditions.[16]
2-067	In our case, intramolecular cyclization between the deprotected amine and the ester in
2-068	the C2 side chain occurred simultaneously to give pyrolizi- dinone 15 in 76% yield in one step.
2-069	The reduction step has already been reported in Nagao’s total synthesis, and com- pound 15 was also known a compound.[17]
2-070	The specific rotation of 15 was negative and had a value similar to the reported data.[17a] Thus, we have achieved the formal synth- esis of (-)-trachelanthamidine 16.
2-071	In conclusion, we have successfully developed the efficient synthesis of chiral 2-alkyl-substituted 2,5-dihydropyrroles from optically active aza-Baylis-Hillman adducts.
2-072	The con- version requires three steps and was achieved in high yields with simple manipulation.
2-073	No loss of enantiomeric excesses was observed.
2-074	This conversion was also successfully employed in the preparation of an optically active pyrrolidine alkaloid.
2-075	The formal synthesis of (-)-trachelanthamidine was achieved in 11 steps in 22% overall yield from a chiral sulfinimine.
2-076	Dur- ing the synthesis, the high trans-selectivity in the hydrogenation was observed.
2-077	Since optically active 2,5-dihydropyrroles are regarded as potential precursors in many heterocyclic synth- eses, our methodology will provide a convenient route in the synthesis of these natural products.
