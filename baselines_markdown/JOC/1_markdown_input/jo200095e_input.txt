1-001	1,4-Dihydropyridines (1,4-DHPs) are a well-known class of biologically active heterocycles as well as analogues of NADH coenzymes.
1-002	Probably the best known family of 1,4-DHPs are calcium channel blockers, used primarily to treat vascular disorders.[1,2]
1-003	They have also been used as antitumor[3] and antidiabetic agents,[4] HIV protease inhibitors,[5] and drugs in the treatment of a number of other diseases.[6-8]
1-004	1,4-DHPs, being NADH mimics, are used as hydride sources in a wide variety of organocatalytic asymmetric reductions.[9-13] The simplest synthetic route to symmetrical 1,4-DHPs is the classical Hantzsch condensation of aryl aldehydes with ammonia or amines and 2 equiv of β-keto esters developed in 1882.[14]
1-005	However, the methodology is not applicable for the synthesis of chiral, unsymmetrical 1,4-DHPs.
1-006	Although the symmetrical DHP unit is present in many drugs, it is not always a requirement for their activity but rather a consequence of a Hantzsch condensa- tion reaction.
1-007	In many cases, chiral and/or unsymmetrical DHPs have exhibited higher or in the case of enantiomers an opposite pharmacological activity.[15,16]
1-008	The value of the eudismic ratio can be as high as 1000.[17]
1-009	Although enantiomerically pure 1,4-DHPs are of great biolo- gical importance, a general methodology for accessing these types of compounds remains intangible.
1-010	While a number of routes to racemic unsymmetrical DHPs have been described,[18-24] the enantiomerically pure compounds are conventionally produced either by enzymatic[25] or chemical[26-29] resolution of racemates or by the use of chiral auxiliaries.[30]
1-011	Only a few catalytic asymmetric synthesis procedures for chiral 1,4-DHPs have been reported.
1-012	Takemoto et al.[31,32] developed a new Brønsted acid and novel thiourea derivative cocatalyzed method for the addition of β-enamino esters to enals to give access to chiral 1,4-DHPs with moderate to good enantioselectivities.
1-013	However, the ee value of the products did not exceed 80%.

1-015	Gong and co-workers[33] established a one-pot protocol, using chiral phosphoric acid as a catalyst and primary amines, β-dicar- bonyl compounds, and R,β-unsaturated aldehydes as starting materials to yield 1,4-DHPs, with good to excellent enantios- electivities.
1-016	Aromatic enals gave high yields, whereas a β-alkyl- substituted unsaturated aldehyde furnished the product with a low yield and modest selectivity.
1-017	Jørgensen’s aminocatalytic approach[34] was based on the activa- tion of R,β-unsaturated aldehydes by diaryl prolinol silyl ether derivatives, which enabled them to direct the Michael addition of β-dicarbonyl compounds in a highly enantioselective manner.
1-018	A primary amine was added to obtain 1,4-DHPs in a one-pot synthesis with up to 95% ee.
1-019	Although the conversion of the starting materials was almost quantitative, yields of the targets remained moderate (31-60%).
1-020	The last observation was explained by the reversible nature of the initial Michael addition.
1-021	In addition, the simulta- neous presence of the competing secondary amine as a catalyst and the primary amine as a nucleophile decreased the yield of the one-pot reaction.
1-022	The established method was mostly limited to the use of aliphatic unsaturated aldehydes as it was shown that aromatic R,β-unsaturated aldehydes (especially with electron- donating substituents) led to the formation of product with a low yield and selectivity.
