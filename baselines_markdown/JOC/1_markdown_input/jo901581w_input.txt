1-001	The binding of anions with supramolecular hosts is a fascinating and growing field.[1,2]
1-002	Researchers have relied on a variety of methods to investigate structural interactions and binding propensities.
1-003	X-ray crystallography has histori- cally been the method of choice for definitive structural answers because it gives an atomic-level view of host/guest interactions, albeit in the solid state.
1-004	A variety of titration techniques have been used to determine anion-binding con- stants in solution.

2-008	1H NMR Studies.
2-009	The 1H NMR spectra of F- with the cryptands L1-L3 in DMSO-d6 indicate proton coupling with the internally held I = 1/2F-.
2-010	A new doublet amide NH signal at 11.85 ppm appears upon addition of F- to L1 (JHF = 27 Hz) (Figure 1a), and a similar pattern is observed for the thioamide cryptand L3.
2-011	Both the amide and iso- phthaloyl H atoms in L2 couple with F- (JHF = 14 Hz) (Figure 1b).
2-012	Similar coupling of pyrrole NH with bound F- has also been observed by Sessler and Gale.[10,11]
2-013	19F NMR Studies.
2-014	The F- signal shifts upfield from free F- at -96.7 to bound F- at -111.7 ppm upon addition of L1 to a solution of F- in DMSO-d6.
2-015	The new signal appears as a septet (JHF = 27 Hz) due to coupling of F- with the six equivalent amide H atoms (no. of multiplets = 2nI þ 1, where I = nuclear spin and n = number of atoms) (Figure 2a,b).
2-016	These findings provide further confirmation that F- is present as an inclusion complex in solution.
2-017	A similar upfield shift is observed with the thioamide L3.
2-018	However, in addition to the septet (JHF = 25 Hz), the spectrum consists of a series of multiplets (Figure 2d), which are the result of deuterium exchange as described below.
2-019	The F- undergoes a downfield shift for the isophthaloyl- containing cryptand L2 and displays a higher order multiplet pattern (JHF = 14 Hz) (Figure 2c).
2-020	This multiplet was subse- quently determined to be an unresolved dectet that resulted from F- coupling with the six amide and three phenyl H atoms.
2-021	The upfield chemical shifts of the F- signal with L1 and L3 can probably be attributed to the presence of the lone pair of electrons on the pyridine N atoms, which tends to shield the F-; the downfield shift observed for L2 is most likely the result of the ring current of aromatic ring.
2-022	Similar H-bonding coupling of bound F- in 19F NMR has previously been observed with pyrrole NH protons in calix[4]pyrrole-fluoride complexes.[11]
2-023	Deuterium Exchange.

2-026	The series appears slightly upfield of the highest order multiplet, with each signal evenly separated by 0.46, 0.31, and 0.44 ppm for the three hosts L1, L2, and L3, respectively (Figure 3).

2-034	Fluoride Binding Studies.
2-035	All three hosts display high affinity for F-, as anticipated by the strong H-bonding tendency of this ion.
2-036	L1, L2, and L3 all showed slow exchange in DMSO-d6 upon addition of up to 1 equiv of [n-Bu4Nþ][F-] at room temperature.
2-037	Calculations using EQNMR[13] indicate very high binding for L1 at the limits of NMR detectability (K = 8.1 105) and binding slightly more than a factor of 10 lower for L2 (K = 3.3 104) and L3 (K = 3.2 104).
2-038	In general, for simple amido-based mono- cycles, the pyridine analogue displays significantly higher binding than the isophthaloyl derivative, so the superior binding of L1 is as anticipated.
2-039	We and others have attribu- ted this to the preorganization effect of the pyridine N atom on the nearby amide H atoms.[14,15]
2-040	The lessened affinity for L3 for F-, however, is not easily explained.
2-041	In the monocycles, the thioamides show heigh- tened binding for anions as we communicated earlier.[9]
2-042	Indeed, the rationale for exploring thioamides was for their enhanced H-bonding capability that we predicted would result in higher anion affinities.[16]
2-043	The crystal structures of the two free bases, described below, do not shed light on the situation, since both the amido- and thioamido- cryptand are structurally very similar and almost superimposable.
2-044	The only real experimental difference between the two hosts, then, is in the 19 F NMR, and involves the exchange process.
2-045	As seen in Figure 2d, the thioamide L3 undergoes amide H atom exchange when immediately placed in solution, while the exchange process for the corollary amides L1 and L2 is slower, occurring over days.
2-046	It is possible that the enhanced acidity of the thioamide group works against binding, since in a dissociative mechanism for D exchange the host would bear a negative or repelling charge to an incoming F-during any given instance in the rapid exchange process.

3-001	Bicyclic cryptand hosts with differing H-bond donor functional groups (amide vs thioamide) and spacers (pyri- dine vs isophthaloyl) have been synthesized by simple one- or two-step procedures.
3-002	The similar cryptand frameworks have allowed for an evaluation of anion-binding propensities and structural influences.
3-003	The effect of preorganization has also been assessed by comparing L1 hosts containing pyridine (prone to preorganize adjacent amides) and L2 hosts containing isophthaloyl (no preorganization of adjacent amides) spacers.
3-004	Binding studies indicated that the preorganized pyridine host L1 showed superiority in F- binding.
3-005	The encapsulation of the F- in L1 and L2 was confirmed by crystal structures as well as solution 19 F NMR studies.
3-006	Coupling between the F- and the amide H atoms as well as the isophthaloyl H atoms in L2 was observed.
3-007	The strong H-bonding interactions between amides and the bound F- facilitated an interesting deuterium exchange of amide NHs in solution.
3-008	The crystal structures of the two solvated free bases, the amide and thioamide, were virtually superimpo- sable.
3-009	Both showed a stacking of two of the pyridine arms, indicating a preference for this interaction and the resulting conformation.
3-010	In the two F-complexes the crystal structures showed that macrocycles were pulled in to surround the strongly bound F- ion.
3-011	These studies emphasize the utility of F-ion as a probe for both solid-state and solution studies of anion binding.
3-012	They also settle the question of whether the anion remains en- capsulated in solution and reveal some unanticipated chem- istry, the D exchange reaction.
3-013	Finally, these findings add to the growing information base and understanding of the aptitude of amide- and thioamide-based cryptands as hosts for anions.
