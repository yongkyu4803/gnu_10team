1-001	Noble metals are used widely in a range of electrochemical applications, such as electrocatalysis, electrochemical analysis, electrochemical synthesis, etc., owing to their inertness and catalytic properties, generally through chemisorption on the surface.
1-002	The electrocatalytic activity of the metals is of great interest, particularly in the field of fuel cells.
1-003	Pt is the most commonly used cathode catalyst in fuel cells, as it shows high activity toward a sluggish oxygen reduction reaction (ORR).
1-004	Recently, there have been an increasing number of studies searching for more efficient electrocatalysts as a replacement for the expensive and rare Pt.
1-005	In general, Au is regarded as a poor catalyst for ORR due to the weak chemisorption properties caused by the filled d-band.[1-3] To improve the activity of Au, there have been a series of efforts using the following approaches: (i) increasing the surface area using nanoparticles (NPs) 4-7 or nanoporous structures,[6] (ii) employing a single crystalline structure,[4-10] (iii) modification with second metals,[5,10] and (iv) a combination of these methods.
1-006	Some examples of these efforts are as follows.
1-007	In fact, the Au(100) facet[4,5] and Au(100)-like AuNPs 6 were reported to show high catalytic activity toward the ORR.
1-008	The particle-size dependent ORR activity of AuNPs was studied. 7 Single crystal Au(111) exhibited an enhanced positive catalytic effect for the ORR by surface modification with Cu and Ag ad-layers. 8
1-009	The effective ORR via the four-electron-transfer pathway to form water was confirmed at nanoporous Au made by dealloying Ag/Au alloys.[9]
1-010	More recently, the catalytic activity of Au-based bimetallic NPs was reported to be superior to that of each separate metal. 10 The effect of the particle size of AuNPs on the kinetics of the ORR in acidic medium was examined, and greater catalytic activity was confirmed on the smaller AuNPs.[11]
1-011	However, the approaches described here are rather limited
1-012	because they include difficult and complicated fabrication and modification processes.
1-013	The electrocatalytic activity of noble metals can be altered dramatically by surface pretreatments: the first discovery was the enhancement of Pt activity by pulsing between the anodic and cathodic potentials.[12,13]
1-014	With regard to Au, activation with electrochemical,[14-17] thermal,[16-18] and chemical pretreatments[19,20] was reported.
1-015	Indeed, the catalytic effects of activated Au were investigated mostly for oxidation reactions rather than reduction reactions.
1-016	The oxidation of hydrogen,[14] hydroxide,[15] some organic substances (diethyl amine, hydrazine, glyoxal),[17] and hydrogen peroxide[20] has been reported.
1-017	On the other hand, there are relatively few reports on the application of activated Au for reduction reactions, such as nitrobenzene[18] and dichromate.[21]
1-018	However, none of these were for the ORR.
1-019	This paper reports the significantly enhanced catalytic activity of Au, which was activated electrochemically by repetitive potential cycling, toward the ORR as well as the oxidation of H2 O2 under neutral conditions, which are important in the area of biofuel cells and biosensors.

2-016	AuNP Synthesis.
2-017	AuNPs (∼10 nm diameter) were synthe- sized using a similar method to the one previously described.[22]
2-018	First, 20 mL of 1.0 mM HAuCl4 and 2 mL of 38.8 mM sodium citrate aqueous solutions were mixed with magnetic stirring.
2-019	Subsequently, 50 μL of NaBH 4 (0.75%) in water was added slowly to this solution until the solution color changed.
2-020	Magnetic stirring was stopped once the color of the solution turned a deep red color.
2-021	The prepared solution containing AuNPs was stored under refrigerated dark conditions before and after use.
2-022	The sizes of the AuNPs were confirmed by ultraviolet/visible spectroscopy (UV-vis, HP 8453, Hewlett-Packard Wilmington, DE, USA) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM, JEM 1230, JEOL, Japan) operated at 80 kV.
2-023	The field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM) images of the surface of a AuNP-loaded GC electrode were obtained using a JSM-6700 (JEOL, Japan).
2-024	The phase structures were examined by X-ray diffraction (XRD; Siemens D500, with a Cu KR radiation) for bulk Au and AuNPs.
2-025	Fabrication of Au Microwire Electrode.
2-026	Au microwire (∼30-mm long and 12.5 μm diameter) was contacted electrically to a tungsten rod using silver conductive epoxy.
2-027	The Au/tungsten rod was inserted with caution into a ∼5 cm long tapered glass capillary leaving out a ∼10 mm long Au wire from the end of the capillary.
2-028	The surface of the Au microwire electrode was heated electrically with nichrome coiled wire (∼800 °C) for 60 s and allowed to cool to room temperature.
2-029	The heating process also caused the glass capillary in which the Au wire was sealed to melt with a 10 mm length protruding from the glass capillary end.
2-030	Electrodes and Electrochemical Pretreatments.
2-031	Au and glassy carbon (GC) rotating disk electrodes (BASi, A ) 0.071 cm2), which served as the working electrode, were polished to a mirror finish using 0.3 and 0.05 μm alumina slurries.
2-032	After alumina polishing, the electrodes were sonicated in a water bath for 10 min.
2-033	Five microliters of the prepared AuNP colloidal solution was loaded onto the clean surface of a GC electrode and then dried at room temperature.
2-034	The loading/drying processes were repeated five times.
2-035	Subsequently, 10 μL of a chitosan solution (0.01 wt %) was transferred to a AuNP-loaded GC electrode to adhere the AuNPs to the GC substrate electrode.
2-036	The chitosan solution was prepared by dissolving chitosan flakes in acetic acid (0.5%, v/v) followed by filtration through a syringe filter (0.45 μm Millex-HA, Millipore) to remove the coarse undissolved flakes and impurities.
2-037	The Au disk and AuNP- modified GC (AuNP/GC) electrodes were pretreated electro- chemically by potential scanning between -0.2 and +1.0 V (vs saturated calomel reference electrode, SCE) for 200 cycles at a scan rate of 50 mV s-1 with a Pt counter electrode (coiled).
2-038	Electrochemical Measurements.
2-039	RDE voltammetry and cyclic voltammetry (CV) were carried out using a RDE-1 rotor/ Epsilon electrochemical analyzer (Bioanalytical Systems Inc., IN, USA) and a 1000A multipotentiostat (CH Instruments TX, USA), respectively.
2-040	A Pt coil counter electrode and SCE reference electrode were used in all electrochemical measure- ments.
2-041	ORR was examined in an O2-saturated 0.05 M phosphate buffer solution (pH 7.4) prepared with triple-distilled water.
2-042	The solution was purged with O2 gas for 30 min before each measurement, and O 2 gas was flowed over the solution during the experiments.

3-065	Thermal Pretreatment.
3-066	The experimental results show that the catalytic activity of the Au and AuNPs was improved by the electrochemical pretreatment.
3-067	The improved activity might be induced by the formation of a thick Au oxide layer, as evidenced by the CV characterization of the Au catalysts before and after the pretreatment.
3-068	The ORR activity of Au on which the thick Au oxide layer was grown by the thermal pretreatment was examined to confirm the effect of the Au oxide on the ORR.
3-069	Indeed, linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) was carried out with a Au microwire electrode (ca. 10 mm long and 12.5 μm diameter), which was fabricated as described in the Experimental Section, before and after the thermal pretreatment.
3-070	The Au microwire electrode was pretreated thermally by heating electrically in a nichrome coiled wire (ca. 800 °C) for 60 s and allowed to cool to room temperature.
3-071	The thermally pretreated Au microwire electrode was moved from air to an O2-saturated solution until the end of the Au microwire first touched the air/solution interface using a micromanipulator.
3-072	Electrode movement was then stopped, and LSV was run.
3-073	Figure 5 shows the LSV results.
3-074	In the O2-saturated solution, the ORR onset potential of the Au microwire electrode exhibited a great positive shift after the thermal pretreatment (i.e., -0.2 V vs +0.04 V, before vs after the treatment, respectively, a Au wire after the thermal pre- treatment is denoted as thAu).
3-075	A similar positive shift in the ORR onset potential was also observed at the Au disk and AuNPs after the electrochemical pretreatment (repetitive po- tential cycling).
3-076	The observed thermal pretreatment effect on the ORR activity of the Au also shows that the Au oxide layer plays a key role in the ORR.
3-077	The XPS spectra of Au also support the formation of Au oxide on the Au surface after the pretreatment.
3-078	(See the Supporting Information.) Indeed, the Au oxide layer presumably enables O 2 to be chemisorped more favorably than the bare Au surface.

4-001	This study examined the effect of the electrochemical pretreatment by repetitive potential cycling on the electrocata- lytic activity of bulk Au and AuNPs under neutral conditions.
4-002	The RDE voltammetric studies showed that the ORR became more favorable and efficient at both the Au and AuNPs after the pretreatment.
4-003	Indeed, more positive onset potentials, higher current densities, and an increase in the n value involved (nearly to 4) were observed at the tAu and the tAuNPs compared to the corresponding untreated ones.
4-004	In particular, for the bulk Au, a considerable positive shift in the ORR onset potential (∼ 300 mV) and a great increase in the n value (<2 to ∼4) were obtained using a simple electrochemical pretreatment.
4-005	Considering all
4-006	the experimental results, the observed catalytic activity was in the order of tAuNPs > uAuNPs > tAu . uAu.
4-007	The increase in the ORR, catalytic activity by the electrochemical pretreat- ment was presumably due to the formation of thick Au oxide layers.
4-008	The tAu and tAuNPs also exhibited an improved activity for the oxidation of hydrogen peroxide.
