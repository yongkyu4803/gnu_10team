1-001	Lithium ion batteries have been a major component of plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs) and electric vehicles (EVs) as power storage devices due to their characteristics of high energy, high power, and long life.
1-002	The module of the lithium ion battery pack in the vehicle is generally designed in a huge size to achieve a satisfied driving range.
1-003	Thus, high safety of the lithium ion battery cell is greatly desired compared with consumer electronics applications, such as laptops and mobile phones.
1-004	Selection of cathode materials for enhanced battery safety is limited due to the trade-off with cathode capacity.
1-005	LiMn 2O 4 has been commercia- lized as a cathode active material to achieve safer lithium ion batteries due to its thermally stable characteristics.[1,2]
1-006	Although the LiMn2 O4 cell can satisfy the battery requirement for EV and PHEV applications, there is still an increasing demand for cell capacity and safety.
1-007	Another important approach for battery safety is the selection of appropriate electrolytes which act as an ionic conductor between the positive and negative electrodes.
1-008	The electrolyte is one of the most important components in the lithium ion battery, because it dictates the power, cycle, and calendar life of lithium ion cells.
1-009	In addition, the electrolyte will affect the cell capacity through the selection of an appropriate cathode and anode as well as cell safety.
1-010	A typical electrolyte for high-power application is based on nonaqueous organic carbo- nates as electrolyte solvents which are flammable and volatile.
1-011	The commercialized electrolyte for the lithium ion battery is generally composed of LiPF6 as the lithium salt and a mixture of linear/cyclic carbonate as the solvent.
1-012	Ethylene carbonate (EC)

1-014	is indispensible for the graphite anode because of its miraculous ability to enable the lithium ion intercalation process on the graphite anode.
1-015	Previous studies[36] have revealed that the sur- face film formed on the graphite by decomposition of EC acts as a passivation film or solid electrolyte interface (SEI), which pre- vents side reactions between the lithiated graphite and the electrolyte, thus ensuring regular lithium ion insertion into graphite layers.
1-016	It is well accepted that SEI property is critical for lithium ion battery performance.[711]
1-017	To achieve safer lithium ion batteries for automotive applica- tion, the battery group at Argonne National Laboratory has been focusing on silicon-based electrolytes.
1-018	At the first stage of the research, lithium bis(oxalate)borate (LiBOB) was found to be a suitable lithium salt for the silicon-based electrolyte.1216 In addition to acting as a lithium salt, it can also provide a passivation film on the surface of the graphite anode.1721Recently, we published our results on the tris(ethylene glycol)-substituted trimethylsi- lane (1NM3; its chemical structure is shown in Figure 1)LiBOB-based electrolyte, one representative of the silicon-based electrolytes, in LiNi1/3Co1/3Mn 1/3O 2 (NCM)/graphite (MAG) lithium ion chemistry[22] and LiMn2 O 4/graphite lithium ion chemistry.[23]
1-019	Since EC, the major SEI formation component for carbonate-based electrolytes, is absent in the 1NM3LiBOB silane electrolyte, we expect that the SEI characteristics and
1-020	components formed by the silane electrolyte will be different from those formed by EC-based carbonate electrolytes.
1-021	The purpose of this work is to investigate the new SEI and its characteristics formed by 1NM3LiBOB electrolyte using LiM- n2O 4 as the cathode and graphite as the anode.
2-001	LiBOB (99.87%) and LiPF6 (99.98%) were purchased from Novolyte.
2-002	LiPF6 (1.2 M) in 3/7 ethylene carbonate (EC)/ethyl methyl carbonate (EMC) were purchased from Tomiyama Pure Chemical Industries (Japan).
2-003	These salts and electrolyte were used as received.
2-004	The LiMn2 O 4 cathode and MAG anode were provided by EnerDel Inc.
2-005	The cathode active material loading was 11.5 mg cm2 and was balanced with the corre- sponding anode electrode.
2-006	Poly(vinylidene fluoride) (PVdF) was used as the binder for both the cathode and anode, and acetylene black (AB) was used as the conductive additive for the cathode.
2-007	1NM3 solvent was synthesized and analyzed according to our previously reported procedure.221H and 13C NMR and FT-IR spectroscopies were employed to identify the structure of 1NM3 and its purity.
2-008	A total of 2032 coin cells were assembled with LiMn2O 4 as the cathode, MAG graphite as the anode, and microporous polyethylene/polypropylene/polyethylene as the separator.
2-009	The effective electrode area was 1.6 cm2.
2-010	Electrodes were dried in a vacuum oven at 80 °C overnight before cell assembly.
2-011	A two-cycle formation step was applied at room temperature by the C/10 rate by a Maccor cycler.
2-012	Formation was operated in constant currentconstant voltage charge and constant current discharge mode, and the voltage range was controlled between 4.2 and 3.0 V. Performance data were acquired and analyzed by the software associated with the instrument.
2-013	After the formation process, cells in the fully discharged state were carefully disassembled without any electrical shortage.
2-014	Then due to removal of the electrolyte solvent and salt, electro- des from the disassembled cell were dipped in dimethyl carbo- nate (DMC) for 10 min.
2-015	The dipping process was repeated twice with fresh DMC, and the electrodes were dried under vacuum.
2-016	All disassembly operation was performed in an argon-filled glovebox with an O2 level lower than 5 ppm.

2-018	Samples were mounted on SEM stabs in an Ar glovebox and stored in a transfer container.
2-019	The graphite layer structure of the cycled anode electrode was determined on a Rigaku MiniFlex X-ray diffractometer using Cu Kα radiation (30 kV, 15 mA) with a scanning speed of 1.2 deg (2θ) min[1].
2-020	To analyze the chemical component on the surface of the electrode, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analysis was performed.

3-002	Electrochemical Characteristics.
3-003	1NM3 consists of trimethylsilane with an oligo(ethylene glycol) chain and dis- solves most of the lithium salts, such as LiBOB, LiPF6, LiBF 4, and lithium bis[trifluoromethyl)sulfonyl]imide (LiTFSI).
3-004	In pre- vious papers,[13,22,23] we selected LiBOB as the lithium salt due to its solid electrolyte interface forming capability.
3-005	Figure 2 shows the first charge and discharge profiles of the LiMn2 O4/MAG cell with three electrolyte systems, 0.8 M LiBOB1NM3 (1NM3LiBOB), 1.0 M LiPF61NM3 (1NM3LiPF6 ), and 1.2 M LiPF6EC/EMC (3/7) (EC/EMCLiPF6 ) .
3-006	The cell with EC/EMCLiPF 6, the conventional electrolyte, showed a small shoulder around 3.3 V on the charging curve due to EC reduction and SEI film formation.[4,25,26]
3-007	The Coulombic effi- ciency of EC/EMCLiPF6 was 83.4%.
3-008	In contrast, the cell with 1NM3LiBOB electrolyte showed a small plateau at the early charge stage around 2.2 V, and the Coulombic efficiency was much lower (70.2%).
3-009	This plateau was assigned to LiBOB decomposition and SEI film formation.[23,27]
3-010	Nevertheless, the cell with 1NM3LiPF6 electrolyte showed a larger charging capacity and less reversibility compared with the other two electrolytes with a Coulombic efficiency of only 7.7%.
3-011	This indicates that 1NM3LiPF6 electrolyte lacks SEI formation ability.

4-001	The electrode surface film formed by the 1NM3LiBOB electrolyte was investigated for the LiMn2 O4/MAG cell.
4-002	XPS analysis of the electrode surface showed that the SEI formed on the graphite anode is composed of lithium oxalates, lithium borooxalates, and LixBOy.
4-003	Additionally, PVdF binder also parti- cipates in the SEI formation process by releasing fluorine to form LiF in 1NM3LiBOB electrolyte.
4-004	Contrary to our expectation, 1NM3 solvent reduction and participation of anode SEI film formation were not observed in this study.
4-005	On the cathode surface, no Si and B elements were detected from the XPS profiles, indicating that 1NM3LiBOB electrolyte is relatively stable on the cathode side and does not participate in cathode SEI formation.
4-006	While 1NM3LiPF 6 electrolyte lacks the SEI forma- tion function and could not maintain graphite structure during the electrochemical process, the 1NM3LiBOB combination is an excellent electrolyte for graphite-based lithium ion batteries.
