1-001	There has been significant research interest in dye-sensitized solar cells (DSSCs) since the breakthrough work of O’Regan
1-002	and Gratzel in 1991,[1] and most recently DSSCs with efficiency
1-003	as high as 12.7% have been achieved.[2]
1-004	The application of nanostructured materials such as nanoparticles and nanowires
1-005	in DSSCs could lower the fabrication cost, create flexible structures, and provide possibilities to improve light absorption
1-006	and carrier collection in the devices.
1-007	Thus far the majority of published works has been focused on photoanodes based on
1-008	1,3−5 6−8 9−11
1-009	binary metal oxides such as TiO 2, ZnO, and SnO2.
1-010	Among them, TiO2 is the most popular and widely studied one
1-011	due to its high power conversion efficiency and low preparation cost.[1,12]
1-012	However, research has also shown that TiO2 photoanodes could cause photobleaching of the dye molecules
1-013	in the UV region of the solar spectrum,[13,14] highlighting the importance of exploring other promising materials for DSSC applications.
1-014	Compared with simple binary materials, ternary metal oxides such as Zn2SnO 4, Zn2TiO4, SrTiO3, etc. offer several advantages on certain aspects.[15−17] First, ternary metal oxides provide a broader material spectrum for sensitized solar
1-015	cell applications.
1-016	Second, by altering the compositions of the ternary metal oxides, their chemical and electrical properties
1-017	can be easily modified.
1-018	A well-known example is ITOby adjusting the In:Sn ratio, the material’s electrical conductivity, optical transmission, and surface work function can be easily changed.[18]
1-019	The ability to fine-tune these properties is crucial for realizing a better control of carrier transport and collection in sensitized solar structures.[19]
1-020	In addition, ternary metal oxides
1-021	also exhibit better corrosion resistance than many binary oxides,
1-022	which could improve the long-term cell stability under the real world conditions.[15]

1-024	In this work we investigated the synthesis of Zn 2SnO4 nanowires and present for the first time the results of dye-sensitized solar cells fabricated using these nanowires.
1-025	Zn2SnO4 is a ternary metal oxide with a wide band gap (∼3.6 eV)[20] and high electron mobility (10−15 cm3 V−1 s−1).[21]
1-026	It is also an earth-abundant material, which makes it suitable for the fabrication of low-cost DSSCs.[18]
1-027	Nanostructures based on Zn2SnO4 have been reported previously;[22−25] however, to the best of our knowledge, so far only Zn2SnO4 nanoparticles have been utilized as photoanodes in DSSCs.[15,22]
1-028	Compared with nanowires, nanoparticles have larger surface area for dye loading, and the overall power conversion efficiencies of nanoparticle-DSSCs are usually higher than those of nanowire- DSSCs.
1-029	Nonetheless, nanowires still present several attractive features as photoanodes in DSSCs.
1-030	For example, nanowires provide direct transport pathway for photogenerated electrons and hence can result in enhanced charge collection efficiency in the devices.[6]
1-031	Due to the improved diffusion length, longer wires and thus thicker nanowire films can be incorporated into DSSCs, which can potentially improve the quantum efficiency in the long-wavelength region of the solar spectrum.[26]
1-032	In addition, the reduced surface area and fast transport in nanowires could also suppress the back electron transfer reaction that occurs at the photoanode/redox electrolyte solution interface and lead to a higher open-circuit voltage (VOC).[27]

2-001	Nanowire Fabrication.
2-002	Zn2SnO4 nanowires were synthe- sized on silicon substrates by chemical vapor deposition through the vapor−liquid−solid (VLS) growth mechanism.
2-003	A gold film with a thickness of 3 nm was first deposited on a silicon substrate by thermal evaporation as the catalyst.
2-004	Source material that consists of Zn foil (Alfa Aesar, 0.25 mm thick, 99.98%) and SnO powder (Alfa Aesar, 99.9%) was loaded into an alumina boat.
2-005	The alumina boat was placed at the center of a 1 in. single zone tube furnace, and the gold-coated silicon substrate was positioned at a distance of 5 cm downstream from the source boat.
2-006	The tube furnace was evacuated to 10 mtorr and purged by dry N2 gas three times.
2-007	The temperature of tube furnace was then raised to 900 °C with a ramping rate of 30 °C/min.
2-008	Upon elevating the furnace temperature, a 100 sccm N2 gas flow was introduced into the tube furnace, and the pressure was kept at 10 torr throughout the whole growth process.
2-009	An O 2 flow was also injected into the tube at a certain temperature during the ramping process.
2-010	The effects of the source material ratio, O 2 flow rate, and O2 injection temperature on Zn2SnO4 nanowire growth were inspected, and an optimized growth condition was realized, which could repeatedly produce single crystalline Zn2SnO4 nanowires with a high yield.
2-011	Structural Characterization.
2-012	The crystalline structure of the Zn2SnO4 nanowires was characterized by X-ray diffraction (XRD) using a Philips X’Pert MPD diffractometer and a Cu Kα source.
2-013	The morphology was studied by a FEI Quanta 450FEG field emission scanning electron microscope (SEM).
2-014	The transmission electron microscopy (TEM) examination was conducted with a FEI Tecnai F20-UT microscope.
2-015	The simulation of high-resolution TEM (HRTEM) images was conducted by using a multislice code, National Center for Electron Microscopy Simulation Software (NCEMSS).
2-016	DSSCs Fabrication.
2-017	The fabrication of Zn2SnO4 nanowire- DSSCs started with a printing transfer process of the nanowires from the silicon substrate to F:SnO 2 (FTO) coated transparent substrate (MTI Corporation, 2.5 mm thick, 12−14 Ω/sq, 82−84.5% visible transmittance).
2-018	The FTO substrate was first spin- coated with a 100 nm thick Zn2SnO4 precursor film [0.16 M tin 2-ethyhexahoate (Alfa Aesar, 96%), 0.33 M zinc acetate (Alfa Aesar, 98%), and 0.5 M triethanolamine (Alfa Aesar, 98%) in ethanol].
2-019	This Zn2SnO4 precursor film served as an adhesion layer between the transferred nanowires and the FTO substrate and could improve the electrical contact between the two after annealing.
2-020	In a typical printing transfer process, Zn 2SnO4 nanowires on a silicon substrate were faced down and pressed to the precursor-coated FTO substrate under a pressure of 1000 g/cm[2], which was maintained for 1 min before release.
2-021	This approach could reproducibly create nanowire thin films with a thickness of about 20 μm.
2-022	The FTO substrate with nanowires was then annealed at 500 °C for 5 h to transform the precursor into a Zn2SnO 4 film.
2-023	To sensitize the Zn2SnO4
2-024	nanowires, a 0.05 mM (Bu 4N)2Ru(dcbpyH)2(NCS)2 (N719 dye) in dry ethanol solution was used, and the sensitization time ranged from 15 min to 24 h.
2-025	After dye loading, the nanowires were rinsed in ethanol and blow dried by N2 .
2-026	Another FTO substrate coated with 20 nm Pt was used as the counter electrode and was bonded together with the nanowire/ FTO substrate through a hot-melt spacer (Bynel, Dupont, 75 μm).
2-027	Liquid electrolyte (50 mM I2, 0.5 M LiI, and 0.5 M 4-terbutylpyridine in 3-methoxypropionitrile) was injected into the capsulated space through predrilled holes.
2-028	Characterization of device performance was carried out immediately after the fabrication.
2-029	The synthesis of Zn2SnO4 nanoparticles and fabrication of Zn2SnO4 nanoparticle-DSSCs followed previ- ously reported procedures.[15,22]
2-030	The thickness of the Zn2SnO4 nanoparticle film was controlled to be 20 μm, the same as the Zn2SnO4 nanowire film.
2-031	J−V, IPCE, and EIS Measurements.
2-032	Current density− voltage (J−V) curves were acquired by a source-measurement unit in the dark and under a simulated sunlight (100 mW/cm[2], calibrated by a KG-5 filtered silicon photodiode) using a setup with a xenon lamp.
2-033	Incident-photon-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) data were obtained with a monochromatic light source that consists of a 50 W tungsten halogen lamp and a monochromator.
2-034	The output of the monochromator was modulated by a chopper, and the photocurrent was measured using a lock-in amplifier (Stanford Research SR830).
2-035	Two- electrode mode was used to obtain the Tafel plots of the DSSCs.
2-036	EIS measurement was performed using an Ivium CompactStat potentiostat.
2-037	The measurement followed the procedure described in ref 28, and the spectra were obtained at a bias of −0.6 V with 10 mV modulation from 0.1 Hz to 1 MHz.
2-038	All measurements were carried out in the dark.
3-001	Research efforts have been previously made to fabricate Zn 2SnO4 nanowires by chemical vapor deposition using different source materials.[24,25,29]
3-002	However, these methods generally require strict parameter windows that are difficult to reproduce.
3-003	Unlike the growth of binary oxide nanowires, introducing a third element into the synthesis of ternary nanostructures may result in multiple phases in the final products.
3-004	For example, in the VLS growth of Zn2 SnO4 nanowires, other phases including ZnO and SnO 2 may also present.
3-005	The ZnO phase can be easily removed by acid etching due to the instability of ZnO in acidic solutions.[30]
3-006	However, it is very difficult to eliminate the SnO2 phase using similar chemical methods.
3-007	Furthermore, previous studies have shown that the conduction band of SnO2 is lower than those of conventional solar cell anode materials (TiO 2 or ZnO), which causes a higher recombination rate of the injected electrons with the electrolytes.[10,11]
3-008	Therefore, proper synthesis con- ditions should be developed to avoid the growth of the SnO2 phase in Zn2 SnO4 nanowire fabrication.
3-009	In this research, a series of experiments were carried out to investigate the effects of the ratio of source material, oxygen flow rate, and oxygen injection temperature on the phases of the fabricated nanowires.
3-010	Figure 1a shows the XRD characterization data of the nanowires synthesized with different Zn:Sn ratio but same oxygen flow rate (3 sccm) and oxygen injection temperature (800 °C).
3-011	For the Zn-deficient growth (Zn:Sn = 1:1), only a small amount of nanowires including Zn 2SnO4 as well as unwanted phases such as SnO2 and Sn were obtained, which made the purification process very difficult.
3-012	For the growth with
3-013	a Zn:Sn ratio of 2:1, the majority of the nanowires were Zn2SnO4 and ZnO; however, the SnO2 phase still presented.Only in Zn-abundant growth with a Zn:Sn ratio of 3:1 or 4:1 could the SnO2 phase be completely eliminated, and only Zn2SnO4 and ZnO nanowires were obtained in the final product.
3-014	The molar ratio of Zn2SnO4 to ZnO was roughly 3:1 in such a growth, and the yield of the Zn2SnO4 nanowires could be further increased if the Zn content in the source material was higher.
3-015	The ZnO phase could be easily removed by soaking the nanowires in a 0.01% hydrogen chloride water solution, and the XRD data after the etching is also presented in Figure 1a, showing that only pure Zn2SnO4 nanowires were obtained after this etching process.
3-016	Similar studies were also carried out for oxygen flow rate and injection temperature, and the optimized growth parameters for Zn2SnO4 nanowires were determined to be Zn:Sn ratio of 3:1, oxygen flow rate of 3 sccm, and oxygen injection temperature of 800 °C. 4-001	In this study we carried out the synthesis of Zn2SnO4 nanowires and fabricated DSSCs based on these nanostruc- tures.
4-002	Optimized conditions for a reproducible synthesis of Zn2SnO4 nanowires were achieved, and the effect of dye adsorption on cell performance was investigated.
4-003	A more than 0.1 V improvement on open-circuit voltage was constantly observed when Zn2SnO4 nanowires were used as the photoanode instead of Zn2SnO4 nanoparticles.
4-004	The higher open-circuit voltage of Zn2SnO4 nanowire-DSSCs could be attributed to a suppressed back electron transfer process in the devices.
4-005	A key factor that limits the performance of nanowire-DSSCs is the smaller surface area for dye loading.
4-006	However, as our research has shown, this disadvantage could in fact result in an improved open-circuit voltage, which could benefit device application under certain special conditions.
4-007	Nanowire-based photoanodes could also provide an important test platform for the investigation of selected fundamental issues in sensitized solar cells, which could generate additional insight on the understanding of carrier generation and collection processes in such structures.
