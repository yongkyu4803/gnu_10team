1-001	Hematite (α-Fe2O3) has emerged as a promising photocatalyst
1-002	for solar water splitting due to its favorable optical band gap (∼2.2 eV), excellent chemical stability in a wide pH range (pH
1-003	> 3), earth abundance, and low cost.[1−10] The band gap gives a theoretical upper limit of solar-to-hydrogen efficiency of 16.8%
1-004	and a photocurrent of 12.6 mA cm−2 under the standard AM 1.5G 1 sun illumination.[4]
1-005	Therefore, enormous efforts have been focused on improving the performance of α-Fe2O3 photoanodes in photoelectrochemical (PEC) water splitting
1-006	by employing various strategies, such as nanostructure engineering via different synthesis methods, doping, or forming
1-007	a heterojunction.[11]
1-008	The preparation of α-Fe2O3 nanowires,[6,12] nanotubes,[13] nanopetals,[11] nanonets,[14] and dendritic nano- cauliflowers[15] are examples of successful attempts that achieved improvement in the light harvesting and charge carrier separation to enhance the PEC performance.
1-009	Several physical
1-010	and chemical methods have been developed to obtain such nanostructures of α-Fe2O 3.
1-011	Among these, pulse reverse electrodeposition (PRED)[11] has been relatively less employed despite having several potential advantages as it serves a potential strategy to tailor both the chemical composition and
1-012	the structure of materials including metallic films and alloys.
1-013	It perturbs the electrocrystallization process to change the
1-014	© 2015 American Chemical Society 5281
1-015	morphology, texture, and properties of the electrodeposits.
1-016	In PRED, a periodic waveform consisting of a cathodic pulse and anodic pulse is applied to deposit the desired material.
1-017	The mechanism of PRED is simply based on the adsorption or desorption phenomena at the electrode−electrolyte interface.
1-018	The nucleation and growth of the deposited material depends on the adsorption−desorption rate of the species in the electrolyte, controlled by PRED.
1-019	Although a simple method, PRED requires careful attention for optimization of the parameters such as cathodic current density, anodic current density, duty cycle, pulse period, and the deposition time.
1-020	It can be employed to obtain nanostructures with greater control over the grain size and hardness through variation of such process parameters.
1-021	The schematic of potential pulse reverse waveform for electrodeposition of iron oxide is shown in Scheme 1.
1-022	The use of PRED for synthesizing α-Fe2O 3 electrodes is rather a new approach, and the influence of the PRED process parameters for the performance improvement has not yet been investigated.

2-001	2.1.
2-002	Materials Synthesis.
2-003	Pulse reverse electrodeposition was carried out in a two-electrode configuration cell.
2-004	Trans- parent conducting glass (fluorine doped tin oxide, FTO, 10−15 Ω cm−1) substrate was used as a working electrode, and convoluted platinum wire as a counter electrode.
2-005	The distance between the two electrodes was ∼1 cm.
2-006	Prior to deposition of iron oxide films, FTO substrates (1 cm × 2.5 cm) were successively cleaned by ultrasonic degreasing in acetone, ethanol, and deionized water (each for 10 min) then dried using a nitrogen stream.
2-007	The electrolyte consisted of 6 g of ferrous sulfate, 0.15 g of ascorbic acid, 0.05 g of amidosulfonic acid, and 1.5 g of boric acid in 0.1 L of deionized water (pH = 5.71).
2-008	The pH of the resulting electrolyte solution was 2.6.
2-009	The chemicals were purchased from Alfa Aesar and Kanto Chemicals and were used without further treatment.
2-010	Potential control was provided by means of a function generator.
2-011	The PRED of Fe2O3 films was carried out by keeping the amplitude (peak-to-peak voltage) of the square-wave pulse fixed at 10 V (−6/+4 V).
2-012	The other deposition parameters such as duty cycle (20%), pulse period (frequency), and the total deposition time were varied systematically.
2-013	At least three electrodes of each condition were prepared.
2-014	As-grown films were dark grayish in
2-015	color and reflected the light.
2-016	Films were rinsed several times in deionized water and dried using nitrogen stream.
2-017	The two-step annealing method was followed to obtain highly photoactive photoanodes.[16]
2-018	The as-grown films were heated to 550 °C at the rate of 5 °C min−1 and baked there for 4 h.
2-019	Finally, the films were annealed at 800 °C briefly for 15 min and subsequently transferred to a forced-convection oven preheated at 100 °C for 10 min to avoid breaking of the electrodes and the supporting ceramic crucibles.
2-020	The sintered films appeared reddish or dark brown in color, depending on the film thickness.
2-021	2.2.
2-022	PEC Measurements of α-Fe2O3 films.
2-023	The photo- activity of the synthesized α-Fe2 O3 photoanodes was measured in a PEC cell consisting of a three-arm glass compartment with a circular quartz window for light illumination.
2-024	The PEC cell comprised Fe2O3/FTO photoanode as the working electrode, Pt wire as the counter electrode, Ag/AgCl (saturated with KCl) as the reference electrode, and 1 M NaOH as an electrolyte.
2-025	All potentials were measured relative to the Ag/AgCl electrode and were converted to the reversible hydrogen electrode (RHE) scale using the Nernst eq 1.[15]
2-026	VRHE = VAg/AgCl + 0.059pH + V°Ag/AgCl (1)
2-027	where VRHE was the converted potential vs RHE, VAg/AgCl° = 0.1976 V at 25 °C, and VAg/AgCl was the experimental potential against the Ag/AgCl electrode.
2-028	The simulated light illumination of 1 sun (100 mW cm−2) was provided using a solar simulator (Abet Technologies).
2-029	The photocurrent−voltage measurement, electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS), and Mott−Schottky studies were performed using a portable potentiostat (Ivium, Netherland) equipped with an electrochemical interface and impedance analyzer facility.
2-030	The EIS study was performed under the 1 sun illumination at 0.235 V vs Ag/AgCl (i.e., 1.23 V RHE).
2-031	Mott−Schottky measurement was performed under dark condition with a dc applied potential window of −0.6 to 0.4 V vs Ag/AgCl at the ac frequency of 0.5 kHz.
2-032	The amplitude of the ac potential was 10 mV for both EIS and Mott−Schottky measurements.
2-033	The experimental EIS data were validated using the Kramers−Kronig transform test and then fitted to a suitable equivalent circuit model using the ZView (Scribner Associates Inc.) program.

2-058	The absorbance spectra were recorded with a baseline correction using FTO substrate as a reference.
3-001	3.1.
3-002	Optimization of PRED Process Parameters.
3-003	The optimization of the process parameters for synthesis of α-Fe2O3 photoanodes was carried out based on their PEC output under 1 sun illumination.
3-004	Three samples of α-Fe2 O3 were prepared under identical conditions for each process parameter.
3-005	The similar sets of samples were also prepared on three different days to check reproducibility for PEC performance.
3-006	Hence, presented data are the averaged values from the measurement of three independently prepared series of thin film hematite electrodes, with error bars representing standard deviation.
3-007	Initially, deposition parameters such as pulse voltage (−6/+4 V), pulse duty cycle (20%), and film deposition time (30 s) were fixed with variation of only the pulse period as 0.001, 0.01,
3-008	0.1, 1, 10, 100, 1000, and 5000 ms.
3-009	Figure 1a shows the current density−voltage (J−V) curves of α-Fe2 O3 photoanodes
3-010	prepared by two-step annealing process with the different pulse periods.
3-011	The optimum photocurrent density (Jph) of 474 μA cm−2 at 1.23 VRHE was observed for the pulse period of 10 ms.
3-012	Thus, 10 ms was chosen as the optimized value of pulse period and was kept constant during further experiments for optimization of the process parameters.
3-013	The variation of Jph measured at 1.23 VRHE for different pulse periods is shown in Figure 1b.
3-014	The α-Fe2O 3 photoanode synthesized with deposition time of 30 s and annealed at low temperature (550 °C for 4 h) also demonstrated photoactivity with Jph of 116 μA cm−2 at 1.23 V RHE (Figure S1, Supporting Information).
3-015	Next, the pulse voltage (−6/+4 V), pulse period (10 ms), and film deposition time (30 s) were kept constant while the duty cycle was varied.
3-016	The duty cycle variation was limited because the films were inhomogeneous, the film quality was poor, and the resulting photocurrent was also poor except for the 20% duty cycle.
3-017	The α-Fe2O3 has a short hole diffusion length (2−4 nm) and a light penetration depth (1/α) of around 118 nm at the wavelength of 550 nm.
3-018	To ensure optimum absorption (>90%) of the incident light, the film thickness must be greater than 2.3 times the value of 1/α.[18]
3-019	The films should have an enough thickness that would offer sufficient amounts of light absorption, but not too thick that would hold electrons too long in the film and expose them to charge recombination.
3-020	Hence, the film thickness effect on photocurrent response was studied by varying the PRED time.
3-021	The pulse period (10 ms), the duty cycle (20%), and the pulse voltage (−6/+4 V) were kept constant during PRED time variation.
3-022	The thinner films of α-Fe2O 3 (with PRED time 2O3 films prepared by PRED for the deposition times of 5, 10, and 20 s.
3-023	The photocurrent measured at 1.23 VRHE for 5 s deposition time was ∼300 μA cm−2, which is slightly lower than that for the 30 s deposition time.
3-024	However, the photocurrent of thinner films is significantly higher (in a few cases comparable) than that (∼50 μA cm−2 at 1.23 VRHE) observed for the similar planar hematite electrodes prepared by other methods such as atomic layer deposition,[22,24] electrodeposition,[21] spray pyrol- ysis,[23] and sputtering.[25]
3-025	The onset potential for 550 °C-
3-026	annealed α-Fe2O3 photoanode (30 s deposition time) was observed to be more cathodic (Figure S1).
3-027	However, after second annealing at 800 °C, it shifted back toward the anodic region while enhancing the photocurrent considerably.
3-028	The reason for the shift in onset is yet unknown and needs further detailed study.
3-029	Recently, it was shown that increased oxygen vacancies owing to annealing at 500 °C in an argon atmosphere resulted in more cathodic shift in onset potential of undoped hematite.[26]
3-030	Considering the higher photocurrent observed for 30 s deposition time, we varied the deposition time further to achieve higher photocurrent.
3-031	Since it is a general trend that thicker films involving nanostructured morphologies result in better light absorption while better charge collection is obtained on thinner films, we directed the thickness variation study in order to achieve trade-off between optimum light absorption and charge collection in α-Fe2O3.
3-032	The deposition time was varied as 30, 60, 90, 120, 180, 240, and 300 s.
3-033	The color of the annealed films varied from red to dark brown.
3-034	The iron oxide film deposited at 300 s was dark brown in color and opaque.
3-035	Figures 2a and 2b show the J−V curves and Jph
3-036	variation measured at 1.23 VRHE for α-Fe2 O3 photoanodes prepared with different deposition times, respectively.
3-037	An optimum Jph of 504 μA cm−2 at 1.23 VRHE was noted for 45 s deposition time, and the Jph gradually waned to 34 μA cm−2 with increasing deposition time.
3-038	The increase in deposition time brings about a proportional increase in the α-Fe 2O3 film thickness, and too thick a film suffers from efficiency loss by charge recombination.
3-039	Another interesting finding from our study as seen from Figures 1 and 2 is that the onset potential of photocurrent for α-Fe 2O3 photoanodes is ∼0.75−0.85 VRHE.
3-040	The photocurrent onset potential in the present study was determined from the intersection of the dark current and the line tangent to the inflection point of the early photocurrent rise.[27]
3-041	The α-Fe2O3 photoanode prepared at 45 s deposition time showed onset potential of as low as 0.75 VRHE.
3-042	Such a significant cathodic shift of ∼0.1−0.2 V relative to a typical onset photocurrent potential (∼0.9−1 VRHE)[3,15,28] suggests that water oxidation reactions can accelerate at lower bias.
3-043	The shift of onset potential for the water oxidation reaction results from the energy that is absorbed in semiconductor electrodes by photoexcitation to increase the concentration of interfacial holes in the valence band.
3-044	The reason for cathodic shift in onset
3-045	potential could be attributed to surface passivation effect owing to the decrease in charge transfer resistance from surface states.[29]
3-046	The onset potential of a pure Fe2O3 photoanode can be lowered through passivating the surface states by means of aging the photoanodes in saturated NaCl solution.[30]
3-047	However, the α-Fe2O3 photoanodes in the present study were fabricated by PRED without inducing any oxygen evolution reaction catalyst or passivation layer.
3-048	In this context, we believe that the synthesis conditions of PRED method help passivate the surface states.
3-049	However, it necessitates further study to assert these findings.

3-150	Growth Mechanism.
3-151	In general, the growth mechanism of materials by the electrodeposition method is quite a complex process due to the possible influence of many different parameters such as deposit−substrate interactions, structure and structural orientation of the substrate, current density, electrode potential, electrolyte composition, mass transport, temperature, pH, etc.
3-152	Like any other electrochemical synthesis method, PRED controls the electrocrystallization via two fundamental electrokinetic phenomena: nucleation and growth of crystal grains.
3-153	In this work, we studied the growth rate and morphology of prepared iron oxide films with respect to conditions of the pulse reverse electrodeposition synthesis and the high temperature annealing treatment of the electrodes.
3-154	In the present study, PRED involved the application of cathodic and anodic pulses switched at −6 and +4 V over a short duration by varying the pulse polarization time from 0.001 to 1 ms.
3-155	In PRED, uniform deposition is induced by partial dissolution of the deposited species during a slight anodic polarization pulse, Ea, after interruption of the cathodic deposition pulse, Ec (see Scheme 2).
3-156	During the reduction stage of PRED, the Fe ions solvated in the electrolyte arrive at the surface of the electrode, promoting a diffusion motion, a stimulated migration, or even a convective motion depending on the experimental conditions.
3-157	As soon as the cathodic pulse is applied to the electrode (FTO substrate), the reduction process starts.
3-158	The reduced iron species are then adsorbed and migrate to an energetically favorable site at a brisk rate.
3-159	In potential controlled experiments like in this study, the continuity of this mechanism over the entire electrode surface leads to the formation of a density of nuclei and the establishment of a macroscopic electrical current flowing through the electro- chemical cell.
3-160	The current can be directly related to the formation of the deposits.
3-161	Because the applied cathodic pulse potential is more negative than both the potentials of iron deposition and hydrogen ion reduction, the deposition of iron species and hydrogen evolution takes place simultaneously.
3-162	Moreover, oxygen evolution and bubble formation also occur at the counter electrode.
3-163	The evolved hydrogen gas molecules tend to form bubbles on the electrode surface.
3-164	These bubbles, after reaching some critical size, detach from the electrode and disappear into the electrolyte/air interface.
3-165	Hence, PRED replenishes the diffusion layer and allows for the selective dissolution of electrode protrusions.

4-001	High-quality hematite (α-Fe 2O3) photoanodes with nano- structured morphology were successfully prepared by a facile pulse reverse electrodeposition (PRED) method.
4-002	The process parameters were optimized via photoelectrochemical perform- ance of the photoanodes under 1 sun illumination.
4-003	The morphology of the α-Fe2O 3 prepared by PRED was affected by both the process parameters and the high-temperature annealing.
4-004	The films were nonporous, granular, and nano- crystalline.
4-005	Optimum photocurrent performance was noted for the α-Fe2O3 electrodes prepared with the duty cycle of 20%, pulse period of 10 ms, and deposition time of 45 s (200 nm film thickness).
4-006	The enhancement in photocurrent density was attributed to an increase in the charge carrier density, as estimated from Mott−Schottky analysis.
4-007	Moreover, synthesis conditions of PRED method help in passivating the surface states, thereby shifting the onset of photocurrent in order to accelerate the water oxidation kinetics.
4-008	This is the first systematic study of α-Fe2O 3 electrodes prepared using PRED with regard to optimization of the process parameters and their application in photoelectrochemical water splitting.
