1-001	Photocatalytic H2 production with heterogeneous photocata- lysts has received much attention due to its attractive potential of supplying H2 utilizing solar energy.[1-5] For the purpose of producing H2 with high efficiency, much effort has been devoted to the exploration of novel semiconductor catalysts or new photocatalytic reaction systems.[6-17] Up to now, a lot of photocatalysts, including (oxy)nitrides, such as TaON,[6] Ta3N5,[7] Y Ta O5N2,[8] (Ga Zn )(N O ),[9] and (Zn Ge)(N O ),[10] and
1-002	2 2 1-x x 1-x x 1+x 2 x
1-003	(oxy)sulfides, such as (AgIn)xZn2(1-x)S2,[11] ZnIn2 S4,[12] and Sm2Ti2S2O5,[13] have been synthesized and demonstrated to be able to produce H2 under visible light irradiation.
1-004	In addition to the semiconductor materials, surface modification of photo- catalysts with cocatalysts is also crucial for photocatalytic H2 production reactions.
1-005	Cocatalysts can offer the low activation potentials for H2 or O2 evolution and act as active sites for H2 or O2 formation.[18-25] Moreover, cocatalysts could also suppress the recombination of photogenerated charges by the efficient separation of charges on different sites.
1-006	However, for most photocatalysts reported so far, mainly noble metals or their oxides are used as the cocatalysts,[26-28] while little work was done on using inexpensive cocatalysts as the substitutes for noble metals.
1-007	In our recent work, MoS2 was found to be an efficient cocatalyst for CdS in the photocatalytic H2 production reac- tions,[29] while factors influencing the photocatalytic activity of MoS2/CdS and the reasons for the enhanced activity of MoS2/CdS are not clear yet.
1-008	In the present work, we focused on these two aspects and found that the rate of H2 evolution on MoS2/CdS was influenced by the chemical state of the Mo species loaded on CdS, the junctions formed between CdS and MoS2, and the surface area and the crystallinity of the catalyst.
1-009	Moreover, we proved that the excellent H2 activation property of MoS2, together with the junctions formed between MoS2 and
1-010	CdS, is a most important factor responsible for the high activity of MoS2/CdS.

2-002	Preparation of Catalysts.
2-003	The CdS powder was ob- tained commercially, and (NH4 )2MoS4 was prepared according to the literature.[30]
2-004	The preparation procedure of the catalyst is shown in Scheme 1.
2-005	CdS was impregnated with ammonia solution containing different amounts of (NH4)2MoS4 and then dried with a water bath.
2-006	The resulting (NH4)2MoS4/CdS precur- sor powder was calcined at temperatures from 423 to 773 K for 2 h in H2S to obtain the MoS2/CdS catalyst.
2-007	In most cases, commercial CdS was directly used as the substrate to be loaded with MoS 2, and the resulting sample was denoted as MoS2/CdS.
2-008	In some cases, CdS was first calcined at 773 K for 5 h in H2S, and the resulting CdS, which is denoted as CdS(HT), was used as the substrate.
2-009	The corresponding catalyst was denoted as MoS 2/CdS(HT).
2-010	MoS2 powder was obtained by calcining (NH4)2 MoS4 at temperatures from 573 to 773 K in H2S for 2 h.

2-029	2.3.
2-030	Photocatalytic Reactions.
2-031	The photocatalytic reactions were carried out in a Pyrex reaction cell connected to a closed gas circulation and evacuation system.
2-032	A 0.1 g portion of catalyst was suspended in 200 mL of aqueous solution containing 20 mL of different organic reagents as sacrificial electron donors.
2-033	Pt was loaded on CdS by a photoreduction method using H2PtCl6 aqueous solution.
2-034	The suspension was then thoroughly degassed and irradiated by a Xe lamp (300 W) equipped with an optical cutoff filter (λ > 420 nm) to eliminate ultraviolet light and a water filter to remove infrared light.
2-035	The temperature of the reactant solution was maintained at 283 ( 5 K by a flow of cooling water during the reaction.
2-036	The amount of H2 produced was analyzed using an online gas chromatography.
2-037	The activities of different catalysts were compared by the average rate of H2 evolution in the first 5 h.
2-038	The apparent quantum efficiency (Φ) was estimated by the method reported in the literature[31] and calculated using the following equation: Φ(%) ) (2 × R/I) × 100, where R and I represent the number of evolved H2 molecules and the number of incident photons, respectively.
2-039	Here, Φ is quantum efficiency, where it is assumed that all incident photons are absorbed by the photocatalyst.
2-040	The number of incident photons was measured using a calibrated Si photodiode.

2-042	Electrochemical Measurements.
2-043	The H2 evolution property of MoS2 was investigated by electrochemical measure- ments on two electrodes: bare FTO and MoS2/FTO electrodes.
2-044	The MoS2/FTO electrode was prepared by dropping a small amount of (NH4)2MoS4 methanol solution onto the FTO glass using a pipet, followed by a calcination at 573 K for 2 h in H2S to convert (NH4)2MoS4 to MoS2.
2-045	Because treatment with temperatures higher than 673 K in H2S will damage the FTO surface, electrochemical measurement were done on the MoS2/FTO electrode prepared at 573 K. Electrochemical experiments were performed in a three-electrode cell made of quartz.
2-046	A Pt plate and a saturated calomel electrode (SCE) were employed as the counter and reference electrodes, and the as-prepared electrodes were used as the working electrodes.
2-047	A 0.5 M Na2SO4 solution was used as the electrolyte.
2-048	Current-voltage curves were recorded by an electrochemical workstation (CHI660A, Shanghai Chenhua Instruments, China).

The Mo 3d peaks at 231.9 and 228.8 eV indicate the formation of MoS2,[32] and a higher binding energy at 231.9 and 228.8 eV can be attributed to Mo species with the valence between IV and VI.

3-065	H2 Evolution Property of MoS2.
3-066	In the photocatalytic H2 production reactions, the reduction of protons to H2 is a very important step.
3-067	The H2 evolution property of MoS2 is supposed to be an important reason for the enhanced activity of MoS2/CdS.
3-068	Therefore, photoelectrochemical measurements were con- ducted to investigate the role of MoS 2.
3-069	Figure 8 shows the current-voltage curve of the bare FTO and MoS2/FTO elec- trodes.
3-070	Cathodic current attributed to the reduction of water to H2 was observed for the bare FTO electrode with the increase of the applied potential, while the current is extremely low.
3-071	However, there is a great increase in the cathodic current for the MoS2/FTO electrode at similar potentials.
3-072	When the potential is set at -0.9 V versus SCE, the current observed on the MoS2/FTO electrode was about 39 times higher than that on the bare FTO electrode.
3-073	Therefore, MoS2 was clearly demonstrated to be a good material that can catalyze the evolution of H2, which is deemed to be an important reason for the enhanced activity of the MoS 2/CdS catalyst.
3-074	3.5.
3-075	H2 Evolution on MoS2/CdS Catalyst in the Presence of Different Sacrificial Reagents.
3-076	In the optimized conditions, the MoS2/CdS catalyst was demonstrated to be more active than Pt/CdS when using lactic acid as the sacrificial reagent in the photocatalytic H 2 production reactions.
3-077	To compare the photo- catalytic activity of MoS 2/CdS with that of Pt/CdS in detail, different sacrificial reagents were used in the photocatalytic reactions.
3-078	Figure 9 shows the rate of H2 evolution on 0.2 wt % MoS 2/CdS and 0.2 wt % Pt/CdS catalysts using methanol, ethanol, glycol, glycerol, and lactic acid as the sacrificial reagents.
3-079	CdS alone exhibits extremely low activity for H2 evolution without loading cocatalysts in the presence of the tested sacrificial reagents (not shown).
3-080	After loading 0.2 wt % of MoS 2 or Pt as cocatalyst on CdS, the activity of MoS2/CdS or Pt/CdS is greatly increased for all these sacrificial reagents.
3-081	The rate of H2 evolution on 0.2 wt % MoS2/CdS for different sacrificial reagents is in the order of lactic acid > glycerol > glycol > ethanol > methanol.
3-082	The rate of H2 evolution on 0.2 wt % MoS2/CdS is always higher than that on 0.2 wt % Pt/CdS in the presence of the above sacrificial reagents.
3-083	Therefore, 0.2 wt % MoS2/CdS is demonstrated to be a superior catalyst than 0.2 wt % Pt/CdS for the production of H2, irrespective of the different sacrificial organic reagents used.
3-084	Considering the fact that Pt has a higher H2 activation ability than MoS2 (Figure
3-085	8),[35] the higher activity of MoS2/CdS than Pt/CdS for photo- catalytic H2 production is mainly due to the better junctions formed between MoS2 and CdS, which leads to a more efficient interelectron transfer between MoS2 and CdS.
3-086	It is interesting to note that the rate of H2 evolution on MoS2/CdS catalysts varies greatly when using different sacrificial reagents, which is similar to the results obtained for the Pt/CdS catalyst.
3-087	It was reported that CdS demonstrates diverse ability for the oxidation of different sacrificial reagents.[36]
3-088	Considering the high catalytic efficiency of MoS2 and Pt for the activation of H2, the rate-determining step for the photocatalytic H2 production reactions on MoS2/CdS and Pt/CdS is supposed to
3-089	be the oxidation of the organic reagents, which leads to the drastic difference between the reaction activity for different sacrificial reagents.
4-001	The photocatalytic H2 production on CdS under visible light (λ > 420 nm) can be significantly enhanced by loading MoS2 as a cocatalyst using different organic sacrificial reagents.
4-002	The optimized 0.2 wt % MoS2/CdS catalyst demonstrated even higher activity than the 0.2 wt % Pt/CdS catalyst, regardless of the different sacrificial reagents used.
4-003	It is found that the excellent catalytic property of MoS2, together with the junctions formed between MoS2 and CdS, is mainly responsible for the greatly enhanced activity of MoS2/CdS in the photocatalytic H2 production.
