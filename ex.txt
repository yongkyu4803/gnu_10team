Photoinduced proton transfer in hydroxyaryl ketones has been studied extensively over the past two decades [1–3]. Several groups have reported that ortho-substituted derivatives form quinone methides upon excitation [4,5], and these reactive intermediates have been used in photocaging, polymer cross-linking, and the alkylation of nucleic acids [6–8]. The efficiency of quinone methide formation depends on the acidity of the phenol in the excited state and on the ability of the solvent to accept a proton. Water is known to mediate the transfer through a hydrogen-bonded chain [9], whereas aprotic solvents slow the process considerably [10].

The lifetime of the quinone methide controls which nucleophiles it can trap. Short-lived species react mainly with the solvent, while long-lived species can be intercepted by amines, thiols, and dienes [11,12]. It has been suggested that bulky substituents adjacent to the exocyclic methylene group could protect the intermediate from nucleophilic attack [13]. However, the influence of such substituents on the lifetime and selectivity of these intermediates has not been examined systematically, and most reported examples carry only methyl or phenyl groups [4,14].

In this work, we prepared a series of tert-butyl and adamantyl substituted hydroxybenzophenones 3a-3d and investigated their photochemistry in aqueous acetonitrile by preparative irradiation, fluorescence spectroscopy, and laser flash photolysis. We also performed DFT calculations to locate the transition states for the proton transfer. We expect that steric shielding increases the lifetime of the transient species and that the substitution pattern controls the reactivity toward different classes of nucleophiles. Such control would make these compounds useful as photoactivatable alkylating agents.
